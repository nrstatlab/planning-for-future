MSc Statistics Study Material

Prescribed curriculum, from 2025–26 · four semesters · 96 credits

The postgraduate programme, following the prescribed M.Sc. (Statistics) course curriculum, with effect from the academic year 2025–26. Four semesters, 96 credits. The syllabus for Semesters I and II has been worked from the official document and is set out below, and the papers are now being written out unit by unit — one paper at a time, in full.

Written so far: STS-102 Linear Algebra & Linear Models, STS-103 Probability Theory and STS-104 Distribution Theory, with the practicals STS-106 and STS-107 — each written unit by unit, with the proofs worked one step at a time and every number in every example computed rather than quoted. They are linked from the Semester I table below.

Not written yet: the other eleven papers of Semesters I and II. Each becomes a folder of unit pages the way the undergraduate subjects are written, and the table shows a plain code with no link until its paper is done — so what is finished and what is not can be told at a glance rather than discovered by clicking.

Where this overlaps the undergraduate notes

Several Semester I and II papers cover ground the site already teaches at undergraduate level. Those pages are a starting point, not a substitute — a Master's paper goes further on every one of them — but they are worth knowing about while the MSc material is written:

  • STS-103 Probability Theory and STS-104 Distribution Theory — see Theory of Probability, Discrete Distributions and Continuous Distributions.
  • STS-201 Estimation Theory — see Inferential Statistics, whose first three units were rewritten from textbook sources with every step shown.
  • STS-203 Design and Analysis of Experiments — see Design & Analysis of Experiments.
  • STS-204 Sampling Theory — see Sampling Techniques.
  • STS-106, STS-107 and STS-108 use R — see Computational Statistics & R Programming.

Why this matters for competitive exams

Several of the exams in the competitive exams section recruit at postgraduate level, so their papers are set at Master's standard. Mapping one of those syllabi onto undergraduate notes alone would produce a map that is mostly amber and red — which is why the RBI map is waiting on this material rather than the other way round.
