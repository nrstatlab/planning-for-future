The postgraduate programme, following the prescribed M.Sc. (Statistics) course curriculum, with effect from the academic year 2025–26. Semesters I and II — sixteen papers, 48 credits — have been worked from the official document and are set out below in full, paper by paper and unit by unit.

All sixteen papers of Semesters I and II are written. The four Semester I theory papers — STS-101 Mathematical Analysis, STS-102 Linear Algebra & Linear Models, STS-103 Probability Theory and STS-104 Distribution Theory, carrying the practicals STS-106 and STS-107; the four Semester II theory papers — STS-201 Estimation Theory and STS-202 Multivariate Analysis, which between them carry both sections of STS-205, and STS-203 Design and Analysis of Experiments and STS-204 Sampling Theory, which between them carry both sections of STS-206; and the four standalone practicals STS-105 Statistical Methods using Python, STS-108 Data Handling using R, STS-207 Statistical Analysis using SPSS and STS-208 Data Science using Python.

Each is written unit by unit, with the proofs worked one step at a time and every number in every example computed rather than quoted. Where a topic is already taught in the undergraduate subjects, these pages link it and start where it stops instead of teaching it twice.

A topic examined by every syllabus that exists — university MSc, UGC NET, CSIR NET and the Indian Statistical Service alike — is written to the topic rather than to a paper code, because no one prescribed syllabus owns it. Those are gathered under Postgraduate core below.

Semester I

Eight papers, 24 credits. Four theory and four practical.

STS-101

Mathematical Analysis

The analysis the rest of the programme quietly assumes, proved rather than asserted: sequences and series with the convergence tests worked; limits, continuity and differentiability; Riemann integration; functions of several variables, with the mixed partials that disagree; and the uniform convergence that decides when a limit may be taken inside an integral.

 STS-102 · STS-106

Linear Algebra & Linear Models

Vector spaces, rank and the four fundamental subspaces; generalized inverses, with the Moore–Penrose conditions checked one at a time; quadratic forms and their eigenvalues; and least squares on rank-deficient normal equations, where estimability is the question the arithmetic cannot answer for you. Carries the STS-106 practical, conventional and in R without packages.

 STS-103

Probability Theory

Measure-theoretic probability: fields and sigma-fields, measurable functions, the expectation as an integral; the convergence relations with the counterexamples that separate them; characteristic functions and the inversion theorem; and the laws of large numbers and the central limit theorem proved, not quoted.

 STS-104 · STS-107

Distribution Theory

The standard families with their moment generating and characteristic functions; the relations between them derived rather than tabulated; order statistics and their joint density; the sampling distributions \(\chi^{2}\), \(t\) and \(F\) built from the normal; and the compound and truncated forms. Carries the STS-107 practical.

 STS-105

Statistical Methods using Python

Twelve programs with no statistical package at all, as the paper's own note requires — matrices, sorting, the summary measures, moments, five random number generators, six distributions fitted and tested, correlation and regression, seven tests of hypotheses and both analyses of variance. Every listing was run and prints the output it produced.

 STS-108

Data Handling using R

The whole workflow on one data set: measurement scales and what each permits, the pre-processing order, six transformations judged by what they do to the skewness, the diagrams base R has no button for, cross-validation with over-fitting measured, the confusion matrix and its six ratios, and five non-parametric tests compared with their parametric counterparts.

Semester II

Eight papers, 24 credits, in the same shape.

STS-201 · STS-205 A

Estimation Theory

Unbiasedness, consistency, sufficiency and completeness, with the Rao–Blackwell and Lehmann–Scheffé theorems proved; the Cramér–Rao bound and when it is attained; maximum likelihood and the method of scoring; Bayes estimation under three loss functions; and interval estimation, including the shortest interval rather than the equal-tailed one.

 STS-202 · STS-205 B

Multivariate Analysis

The paper with no undergraduate counterpart, so everything is built here: random vectors and dispersion matrices, the multivariate normal and its partition theorem, the Wishart as the \(\chi^{2}\) in matrix form, Hotelling's \(T^{2}\) and Wilks' \(\Lambda\), discriminant analysis, principal components, canonical correlations, clustering, scaling and factor analysis — one correlation matrix running through the paper so the methods can be compared on the same data.

 STS-203 · STS-206 A

Design and Analysis of Experiments

Several observations per cell and the pure error they buy; proportional and disproportionate cell frequencies; multiple comparisons with the studentized range computed rather than quoted; factorials and Yates's algorithm through three passes; confounding, fractional replication and split plots; incomplete blocks; and response surfaces, with the rotatability condition derived and then checked numerically.

 STS-204 · STS-206 B

Sampling Theory

Unequal probability sampling with Hansen–Hurwitz, Lahiri, Horvitz–Thompson and Yates–Grundy, the last verified by complete enumeration; the bias of the ratio estimator computed exactly over every possible sample; cluster sampling and the design effect; and two-stage sub-sampling, non-sampling error, randomized response and small area estimation.

 STS-207

Statistical Analysis using SPSS

All nine prescribed areas as procedure rather than theory — the Variable View settings that decide what SPSS will let you do, the syntax for each analysis, and the footnote that has to be acted on. Model selection, logistic regression and probit analysis are worked in full, because they are the only parts of the paper not taught elsewhere on this site.

 STS-208

Data Science using Python

One Extract–Transform–Load pipeline end to end: five formats parsed into one structure, an anomaly pass that names the fault in every rejected record, binary files, the re module, a normalised schema with its constraints enforced and its transaction rolled back, the same data as documents, and NumPy and Pandas to finish.

Postgraduate Core, Written to the Topic

Some postgraduate topics are examined by every syllabus that exists — university MSc, UGC NET, CSIR NET and the Indian Statistical Service alike — while the paper number they sit under changes from one university to the next. Those are written here to the topic, with no paper code, and each one names the syllabuses that examine it instead of inventing a code for itself.

POSTGRADUATE CORE

Testing of Hypotheses

Where the undergraduate treatment stops: randomized tests and the existence and necessity halves of the Neyman–Pearson lemma; uniformly most powerful tests, monotone likelihood ratio and the Karlin–Rubin theorem, with a worked demonstration that no UMP test exists against a two-sided alternative; the likelihood ratio reduction carried out and Wilks' approximation measured against the exact answer; and Wald's sequential probability ratio test with its operating characteristic and average sample number computed.

Where this overlaps the undergraduate notes

Several Semester I and II papers cover ground the site already teaches at undergraduate level. Those pages are a starting point, not a substitute — a Master's paper goes further on every one of them — but they are worth knowing about while the MSc material is written:

  • STS-103 Probability Theory and STS-104 Distribution Theory — see Theory of Probability, Discrete Distributions and Continuous Distributions.
  • STS-201 Estimation Theory — see Inferential Statistics, whose first three units were rewritten from textbook sources with every step shown.
  • STS-203 Design and Analysis of Experiments — see Design & Analysis of Experiments.
  • STS-204 Sampling Theory — see Sampling Techniques.
  • STS-106, STS-107 and STS-108 use R — see Computational Statistics & R Programming.

Why this matters for competitive exams

Several of the examinations in the examinations section recruit at postgraduate level, so their papers are set at Master's standard, and mapping one of those syllabuses onto undergraduate notes alone would produce a map that is mostly amber and red. The Indian Statistical Service map is the clearest case: a large share of its lines point at these pages rather than at the undergraduate subjects, and without them that map would have far more gaps than it does.
