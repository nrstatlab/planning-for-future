    3D Statistics

    Enter up to five variables with up to 50 observations each. You get the descriptive statistics, tests and confidence intervals, and a comparison with the standard probability distributions. Every result shows its formula with your numbers in it and links to the page that teaches it.

    The calculator runs in your browser. Your numbers are not sent anywhere.

    1. Enter data 2. Descriptive statistics 3. Inferential statistics 4. Compare with distributions

    Enter data

    Up to five variables, with up to 50 observations each. First say how many variables and how large each sample is, then type the values into the grid, or paste columns copied from Excel, Google Sheets or a CSV file.

    Step 1. The variables

    Step 2. The sample sizes

    Give each variable its sample size, from 2 to 50. Equal sizes make a balanced design, which paired tests, correlation and regression need. Tests for independent samples (two-sample t, F, ANOVA, Mann–Whitney, Kruskal–Wallis) work with unequal sizes.

    Step 3. The values

    The example is the data of Practical 13 of Linear Algebra & Linear Models, so the correlations on the next tab can be checked against that worked solution (\(r_{yx_1} = 0.988212\), \(r_{yx_1\cdot x_2} = 0.987457\), \(R_{y\cdot x_1x_2} = 0.996957\)).

    The example is the three machines of Design & Analysis of Experiments, Unit 1, Worked Problem 1, with 7, 5 and 6 observations: run the one-way ANOVA on the next tabs and compare it with the worked solution (\(F = 4.885\), \(p = 0.023\)).

    Inferential statistics

    Choose a test, the variables it uses and the level of significance. The result shows the hypotheses, the formula with your numbers in it, the statistic, its degrees of freedom, the p-value, the critical value and the decision.

    Compare with distributions

    The sample is compared with each standard distribution taught in the courses. Each distribution's parameters are estimated from the data. Then a \(\chi^2\) goodness-of-fit test (and, for continuous distributions, a Kolmogorov–Smirnov test) asks whether the data could have come from it. Distributions that cannot describe these data are listed with the reason.

    Test a distribution you specify

    When the parameters are given in advance (for example “Poisson with \(\lambda = 2\)”), nothing is estimated from the data and the degrees of freedom are not reduced.

    How the calculator works

    • Every calculation is done by this page in your browser. Nothing you enter is sent to a server, and nothing is kept unless you tick “Remember my data”. Then it is kept in this browser only.
    • The definitions are the ones the courses use: quartiles by the \((n+1)p\) rule, variance with divisor \(n\) as a descriptive measure and \(n-1\) in the tests, Spearman's coefficient with the tie correction, and the decision written as “reject” or “do not reject” \(H_0\).
    • Every result is checked against independent statistical software (scipy and statsmodels) on fixed data sets each time the site is built.
