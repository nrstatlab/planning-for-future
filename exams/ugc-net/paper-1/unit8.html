Topic Overview — What & Why

Unit VIII asks what a teacher in a networked university should know about the tools they use. Its questions are short and factual: expand an abbreviation, tell two similar things apart (internet and intranet, CC and BCC, SMTP and IMAP), convert a unit (bits, bytes, binary), and name the platform that does a job in Indian higher education or government.

  • ICT is information and communication technology: the hardware, software, networks and media used to create, store, share and use information.
  • The internet is a public network of networks; an intranet is a private one inside an organisation.
  • Higher education's platforms: SWAYAM for courses, NDLI for reading, Shodhganga for theses, DigiLocker and the Academic Bank of Credits for records and credits.
  • E-governance: services delivered online, government to citizen (G2C), to business (G2B), to government (G2G) and to employee (G2E).

Bits and bytes

  • A bit (binary digit) is 0 or 1; a byte is 8 bits.
  • In computing usage, 1 KB = 1,024 bytes, 1 MB = 1,024 KB, 1 GB = 1,024 MB, 1 TB = 1,024 GB. (Strictly these are KiB, MiB and so on; the SI prefixes kilo and mega mean 1,000 and 1,000,000, and storage makers use those.) Questions in this paper usually take 1,024.
  • Binary numbers use powers of 2: 1011 in binary is 1×8 + 0×4 + 1×2 + 1×1 = 11.
  • Network speed is quoted in bits per second (Mbps), file sizes in bytes (MB): a 100 Mbps line moves at most 12.5 MB a second.
EXAMPLE 1 How many bits are in 2 KB? 2 × 1,024 bytes × 8 bits = 16,384 bits.
EXAMPLE 2 "https://www.ugc.gov.in/page.html": https is the protocol, www.ugc.gov.in the domain name (which DNS turns into an IP address), and page.html the file. Together they are a URL.

2. Basics of the Internet

  • The internet is a worldwide network of networks that exchange data using TCP/IP. It grew from ARPANET, a United States defence research network first connected in 1969.
  • The World Wide Web is one service that runs on the internet: pages linked by hypertext, proposed by Tim Berners-Lee at CERN in 1989. E-mail, file transfer and video calls are other services; the web and the internet are not the same thing.
  • A web browser (Chrome, Firefox, Edge, Safari) displays web pages; a search engine (Google, Bing) finds them.
  • Public internet access in India began on 15 August 1995, through VSNL.
  • Ways to connect: broadband over fibre or cable, mobile data (4G, 5G), Wi-Fi, satellite.
  • Cyber security: malware (viruses, worms, ransomware), phishing (fake messages that steal passwords), and the defences: strong passwords, two-factor authentication, updates, firewalls, antivirus.
EXAMPLE 1 A question asks which is a service of the internet rather than the internet itself. The World Wide Web.
EXAMPLE 2 An e-mail claiming to be from a bank asks you to "verify your account" by entering your password on a linked page. That is phishing; the bank's real site would not ask this by e-mail.

4. E-mail

  • An address has a user name, the @ sign and a domain: registrar@university.ac.in.
  • To: the main recipients. CC (carbon copy): others who should see it; everyone sees who was copied. BCC (blind carbon copy): recipients hidden from all the others.
  • Reply answers the sender; Reply all answers everyone in To and CC; Forward sends the message on to someone new.
  • Attachments are files sent with a message. Spam is unwanted bulk mail.
  • Mail is sent by SMTP and received by POP3 or IMAP. The first e-mail between computers on a network was sent by Ray Tomlinson in 1971, who chose the @ sign.
EXAMPLE 1 A teacher writes to 60 parents and does not want any parent to see the others' addresses. She puts them all in BCC.
EXAMPLE 2 A question asks which protocol keeps e-mail on the server and in step across a phone and a laptop. IMAP; POP3 usually downloads and removes it.

5. Audio and Video Conferencing

  • Audio conferencing joins several people by voice (a telephone conference or VoIP call). Video conferencing adds live video, and usually screen-sharing and chat.
  • It needs a camera and microphone, software or a browser, and enough bandwidth (data per second). Latency is the delay between speaking and being heard.
  • A webinar is a seminar on the web, usually one or a few speakers to a large audience who listen and ask questions in chat.
  • In teaching it serves live online classes, viva voce examinations, guest lectures from other cities, and research meetings.
EXAMPLE 1 A thesis viva in which the external examiner joins by video from another university is a use of video conferencing in higher education.
EXAMPLE 2 A lecture streamed live to 500 registered teachers, who can only send questions by chat, is a webinar rather than a meeting.

7. ICT and Governance

  • E-governance is the use of ICT to deliver government services, exchange information and make administration transparent. Its models are G2C (government to citizen: certificates, pensions), G2B (to business: licences, tenders), G2G (between departments) and G2E (to employees: payroll, records).
  • The National e-Governance Plan (NeGP), approved in 2006, set up mission-mode projects for services such as passports, income tax and land records.
  • Digital India, launched on 1 July 2015, aims at digital infrastructure for every citizen, services on demand, and digital literacy.
  • Platforms: DigiLocker (official documents in digital form), UMANG (one mobile app for many government services), Aadhaar (digital identity), UPI (instant payments, from the National Payments Corporation of India, 2016), and Common Service Centres (village-level access points for online services).
  • Benefits: speed, transparency, less corruption, services at any hour. Challenges: the digital divide, privacy and data protection (the DPDP Act, 2023), and cyber security.
EXAMPLE 1 A farmer applies for a land-record copy online at a village Common Service Centre and receives it the same day. A G2C service.
EXAMPLE 2 A state government publishes all tenders online, and bids are submitted and opened on the portal. A G2B service that reduces discretion and so corruption.

  • SMTP sends; POP3 and IMAP receive. BCC hides recipients.
  • Internet: public. Intranet: one organisation. Extranet: the organisation and chosen outsiders.
  • The web (Berners-Lee, 1989) is a service on the internet (from ARPANET, 1969).
