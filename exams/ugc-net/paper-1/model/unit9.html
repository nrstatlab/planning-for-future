    Model Questions — Unit 9: People, Development and Environment

    UGC NET Paper I (General Paper) · 50 questions · Click "Show Answer" for the worked solution

    About this set

    The Millennium and Sustainable Development Goals, human and environment interaction, environmental issues, pollution and waste, climate change, the effects of pollutants on health, energy resources, natural hazards, the Environment (Protection) Act, the National Action Plan on Climate Change and international agreements.

    These are model questions written for practice at the standard of the paper; they are not questions from a past paper. Try each one before opening the answer. Every answer gives the working and a link to the section of the Unit 9 notes that teaches it.

    The questions

    Q1. Consider the statements:(I) The Millennium Development Goals were eight in number and ran from 2000 to 2015.(II) The Sustainable Development Goals are 17 goals with 169 targets, for 2015 to 2030.(III) The Sustainable Development Goals apply only to developing countries.(IV) The Sustainable Development Goals were adopted in the 2030 Agenda, in September 2015.Which of the statements are correct?
    1. I and II only
    2. I, II and IV only
    3. II, III and IV only
    4. I, II, III and IV
    Show Answer

    Answer: (B) I, II and IV only.

    The MDGs were addressed mainly to developing countries; the SDGs apply to all countries. So (III) is false and the other three are correct.

    Read this in the notes: Unit 9, Development and Environment: the MDGs and the SDGs

    Q3. “Development that meets the needs of the present without compromising the ability of future generations to meet their own needs” is the definition of sustainable development given in:

    Answer: (A) the Brundtland Report, Our Common Future (1987).

    The definition comes from the report of the World Commission on Environment and Development, chaired by Gro Harlem Brundtland, published in 1987.

    Q4. Which of the following was NOT one of the eight Millennium Development Goals?
    1. To achieve universal primary education
    2. To reduce child mortality
    3. To provide affordable and clean energy
    4. To improve maternal health
    Show Answer

    Answer: (C) To provide affordable and clean energy.

    Affordable and clean energy is SDG 7; energy had no goal of its own among the MDGs. The eight MDGs covered poverty and hunger, primary education, gender equality, child mortality, maternal health, HIV/AIDS and malaria, environmental sustainability and a global partnership.

    Q6. In the IPAT identity, the environmental impact I is expressed as the product of:
    1. production, agriculture and trade
    2. pollution, area and time
    3. people, attitude and tools
    4. population, affluence and technology
    Show Answer

    Answer: (D) population, affluence and technology.

    Impact = Population × Affluence (consumption per person) × Technology (impact per unit of consumption).

    Q7. The population of a city is unchanged, but every household doubles its use of electricity generated from coal. In terms of the IPAT identity, the impact rises through:
    1. affluence
    2. population
    3. technology
    4. none of the three
    Show Answer

    Answer: (A) affluence.

    Consumption per person has risen while the number of people and the technology (coal-based power) are the same: the change is in A.

    Q9. Smoke from the burning of crop residue in Punjab and Haryana adds to the winter air pollution of Delhi. By its scale, this environmental issue is:
    1. local
    2. regional
    3. global
    4. indoor
    Show Answer

    Answer: (B) regional.

    The cause and the effect lie in different states, hundreds of kilometres apart, but within one region. A city's garbage dump is local; climate change and ozone depletion are global.

    Read this in the notes: Unit 9, Environmental Issues: Local, Regional and Global

    Q10. Which of the following lists contains only global environmental issues?
    1. Noise near a highway, acid rain, ozone depletion
    2. A polluted lake, desertification, climate change
    3. River pollution along a basin, plastic in the oceans, a city's garbage dump
    4. Climate change, ozone depletion, loss of biodiversity
    Show Answer

    Answer: (D) Climate change, ozone depletion, loss of biodiversity.

    Global issues affect the whole planet whatever their source: climate change, ozone depletion, loss of biodiversity, plastic in the oceans. Noise, a lake and a dump are local; acid rain, river-basin pollution and desertification are regional.

    Read this in the notes: Unit 9, Environmental Issues: Local, Regional and Global

    Q11. Given below are two statements, one labelled Assertion (A) and the other Reason (R).Assertion (A): The depletion of the ozone layer had to be dealt with by a global treaty.Reason (R): Ozone-depleting substances released in any country thin the ozone layer for all.Choose the correct answer.
    1. Both (A) and (R) are true and (R) is the correct explanation of (A)
    2. Both (A) and (R) are true but (R) is not the correct explanation of (A)
    3. (A) is true but (R) is false
    4. (A) is false but (R) is true
    Show Answer

    Answer: (A) Both (A) and (R) are true and (R) is the correct explanation of (A).

    Because the harm does not stay where the gases are released, no country can protect itself alone; hence the Vienna Convention and the Montreal Protocol. Both statements are true and (R) is the reason.

    Read this in the notes: Unit 9, Environmental Issues: Local, Regional and Global

    Q12. A stretch of river below a town's sewage outfall has a high biochemical oxygen demand (BOD). This indicates:
    1. a high level of dissolved oxygen
    2. contamination by heavy metals only
    3. a heavy load of organic pollution
    4. very clean water
    Show Answer

    Answer: (C) a heavy load of organic pollution.

    BOD is the oxygen micro-organisms need to break down the organic matter in water. A high BOD means much organic matter, such as sewage, and the dissolved oxygen falls as it is used up.

    Read this in the notes: Unit 9, Pollution and Waste

    Q13. Arrange the following in the order of the waste hierarchy, from the most preferred to the least preferred: (1) Recycle, (2) Dispose, (3) Reduce, (4) Recover energy, (5) Reuse.

    2. 3, 5, 1, 4, 2
    3. 5, 3, 1, 4, 2
    4. 3, 5, 4, 1, 2

    Reduce, reuse, recycle, recover, dispose. It is best not to create waste at all, and disposal is the last resort.

    Read this in the notes: Unit 9, Pollution and Waste

    Q16. Noise is measured in decibels on a logarithmic scale. A sound of 70 dB has how many times the intensity of a sound of 50 dB?
    1. 1.4 times
    2. 20 times
    3. 100 times
    4. 2 times
    Show Answer

    Answer: (C) 100 times.

    Every 10 dB is a tenfold increase in intensity. A difference of 20 dB is $10\times10=100$ times.

    Q18. In a lake that receives run-off rich in fertiliser, algae multiply rapidly, the dissolved oxygen falls and fish die. The process is called:
    1. eutrophication
    2. biomagnification
    3. salinisation
    4. desertification
    Show Answer

    Answer: (A) eutrophication.

    Nutrients feed algal blooms; when the algae die and decay, oxygen is used up. Biomagnification is the build-up of a persistent pollutant along the food chain.

    Read this in the notes: Unit 9, Pollution and Waste

    Q19. Which of the following is NOT one of the six greenhouse gases covered by the Kyoto Protocol?
    1. Methane
    2. Nitrous oxide
    3. Sulphur hexafluoride
    4. Chlorofluorocarbons
    Show Answer

    Answer: (D) Chlorofluorocarbons.

    The Kyoto gases are carbon dioxide, methane, nitrous oxide, hydrofluorocarbons, perfluorocarbons and sulphur hexafluoride. Chlorofluorocarbons, though they also trap heat, are controlled as ozone-depleting substances under the Montreal Protocol.

    Read this in the notes: Unit 9, Climate Change and its Dimensions

    4. the World Health Organization and the Food and Agriculture Organization
    Show Answer

    Answer: (C) the World Meteorological Organization and the United Nations Environment Programme.

    The IPCC was established by the WMO and UNEP to assess the science of climate change; it does not itself do research.

    Read this in the notes: Unit 9, Climate Change and its Dimensions

    Q21. Planting mangroves along a coast stores carbon and also protects the shore against storm surges. In the language of climate policy these are, respectively:
    1. adaptation and mitigation
    2. mitigation and adaptation
    3. mitigation and compensation
    4. adaptation and remediation
    Show Answer

    Answer: (B) mitigation and adaptation.

    Mitigation reduces the cause (emissions, or carbon in the atmosphere); adaptation copes with the effects already happening. Mangroves do both.

    Read this in the notes: Unit 9, Climate Change and its Dimensions

    Q22. The principle that all countries must act on climate change, but that developed countries, which have emitted most, should take the lead, is called:
    1. common but differentiated responsibilities

    3. the precautionary principle
    4. extended producer responsibility

    Answer: (A) common but differentiated responsibilities.

    Common but differentiated responsibilities was set out at Rio in 1992. Climate justice and the fund for loss and damage follow from the same idea.

    Q23. Given below are two statements, one labelled Assertion (A) and the other Reason (R).Assertion (A): Per tonne emitted, methane warms the atmosphere far more than carbon dioxide, yet carbon dioxide contributes most to global warming.Reason (R): Carbon dioxide is emitted in far greater quantity and stays in the atmosphere much longer.Choose the correct answer.
    1. Both (A) and (R) are true and (R) is the correct explanation of (A)
    2. Both (A) and (R) are true but (R) is not the correct explanation of (A)
    3. (A) is true but (R) is false
    4. (A) is false but (R) is true
    Show Answer

    Answer: (A) Both (A) and (R) are true and (R) is the correct explanation of (A).

    The contribution of a gas depends on its strength, the amount emitted and how long it lasts. Methane is strong but short-lived and emitted in smaller amounts. Both statements are true and (R) explains (A).

    Read this in the notes: Unit 9, Climate Change and its Dimensions

    Q24. Which of the following is an adaptation measure and not a mitigation measure?

    2. Improving the fuel efficiency of vehicles
    3. Planting forests to absorb carbon dioxide
    4. Developing drought-resistant varieties of crops
    Show Answer

    Answer: (D) Developing drought-resistant varieties of crops.

    Drought-resistant crops help people live with a changed climate: adaptation. The other three reduce emissions or remove carbon dioxide: mitigation.

    Read this in the notes: Unit 9, Climate Change and its Dimensions

    Q27. Long-term drinking of groundwater contaminated with arsenic, as in parts of the Ganga basin, causes:
    1. skin lesions and cancers

    Q30. Which of the following sources of energy is renewable but confined to particular places, and draws on the internal heat of the Earth?
    1. Biomass
    2. Geothermal
    3. Nuclear
    4. Natural gas
    Show Answer

    Answer: (B) Geothermal.

    Geothermal energy is the Earth's own heat, available where hot rock or water lies near the surface; the best-known Indian site is the Puga valley in Ladakh.

    Read this in the notes: Unit 9, Natural and Energy Resources

    Q31. Which of the following lists contains only non-renewable sources of energy?
    1. Coal, petroleum and natural gas
    2. Coal, wind and biomass
    3. Solar, hydro and geothermal
    4. Petroleum, solar and nuclear
    Show Answer

    Answer: (A) Coal, petroleum and natural gas.

    The fossil fuels are non-renewable and are the main source of carbon dioxide. Wind, biomass, solar, hydro and geothermal energy are renewable.

    Read this in the notes: Unit 9, Natural and Energy Resources

    Q32. Biogas, produced from cattle dung in a village digester, is obtained by:
    1. combustion in excess air
    2. distillation
    3. nuclear fission
    4. anaerobic digestion
    Show Answer

    Answer: (D) anaerobic digestion.

    Micro-organisms break the organic matter down in the absence of air and release a gas, mostly methane; the slurry left over is a good manure. It is a form of biomass energy.

    Q35. A hazard becomes a disaster when:

    A hazard is a threatening event; a disaster is its outcome when the people exposed are vulnerable and cannot cope with their own resources.

    Q36. A municipality forbids the building of new houses on the flood plain of a river. This is an example of:

    Non-structural mitigation works through rules and knowledge: land-use zoning, building codes, insurance, early warning and awareness. Structural mitigation is physical: embankments, shelters, resistant buildings.
