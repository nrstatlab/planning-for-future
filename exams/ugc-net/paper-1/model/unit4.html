    Model Questions — Unit 4: Communication

    UGC NET Paper I (General Paper) · 50 questions · Click "Show Answer" for the worked solution

    About this set

    The process, models and types of communication, verbal and non-verbal communication, inter-cultural and group communication, classroom communication, barriers, and mass media and society.

    These are model questions written for practice at the standard of the paper; they are not questions from a past paper. Try each one before opening the answer. Every answer gives the working and a link to the section of the Unit 4 notes that teaches it.

    The questions

    Q1. Arrange the following in the order in which they occur in the process of communication: (1) Decoding, (2) Feedback, (3) Encoding, (4) Transmission through a channel, (5) Idea in the mind of the sender.
    1. 5, 4, 3, 1, 2
    2. 5, 3, 4, 1, 2
    3. 3, 5, 4, 2, 1
    4. 5, 3, 1, 4, 2
    Show Answer

    Answer: (B) 5, 3, 4, 1, 2.

    The sender has an idea, encodes it into symbols, and sends the message through a channel; the receiver decodes it and responds with feedback, which makes the process two-way.

    Read this in the notes: Unit 4, Communication: Meaning, Types and Characteristics

    Q2. Match the model of communication in List I with its key idea in List II.
    List IList II
    (a) Lasswell(i) Source, message, channel, receiver
    (b) Shannon and Weaver(ii) Overlap of the fields of experience of sender and receiver
    (c) Berlo(iii) Who says what, in which channel, to whom, with what effect
    (d) Schramm(iv) Noise in a linear, mathematical model
    1. (a)-(iii), (b)-(i), (c)-(iv), (d)-(ii)
    2. (a)-(iv), (b)-(iii), (c)-(i), (d)-(ii)
    3. (a)-(iii), (b)-(iv), (c)-(ii), (d)-(i)
    4. (a)-(iii), (b)-(iv), (c)-(i), (d)-(ii)
    Show Answer

    Answer: (D) (a)-(iii), (b)-(iv), (c)-(i), (d)-(ii).

    Lasswell (1948) framed communication as five questions; Shannon and Weaver introduced noise; Berlo (1960) gave the SMCR model; Schramm's circular model says communication works where the fields of experience overlap.

    Q3. A principal sends a circular to all teachers; the teachers talk about it in the staff room; a rumour about it then reaches the students. These are examples, respectively, of:
    1. downward, horizontal and grapevine communication
    2. upward, horizontal and formal communication
    3. downward, diagonal and formal communication
    4. horizontal, downward and grapevine communication
    Show Answer

    Answer: (A) downward, horizontal and grapevine communication.

    A circular from a superior to subordinates is formal downward communication. Talk among equals is horizontal (lateral). A rumour travels by the informal channel, the grapevine.

    Read this in the notes: Unit 4, Communication: Meaning, Types and Characteristics

    Q4. Consider the statements about communication:(I) It is a continuous process, not a single act.(II) Once made, a communication cannot be taken back.(III) It is possible for a person in the presence of others to communicate nothing at all.(IV) It is shaped by its context.Which of the statements are correct?
    1. I and II only
    2. I, III and IV only
    3. I, II and IV only
    4. I, II, III and IV
    Show Answer

    Answer: (C) I, II and IV only.

    Communication is a process, irreversible and contextual. (III) is false: one cannot not communicate; silence, posture and absence also send messages.

    Q5. A lecturer rehearses a difficult explanation silently in her own mind before entering the class. This is:
    1. interpersonal communication
    2. intrapersonal communication
    3. group communication
    4. mass communication
    Show Answer

    Answer: (B) intrapersonal communication.

    Intrapersonal communication takes place within oneself: thinking, planning, self-talk. Interpersonal communication is between two persons.

    Read this in the notes: Unit 4, Communication: Meaning, Types and Characteristics

    Q6. Which of the following models describes communication as helical, growing and building on itself over time?
    1. Aristotle's
    2. Berlo's
    3. Lasswell's
    4. Dance's
    Show Answer

    Answer: (D) Dance's.

    Frank Dance (1967) pictured communication as a helix: it never returns to the same point but builds on what went before. Aristotle's, Lasswell's and Berlo's models are linear.

    Q7. Given below are two statements, one labelled Assertion (A) and the other Reason (R).Assertion (A): Feedback makes communication a two-way process.Reason (R): Through feedback the sender learns how the message has been received and understood.Choose the correct answer.
    1. Both (A) and (R) are true and (R) is the correct explanation of (A)
    2. Both (A) and (R) are true but (R) is not the correct explanation of (A)
    3. (A) is true but (R) is false
    4. (A) is false but (R) is true
    Show Answer

    Answer: (A) Both (A) and (R) are true and (R) is the correct explanation of (A).

    Without feedback the sender cannot know whether the message was understood as intended; with it, sender and receiver exchange roles. Both statements are true and (R) explains (A).

    Q8. A junior lecturer of the Statistics department writes directly to the Dean of another faculty about sharing a laboratory. The direction of this communication is:
    1. upward
    2. horizontal
    3. diagonal
    4. downward
    Show Answer

    Answer: (C) diagonal.

    Diagonal communication crosses both levels and departments. Upward communication goes to one's own superior; horizontal is between equals.

    Q9. In Aristotle's model of communication the three main elements are:
    1. speaker, speech and audience
    2. source, message and channel
    3. sender, noise and receiver
    4. encoder, decoder and interpreter
    Show Answer

    Answer: (A) speaker, speech and audience.

    The classical model, concerned with persuasion, has the speaker, the speech and the audience (with the occasion and the effect). It is the earliest linear model.

    Read this in the notes: Unit 4, Communication: Meaning, Types and Characteristics

    1. to speak
    2. common
    3. to send
    4. message
    Show Answer

    Answer: (B) common.

    Communis means ‘common’: to communicate is to make an idea common to sender and receiver, that is, to share meaning.

    Q11. Match the kind of non-verbal communication in List I with what it concerns in List II.
    List IList II
    (a) Kinesics(i) Touch

    (c) Haptics(iii) Body movement, gesture and posture

    Q12. A speaker conveys sarcasm by the pitch and stress with which the words “Very good” are said. The cue lies in:
    1. kinesics
    2. artifactics
    3. proxemics
    4. paralanguage
    Show Answer

    Answer: (D) paralanguage.

    Paralanguage (vocalics) is everything about the voice other than the words: pitch, tone, volume, pace and pauses. The same words can praise or mock according to it.

    Read this in the notes: Unit 4, Effective Communication: Verbal and Non-verbal

    Q13. In Edward T. Hall's classification of distances, a teacher lecturing to a class from the platform, some four metres or more from the nearest student, is using:
    1. intimate distance
    2. personal distance
    3. public distance
    4. social distance
    Show Answer

    Answer: (C) public distance.

    Hall's distances: intimate (up to about 45 cm), personal (about 45 cm to 1.2 m), social (about 1.2 to 3.6 m) and public (beyond about 3.6 m).

    Read this in the notes: Unit 4, Effective Communication: Verbal and Non-verbal

    Q14. Which of the following is NOT one of the seven Cs of effective communication as business communication lists them?
    1. Concise
    2. Complex
    3. Courteous
    4. Concrete
    Show Answer

    Answer: (B) Complex.

    The seven Cs are clear, concise, concrete, correct, coherent, complete and courteous. Complexity works against clarity.

    Q15. Consider the statements about Mehrabian's 7–38–55 finding:(I) It concerns messages about feelings and attitudes.(II) It applies when the words and the tone or face disagree.(III) It shows that in every kind of communication only 7% of the meaning is carried by words.(IV) The 55% refers to facial expression.Which of the statements are correct?
    1. I and II only
    2. II and III only
    3. I, II, III and IV
    4. I, II and IV only
    Show Answer

    Answer: (D) I, II and IV only.

    Mehrabian studied how listeners judge a speaker's feelings when words and non-verbal cues conflict: about 7% words, 38% tone of voice, 55% face. It is often misquoted as a law of all communication, which it is not: (III) is false.

    Read this in the notes: Unit 4, Effective Communication: Verbal and Non-verbal

    Q16. Given below are two statements, one labelled Assertion (A) and the other Reason (R).Assertion (A): When a speaker's words and non-verbal signals contradict each other, listeners tend to believe the non-verbal signals.Reason (R): Non-verbal signals are harder to control deliberately than words.Choose the correct answer.
    1. Both (A) and (R) are true and (R) is the correct explanation of (A)
    2. Both (A) and (R) are true but (R) is not the correct explanation of (A)
    3. (A) is true but (R) is false
    4. (A) is false but (R) is true
    Show Answer

    Answer: (A) Both (A) and (R) are true and (R) is the correct explanation of (A).

    A teacher who says “Take your time” while looking at the clock is believed by the clock. Because gesture, face and voice are less under conscious control, they are taken as the truer guide. Both statements are true and (R) is the reason.

    Read this in the notes: Unit 4, Effective Communication: Verbal and Non-verbal

    Q17. Which of the following best describes active listening?
    1. Hearing the words while preparing one's reply
    2. Remaining silent throughout
    3. Attending fully, not interrupting, asking questions and restating what was heard
    4. Listening only to the parts one agrees with
    Show Answer

    Answer: (C) Attending fully, not interrupting, asking questions and restating what was heard.

    Hearing is physical; listening is mental. Active listening adds attention, questions and paraphrase, so that the speaker knows the message has been received.

    Q19. A candidate arrives twenty minutes late for an interview, and the panel forms an unfavourable impression before a word is spoken. The non-verbal cue involved is:
    1. haptics
    2. chronemics
    3. oculesics
    4. kinesics

    Answer: (B) chronemics.

    Chronemics is communication through the use of time: punctuality, waiting, pauses. Lateness is read as a message about the person's attitude.

    Q20. In a high-context culture, as Edward T. Hall described it:
    1. meaning is stated fully and explicitly in words
    2. written contracts matter more than relationships
    3. silence carries no meaning
    4. much of the meaning lies in the setting, the relationship and what is left unsaid
    Show Answer

    Answer: (D) much of the meaning lies in the setting, the relationship and what is left unsaid.

    In high-context cultures the listener is expected to read the situation; in low-context cultures the speaker is expected to say exactly what is meant.

    Q21. A host says “We will see” to a visitor's proposal, meaning a polite refusal; the visitor takes it as an open question. The misunderstanding arises mainly from a difference between:
    1. formal and informal channels
    2. upward and downward communication
    3. high-context and low-context styles of communication
    4. verbal and written communication
    Show Answer

    Answer: (C) high-context and low-context styles of communication.

    The host relies on context to convey the refusal; the visitor reads only the literal words. This is the classic difficulty between high-context and low-context communicators.

    Q22. Which of the following is NOT one of Hofstede's dimensions of national culture?
    1. Power distance
    2. Uncertainty avoidance
    3. Individualism and collectivism
    4. High and low context
    Show Answer

    Answer: (D) High and low context.

    Hofstede's dimensions are power distance, individualism–collectivism, masculinity–femininity, uncertainty avoidance, long-term–short-term orientation and indulgence–restraint. High and low context is Hall's distinction.

    3. 5, 3, 1, 4, 2

    Q24. In its second week a new research team quarrels over who should do what; it then agrees on rules and roles. The team has moved:
    1. from forming to storming
    2. from storming to norming
    3. from norming to performing
    4. from performing to adjourning
    Show Answer

    Answer: (B) from storming to norming.

    Conflict over roles is the storming stage; settling rules and roles is norming.

    Q25. Judging the customs of another culture by the standards of one's own, and finding them inferior, is called:
    1. stereotyping
    2. groupthink
    3. ethnocentrism
    4. acculturation
    Show Answer

    Answer: (C) ethnocentrism.

    Ethnocentrism treats one's own culture as the measure of others. Stereotyping is attributing a fixed set of traits to every member of a group.

    Read this in the notes: Unit 4, Inter-cultural and Group Communication

    Q26. A close-knit committee approves a risky plan without examining the alternatives, because no member wishes to disturb the agreement. This is an example of:
    1. groupthink
    2. brainstorming
    3. the grapevine
    4. the two-step flow
    Show Answer

    Answer: (A) groupthink.

    Groupthink (Irving Janis, 1972) is the tendency of a cohesive group to put agreement above realistic appraisal of alternatives.

    Read this in the notes: Unit 4, Inter-cultural and Group Communication

    Q27. In which communication network of a small group do all messages pass through one central person?
    1. Circle
    2. All-channel
    3. Chain
    4. Wheel
    Show Answer

    Answer: (D) Wheel.

    The wheel is the most centralised network and is quick for simple tasks. In the all-channel network everyone communicates with everyone, which suits complex problems and gives the most satisfaction.

    Mary Budd Rowe showed that pausing a few seconds after a question brings longer and better answers from more students.

    Q32. Which of the following is a higher-order, open question?

    3. Who introduced the term ‘statistics’?

    Q33. Consider the statements about feedback given by a teacher to students:(I) It should be prompt.(II) It should be specific.(III) It should be about the work and not the person.(IV) It is most useful when given only at the end of the course.Which of the statements are correct?

    Q38. A manager reports only the good news from the shop floor to the director. This is called:
    1. noise
    2. selective perception
    3. filtering
    4. information overload
    Show Answer

    Answer: (C) filtering.

    Filtering is the deliberate shaping of information by the sender so that it will be seen favourably by the receiver. It is an organisational barrier, common in upward communication.

    Read this in the notes: Unit 4, Barriers to Effective Communication

    Q39. A listener who dislikes a speaker hears only the points that confirm that dislike. The barrier is:
    1. physical
    2. semantic
    3. organisational
    4. perceptual
    Show Answer

    Answer: (D) perceptual.

    Selective perception: people notice and remember what fits their existing frame of reference. Together with stereotypes it makes up the perceptual barriers.

    Read this in the notes: Unit 4, Barriers to Effective Communication

    Q40. Which of the following is a physiological barrier to communication?
    1. Ambiguous wording
    2. Hearing difficulty or fatigue
    3. Rigid hierarchy
    4. Distance between speaker and listener
    Show Answer

    Answer: (B) Hearing difficulty or fatigue.

    Physiological barriers lie in the body of the communicator or receiver: poor hearing or sight, fatigue, illness. Distance is a physical barrier.

    Read this in the notes: Unit 4, Barriers to Effective Communication

    Q41. Consider the following ways of overcoming barriers to communication:(I) Using simple and clear language(II) Checking understanding through feedback(III) Listening actively and with empathy(IV) Sending as much information as possible in every messageWhich of them are effective?

    2. I and II only
    3. II, III and IV only
    4. I, II, III and IV
    Show Answer

    Answer: (A) I, II and III only.

    Too much information in a message causes overload, itself a barrier. Simple language, feedback, the right channel, active listening and empathy are the remedies.

    Read this in the notes: Unit 4, Barriers to Effective Communication

    Q42. Given below are two statements, one labelled Assertion (A) and the other Reason (R).Assertion (A): Noise is a barrier to communication.Reason (R): In communication theory, noise means only loud sound in the physical environment.Choose the correct answer.
    1. Both (A) and (R) are true and (R) is the correct explanation of (A)
    2. Both (A) and (R) are true but (R) is not the correct explanation of (A)

    Answer: (C) (A) is true but (R) is false.

    (A) is true. (R) is false: noise is anything that distorts the message at any stage. It may be physical (sound), physiological, semantic (misunderstood words) or psychological (prejudice, anxiety).

    Read this in the notes: Unit 4, Barriers to Effective Communication

    Q43. Match the theory of mass communication in List I with its idea in List II.
    List IList II
    (a) Hypodermic needle(i) An active audience uses media to satisfy its own needs
    (b) Two-step flow(ii) Media tell people what to think about, not what to think

    Q44. During an election a news channel devotes most of its time to unemployment, and voters later name unemployment as the most important issue. This fits the theory of:
    1. cultivation
    2. agenda-setting
    3. spiral of silence
    4. uses and gratifications
    Show Answer

    Answer: (B) agenda-setting.

    Agenda-setting (McCombs and Shaw, 1972): the prominence the media give to an issue shapes how important the public thinks it is.

    Q45. The statement “the medium is the message” and the idea of the ‘global village’ are due to:
    1. Marshall McLuhan
    2. Harold Lasswell
    3. Wilbur Schramm
    4. George Gerbner
    Show Answer

    Answer: (A) Marshall McLuhan.

    Q46. According to the spiral of silence theory of Elisabeth Noelle-Neumann:
    1. heavy television viewing shapes beliefs about the real world
    2. audiences choose media to satisfy their needs
    3. people who believe they are in the minority tend to remain silent, which makes the majority view seem stronger
    4. media messages reach people through opinion leaders
    Show Answer

    Answer: (C) people who believe they are in the minority tend to remain silent, which makes the majority view seem stronger.

    Fear of isolation keeps those who think they are in the minority from speaking, so the apparent majority grows. Option A is Gerbner's cultivation theory.
