    Model Questions — Unit 7: Data Interpretation

    UGC NET Paper I (General Paper) · 50 questions · Click "Show Answer" for the worked solution

    About this set

    Sources and classification of data, scales of measurement, graphical representation, data and governance, and six data sets (tables, a pie chart given as percentages, and time series) with five questions on each.

    These are model questions written for practice at the standard of the paper; they are not questions from a past paper. Try each one before opening the answer. Every answer gives the working and a link to the section of the Unit 7 notes that teaches it.

    The questions

    Q1. A researcher interviews 300 shopkeepers about digital payments and also uses the monthly figures of digital transactions published by a bank. The two sets of data are, respectively:
    1. secondary and primary
    2. primary and secondary
    3. both primary
    4. both secondary
    Show Answer

    Answer: (B) primary and secondary.

    Data the investigator collects for the present purpose are primary; data collected by someone else for another purpose, such as published figures, are secondary.

    Read this in the notes: Unit 7, Sources, Acquisition and Classification of Data

    Q2. The difference between a questionnaire and a schedule is that:
    1. a questionnaire has open questions and a schedule closed ones
    2. a questionnaire is used in a census and a schedule in a sample survey
    3. a questionnaire collects qualitative data and a schedule quantitative data
    4. a questionnaire is filled in by the respondent, and a schedule by the enumerator who asks the questions
    Show Answer

    Answer: (D) a questionnaire is filled in by the respondent, and a schedule by the enumerator who asks the questions.

    Both are lists of questions. The questionnaire is completed by the respondent, often by post or online; the schedule is completed by an enumerator in an interview, which suits respondents who cannot read or write.

    Read this in the notes: Unit 7, Sources, Acquisition and Classification of Data

    Q4. Consider the statements:(I) A census covers every unit of the population.(II) A sample survey covers a part of the population chosen to represent it.(III) Secondary data are always more accurate than primary data.(IV) Primary data are generally costlier and slower to collect than secondary data.Which of the statements are correct?
    1. I and II only
    2. I, II and III only
    3. I, II and IV only
    4. I, II, III and IV
    Show Answer

    Answer: (C) I, II and IV only.

    (III) is false: secondary data may not fit the purpose, and their definitions, coverage and accuracy have to be checked before use. The other three are correct.

    Q5. Given below are two statements, one labelled Assertion (A) and the other Reason (R).Assertion (A): Secondary data should be scrutinised before they are used.Reason (R): They were collected by another agency, for a different purpose, with its own definitions and methods.Choose the correct answer.

    Answer: (A) Both (A) and (R) are true and (R) is the correct explanation of (A).

    Because the user did not design the collection, the definitions, the coverage, the period and the accuracy may not suit the new purpose. Both statements are true and (R) is the reason.

    Q6. Which of the following is NOT a method of collecting primary data?
    1. Direct personal interview
    2. Observation
    3. Mailed questionnaire
    4. Use of tables from a published census report
    Show Answer

    Answer: (D) Use of tables from a published census report.

    Interview, observation, questionnaire, schedule and experiment produce primary data. Published reports, census tables, journals and administrative records are sources of secondary data.

    Q7. Match the scale of measurement in List I with the example in List II.
    List IList II
    (a) Nominal(i) Temperature in degrees Celsius
    (b) Ordinal(ii) Monthly income
    (c) Interval(iii) Blood group
    (d) Ratio(iv) Rank in class

    2. (a)-(iv), (b)-(iii), (c)-(i), (d)-(ii)
    3. (a)-(iii), (b)-(iv), (c)-(i), (d)-(ii)

    Answer: (C) (a)-(iii), (b)-(iv), (c)-(i), (d)-(ii).

    Nominal: names without order. Ordinal: order without equal differences. Interval: equal differences but no true zero. Ratio: equal differences and a true zero.

    Q8. The statement “40°C is twice as hot as 20°C” is not meaningful because the Celsius scale is:
    1. a nominal scale
    2. an interval scale, whose zero is arbitrary
    3. an ordinal scale
    4. a ratio scale
    Show Answer

    Answer: (B) an interval scale, whose zero is arbitrary.

    Ratios make sense only on a ratio scale, which has a true zero. Zero on the Celsius scale is not an absence of heat, so differences are meaningful (40 is 20 degrees warmer) but ratios are not.

    Read this in the notes: Unit 7, Quantitative and Qualitative Data

    Q9. Responses to an item are recorded as strongly disagree, disagree, neutral, agree, strongly agree. The most appropriate measure of central tendency is the:
    1. median
    2. arithmetic mean
    3. geometric mean
    4. harmonic mean
    Show Answer

    Answer: (A) median.

    The responses are ordered, but the steps between them are not known to be equal: an ordinal scale. The median (and the mode) are meaningful; a mean of such codes is at best a convention.

    Read this in the notes: Unit 7, Quantitative and Qualitative Data

    Q10. Consider the following variables:(I) Number of children in a family(II) Height of a student(III) Number of road accidents in a month(IV) Time taken to finish a testWhich of them are discrete?
    1. I and II only
    2. II and IV only
    3. I, II and III only
    4. I and III only
    Show Answer

    Answer: (D) I and III only.

    Discrete variables are counts that take separate values: children, accidents. Height and time are measurements that can take any value in a range: continuous.

    Read this in the notes: Unit 7, Quantitative and Qualitative Data

    Q11. Consider the statements about scales of measurement:(I) The four scales were set out by S. S. Stevens.(II) A ratio scale has a true zero.(III) The mean is an appropriate average for nominal data.(IV) The mode can be found for data on any of the four scales.Which of the statements are correct?
    1. I and II only
    2. I, II and III only
    3. I, II and IV only
    4. I, II, III and IV
    Show Answer

    Answer: (C) I, II and IV only.

    Nominal data are categories without order or size, so they cannot be averaged by the mean; only the mode applies: (III) is false. The mode, the commonest value, can be found on every scale.

    Read this in the notes: Unit 7, Quantitative and Qualitative Data

    Q12. Which statement about a histogram is correct?
    1. Its bars are separated by gaps, and only their heights matter
    2. Its bars touch, and the area of each bar represents the frequency of its class
    3. It is used to compare categories such as states
    4. It shows the shares of a whole as sectors
    Show Answer

    Answer: (B) Its bars touch, and the area of each bar represents the frequency of its class.

    A histogram shows the distribution of a continuous variable: the bars touch, and area represents frequency, so classes of unequal width need adjusted heights. Separate bars for categories make a bar chart; sectors make a pie chart.

    Read this in the notes: Unit 7, Graphical Representation and Mapping of Data

    Q13. In a pie chart, an item that accounts for 35% of the total is represented by a sector whose central angle is:
    1. 35°
    2. 63°
    3. 105°
    4. 126°
    Show Answer

    Answer: (D) 126°.

    The whole circle of $360^\circ$ stands for 100%, so each 1% takes $3.6^\circ$ and 35% takes $35\times3.6=126^\circ$. The first option confuses the percentage with the angle.

    Read this in the notes: Unit 7, Graphical Representation and Mapping of Data

    Q15. Which of the following graphs is used to locate the median and the quartiles of a frequency distribution?
    1. Pie chart
    2. Bar chart
    3. Ogive
    4. Choropleth map
    Show Answer

    Answer: (C) Ogive.

    An ogive is a cumulative frequency curve. Reading across from half (or a quarter, or three quarters) of the total frequency to the curve and down to the axis gives the median (or the quartiles).

    Read this in the notes: Unit 7, Graphical Representation and Mapping of Data

    Q16. A map on which each district is shaded according to its literacy rate is called a:
    1. dot map
    2. choropleth map
    3. proportional symbol map
    4. flow map
    Show Answer

    Answer: (B) choropleth map.

    A choropleth map shades areas by the value of a variable. A dot map places one dot for a fixed number of cases, and a proportional symbol map draws a circle whose size depends on the value.

    Read this in the notes: Unit 7, Graphical Representation and Mapping of Data

    List IList II
    (a) Collection of Statistics Act, 2008(i) The decennial population census

    Q20. Consider the following as elements of good data governance:(I) Data that are accurate, timely and consistent(II) Protection of privacy and security(III) Clear responsibility for each dataset(IV) Open publication of all data, including personal data, without restrictionWhich of them are correct?
    1. I and II only
    2. I, II and III only
    3. II, III and IV only
    4. I, II, III and IV
    Show Answer

    Answer: (B) I, II and III only.

    Good governance of data means quality, access where it can be open, privacy and security where data must be protected, and accountability. Personal data are not to be published without restriction: (IV) is false.

    Q47. In which month did the sales of Company Y exceed those of Company X for the first time?
    1. March
    2. May
    3. June
    4. April
    Show Answer

    Answer: (D) April.

    Y is below X in January (25 against 30), February (30 against 36) and March (40 against 45). In April Y sells 44 against X's 40: on the line chart the two lines cross between March and April.

    Q48. By what percentage did the sales of Company Y in June exceed those of Company X in June?
    1. 22.2%
    2. 18.2%
    3. 12%
    4. 20%
    Show Answer

    Answer: (A) 22.2%.

    $\dfrac{66-54}{54}\times100=22.2\%$. The base is X's sales, 54; dividing by 66 would give 18.2%, the percentage by which X fell short of Y.

    Q49. The average monthly sales of Company Y over the six months were about:
