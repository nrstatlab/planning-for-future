    Model Questions — Unit 2: Research Aptitude

    UGC NET Paper I (General Paper) · 50 questions · Click "Show Answer" for the worked solution

    About this set

    The meaning and types of research, positivism and post-positivism, experimental, descriptive and historical methods, qualitative and quantitative approaches, the steps of research, thesis writing and referencing, ICT in research, and research ethics.

    These are model questions written for practice at the standard of the paper; they are not questions from a past paper. Try each one before opening the answer. Every answer gives the working and a link to the section of the Unit 2 notes that teaches it.

    The questions

    Q1. A college teacher finds that her students interpret graphs badly, introduces a weekly exercise, measures the change at the end of the term and revises the exercise for the next term. This is:
    1. fundamental research
    2. action research
    3. historical research
    4. ex post facto research
    Show Answer

    Answer: (B) action research.

    Action research is done by a practitioner to improve their own practice, in cycles of plan, act, observe and reflect. It is credited to Kurt Lewin; Stephen Corey took it into education. It does not aim at theory or at generalising beyond the classroom.

    Read this in the notes: Unit 2, Research: Meaning, Types and Characteristics

    Q2. Match the type of research in List I with its description in List II.
    List IList II
    (a) Fundamental(i) Studies the same units over a period of time
    (b) Applied(ii) Seeks to find out what the problem is
    (c) Exploratory(iii) Aims to extend knowledge or build theory
    (d) Longitudinal(iv) Aims to solve a practical problem
    1. (a)-(iii), (b)-(iv), (c)-(i), (d)-(ii)
    2. (a)-(iv), (b)-(iii), (c)-(ii), (d)-(i)
    3. (a)-(iii), (b)-(ii), (c)-(iv), (d)-(i)
    4. (a)-(iii), (b)-(iv), (c)-(ii), (d)-(i)
    Show Answer

    Answer: (D) (a)-(iii), (b)-(iv), (c)-(ii), (d)-(i).

    Fundamental (pure, basic) research extends knowledge with no application in view; applied research solves a practical problem; exploratory research clarifies a problem not yet well understood; longitudinal research follows the same units over time, unlike a cross-sectional study at one point.

    Read this in the notes: Unit 2, Research: Meaning, Types and Characteristics

    Q3. Consider the statements about the characteristics of research:(I) It is systematic: it follows a planned sequence of steps.(II) It is empirical: it rests on evidence.(III) It is replicable: another researcher following the same method should reach the same result.(IV) Its findings depend on the personal preferences of the researcher.Which of the statements are correct?
    1. I, II and III only
    2. I and II only
    3. II, III and IV only
    4. I, II, III and IV
    Show Answer

    Answer: (A) I, II and III only.

    Research is systematic, empirical, logical, replicable, critical and cumulative, and it is objective: its findings should not depend on who did it. So (IV) is false.

    Read this in the notes: Unit 2, Research: Meaning, Types and Characteristics

    Q4. A study measures reading ability in a sample of Class V, Class VIII and Class X students in the same month and compares the three groups. By its time dimension the study is:
    1. longitudinal
    2. experimental
    3. cross-sectional
    4. a panel study
    Show Answer

    Answer: (C) cross-sectional.

    It observes different groups at one point in time: cross-sectional. A longitudinal (or panel) study would follow the same students from Class V to Class X. Nothing is manipulated, so it is not experimental.

    Read this in the notes: Unit 2, Research: Meaning, Types and Characteristics

    Q5. Given below are two statements, one labelled Assertion (A) and the other Reason (R).Assertion (A): Action research does not aim at generalisations that hold for all settings.Reason (R): Its purpose is to improve the practitioner's own practice in a particular setting.Choose the correct answer.
    1. Both (A) and (R) are true and (R) is the correct explanation of (A)
    2. Both (A) and (R) are true but (R) is not the correct explanation of (A)
    3. (A) is true but (R) is false
    4. (A) is false but (R) is true
    Show Answer

    Answer: (A) Both (A) and (R) are true and (R) is the correct explanation of (A).

    Action research is local and practical by design: the practitioner studies and improves what happens in her own classroom or institution. Both statements are true and (R) is the reason for (A).

    Read this in the notes: Unit 2, Research: Meaning, Types and Characteristics

    Q6. A study examines whether a new theory of consumer choice holds, with no application in view; another study tries to reduce the waiting time at the outpatient desk of one hospital. These are, respectively:
    1. applied and fundamental research
    2. fundamental and applied research
    3. action and fundamental research
    4. descriptive and historical research
    Show Answer

    Answer: (B) fundamental and applied research.

    Testing or building theory for its own sake is fundamental research; solving a particular practical problem is applied research.

    Read this in the notes: Unit 2, Research: Meaning, Types and Characteristics

    Q7. Match the name in List I with the idea in List II.
    List IList II
    (a) Auguste Comte(i) Verstehen (interpretive understanding)
    (b) Karl Popper(ii) Grounded theory
    (c) Max Weber(iii) Positivism
    (d) Glaser and Strauss(iv) Falsification
    1. (a)-(iii), (b)-(i), (c)-(iv), (d)-(ii)
    2. (a)-(iv), (b)-(iii), (c)-(i), (d)-(ii)
    3. (a)-(iii), (b)-(iv), (c)-(i), (d)-(ii)
    4. (a)-(iii), (b)-(iv), (c)-(ii), (d)-(i)
    Show Answer

    Answer: (C) (a)-(iii), (b)-(iv), (c)-(i), (d)-(ii).

    Comte is the founder of positivism. Popper held that a scientific statement must be falsifiable. Weber's Verstehen is the understanding of the meanings people give to their actions, the root of interpretivism. Glaser and Strauss (1967) proposed grounded theory.

    Read this in the notes: Unit 2, Positivism and the Post-positivistic Approach

    Q8. According to Karl Popper, the statement “All swans are white” is scientific because:
    1. a large number of white swans have been observed
    2. it has been proved by induction
    3. it is accepted by most scientists
    4. it can be shown false by a single observation of a black swan
    Show Answer

    Answer: (D) it can be shown false by a single observation of a black swan.

    Popper's criterion is falsifiability: a scientific statement must forbid something, so that an observation could refute it. No number of white swans proves the statement; one black swan refutes it. A theory is never proved, only not yet refuted.

    Read this in the notes: Unit 2, Positivism and the Post-positivistic Approach

    Q9. Consider the statements:(I) Positivism holds that reality exists independently and can be known directly by observation and measurement.(II) Post-positivism holds that knowledge is provisional and that a theory can never be finally proved.(III) Interpretivism holds that social reality is constructed by people and seeks their meanings.(IV) Post-positivism rejects the use of quantitative methods altogether.Which of the statements are correct?
    1. I, II and III only
    2. I and II only
    3. I, III and IV only
    4. I, II, III and IV
    Show Answer

    Answer: (A) I, II and III only.

    Post-positivism keeps the scientific method and quantitative measurement but admits error: it uses several measures and methods (triangulation) and treats findings as the best current estimate. It does not reject quantitative methods, so (IV) is false.

    Read this in the notes: Unit 2, Positivism and the Post-positivistic Approach

    Q10. A researcher starts from a theory, derives a hypothesis from it and then collects data to test the hypothesis. The reasoning followed is:
    1. inductive
    2. abductive
    3. deductive
    4. analogical
    Show Answer

    Answer: (C) deductive.

    Deduction goes from the general (theory) to the particular (a hypothesis tested on data); it is the logic of the positivist, quantitative tradition. Induction goes the other way, from observations to a general statement, as in much qualitative research.

    Read this in the notes: Unit 2, Positivism and the Post-positivistic Approach

    Q11. A researcher lives for a year in a village and interviews women at length about what “a good school” means to them. The approach is:
    1. positivist
    2. interpretivist
    3. experimental
    4. post-positivist
    Show Answer

    Answer: (B) interpretivist.

    The study seeks the participants' own meanings, not a measured effect or a law: interpretivism (constructivism), usually pursued by qualitative methods.

    Read this in the notes: Unit 2, Positivism and the Post-positivistic Approach

    Q12. Sixty students are divided at random into two groups. One group is taught with simulations and the other without, and both take the same test. The design is:
    1. pre-experimental
    2. quasi-experimental
    3. ex post facto
    4. true experimental
    Show Answer

    Answer: (D) true experimental.

    Random assignment together with a control group makes a true experimental design (Campbell and Stanley). If two existing sections had been used without random assignment it would be quasi-experimental; with one group and no control, pre-experimental.

    Read this in the notes: Unit 2, Methods of Research: Experimental, Descriptive, Historical

    Q13. In an experiment one group of students is taught with simulations and another without, and both groups take the same test. The independent and the dependent variables are, respectively:
    1. the test score and the method of teaching
    2. the method of teaching and the test score
    3. the students and the teacher
    4. random assignment and the control group
    Show Answer

    Answer: (B) the method of teaching and the test score.

    The independent variable is the one the researcher manipulates, the presumed cause: the method of teaching. The dependent variable is the effect that is observed: the test score.

    Read this in the notes: Unit 2, Methods of Research: Experimental, Descriptive, Historical

    Q14. Given below are two statements, one labelled Assertion (A) and the other Reason (R).Assertion (A): Only the experimental method can establish a cause-and-effect relation directly.Reason (R): In an experiment the researcher manipulates the independent variable while controlling other variables.Choose the correct answer.
    1. Both (A) and (R) are true and (R) is the correct explanation of (A)
    2. Both (A) and (R) are true but (R) is not the correct explanation of (A)
    3. (A) is true but (R) is false
    4. (A) is false but (R) is true
    Show Answer

    Answer: (A) Both (A) and (R) are true and (R) is the correct explanation of (A).

    Because the researcher changes one thing and holds the rest alike (by control and random assignment), a difference in the outcome can be put down to the treatment. Descriptive and correlational studies show association only. Both statements are true and (R) is the reason.

    Read this in the notes: Unit 2, Methods of Research: Experimental, Descriptive, Historical

    Q15. In a study of a new teaching method, the students simply grow older and more mature during the year, and this by itself improves their scores. This threat to internal validity is called:
    1. history
    2. instrumentation
    3. maturation
    4. attrition
    Show Answer

    Answer: (C) maturation.

    Maturation is change in the subjects themselves with the passage of time. History is an outside event during the study; instrumentation a change in the measuring instrument; attrition the loss of subjects.

    Read this in the notes: Unit 2, Methods of Research: Experimental, Descriptive, Historical

    Q16. Consider the statements:(I) Internal validity concerns whether the observed effect is really due to the treatment.(II) External validity concerns whether the result holds beyond the subjects and setting of the study.(III) Random assignment mainly strengthens internal validity.(IV) A descriptive survey can by itself establish causation.Which of the statements are correct?
    1. I and II only
    2. I, II and IV only
    3. II, III and IV only
    4. I, II and III only
    Show Answer

    Answer: (D) I, II and III only.

    Random assignment makes the groups alike in everything but the treatment, which protects internal validity; random selection from the population is what helps external validity. A survey shows things as they are and can show association, not cause: (IV) is false.

    Read this in the notes: Unit 2, Methods of Research: Experimental, Descriptive, Historical

    Q17. A historian finds a letter said to have been written in 1857 and examines its paper, ink and handwriting to decide whether it is genuine. She is carrying out:
    1. external criticism
    2. internal criticism
    3. content analysis
    4. triangulation
    Show Answer

    Answer: (A) external criticism.

    External criticism asks whether the document is authentic: is it what it claims to be, from the time it claims? Internal criticism comes next and asks whether what it says is credible and accurate.

    Read this in the notes: Unit 2, Methods of Research: Experimental, Descriptive, Historical

    Q18. Which of the following is a primary source for a historical study of a university?
    1. A textbook of the history of education
    2. A newspaper article written last year about its early days
    3. The minutes of the meetings of its governing body
    4. An encyclopaedia entry on the university
    Show Answer

    Answer: (C) The minutes of the meetings of its governing body.

    Primary sources are first-hand records made at the time: original documents, minutes, letters, eyewitness accounts. Textbooks, later articles and encyclopaedias report or interpret such records and are secondary sources.

    Read this in the notes: Unit 2, Methods of Research: Experimental, Descriptive, Historical

    Q19. Which of the following is NOT a descriptive method of research?
    1. Survey
    2. Case study
    3. Correlational study
    4. Randomised controlled trial
    Show Answer

    Answer: (D) Randomised controlled trial.

    Descriptive research describes things as they are without manipulating anything: surveys, case studies, correlational, developmental and observational studies. A randomised controlled trial manipulates a treatment and is experimental.

    Read this in the notes: Unit 2, Methods of Research: Experimental, Descriptive, Historical

    Q20. Match the qualitative approach in List I with its focus in List II.
    List IList II
    (a) Ethnography(i) Building a theory from the data
    (b) Phenomenology(ii) An intensive study of a single unit
    (c) Grounded theory(iii) A culture or group studied from the inside
    (d) Case study(iv) The lived experience of a phenomenon
    1. (a)-(iii), (b)-(iv), (c)-(ii), (d)-(i)
    2. (a)-(iii), (b)-(iv), (c)-(i), (d)-(ii)
    3. (a)-(iv), (b)-(iii), (c)-(i), (d)-(ii)
    4. (a)-(i), (b)-(iv), (c)-(iii), (d)-(ii)
    Show Answer

    Answer: (B) (a)-(iii), (b)-(iv), (c)-(i), (d)-(ii).

    Ethnography studies a culture from within, often by long participant observation; phenomenology asks what an experience is like for those who live it; grounded theory builds theory from data; a case study examines one person, group or institution in depth.

    Q21. Consider the statements about quantitative and qualitative research:(I) Quantitative research is mainly deductive; qualitative research is mainly inductive.(II) Quantitative research usually uses large probability samples; qualitative research small purposive ones.(III) Thematic analysis is a technique of quantitative research.(IV) Mixed-methods research uses both kinds of data.Which of the statements are correct?
    1. I, II and IV only
    2. I and II only
    3. I, III and IV only
    4. I, II, III and IV
    Show Answer

    Answer: (A) I, II and IV only.

    Thematic, content and narrative analysis are ways of analysing words and meanings: qualitative. Quantitative analysis is statistical. The other three statements are correct.

    Read this in the notes: Unit 2, Qualitative and Quantitative Methods

    Q22. A study of people recovering from drug addiction recruits each participant through an earlier participant, because no list of such people exists. The sampling method is:
    1. quota sampling
    2. systematic sampling
    3. snowball sampling
    4. stratified sampling
    Show Answer

    Answer: (C) snowball sampling.

    In snowball sampling each participant names or brings others. It is a non-probability method suited to hidden or hard-to-reach populations for which there is no sampling frame.

    Read this in the notes: Unit 2, Qualitative and Quantitative Methods

    Q23. Which of the following lists contains only probability sampling methods?
    1. Simple random, quota, cluster
    2. Stratified, purposive, systematic
    3. Convenience, snowball, quota
    4. Simple random, stratified, systematic, cluster
    Show Answer

    Answer: (D) Simple random, stratified, systematic, cluster.

    In probability sampling every unit has a known, non-zero chance of selection: simple random, stratified, systematic and cluster sampling. Convenience, purposive, quota and snowball sampling are non-probability methods.

    Read this in the notes: Unit 2, Qualitative and Quantitative Methods

    Q24. An interviewer is told to interview 30 men and 30 women, choosing whoever is convenient until the numbers are complete. This is:
    1. stratified random sampling
    2. quota sampling
    3. cluster sampling
    4. systematic sampling
    Show Answer

    Answer: (B) quota sampling.

    Quota sampling fixes the number wanted in each category but leaves the choice of individuals to the interviewer, so it is not a probability method. In stratified random sampling the units within each stratum are chosen at random.

    Read this in the notes: Unit 2, Qualitative and Quantitative Methods

    Q25. To understand why students have stopped using the college library, the most suitable tool is:
    1. in-depth interviews with a small number of students
    2. a count of the books issued each month
    3. a standardised achievement test
    4. a census of all the books in the library
    Show Answer

    Answer: (A) in-depth interviews with a small number of students.

    A ‘why’ question about reasons and meanings calls for qualitative tools: in-depth interviews or focus groups. The count of books issued would show that use has fallen, not why.

    Read this in the notes: Unit 2, Qualitative and Quantitative Methods

    Q26. Given below are two statements, one labelled Assertion (A) and the other Reason (R).Assertion (A): Findings of qualitative research are usually not generalised statistically to a population.Reason (R): Qualitative studies generally use small samples chosen purposively and not at random.Choose the correct answer.
    1. Both (A) and (R) are true and (R) is the correct explanation of (A)
    2. Both (A) and (R) are true but (R) is not the correct explanation of (A)
    3. (A) is true but (R) is false
    4. (A) is false but (R) is true
    Show Answer

    Answer: (A) Both (A) and (R) are true and (R) is the correct explanation of (A).

    Statistical generalisation needs a probability sample of adequate size. Qualitative work chooses a few information-rich cases on purpose and aims at depth of understanding, so its findings are transferred to similar settings by judgement, not by statistical inference. Both statements are true and (R) explains (A).

    Read this in the notes: Unit 2, Qualitative and Quantitative Methods

    Q27. Arrange the following steps of research in the correct order: (1) Collection of data, (2) Formulation of hypotheses, (3) Identification of the problem, (4) Review of literature, (5) Preparation of the research design, (6) Analysis of data.
    1. 3, 2, 4, 5, 1, 6
    2. 4, 3, 2, 5, 1, 6
    3. 3, 4, 2, 5, 1, 6
    4. 3, 4, 5, 2, 6, 1
    Show Answer

    Answer: (C) 3, 4, 2, 5, 1, 6.

    Problem, review of literature, objectives and hypotheses, research design, sampling and collection of data, analysis, then testing of hypotheses, interpretation and the report. The review comes before the hypotheses because it shows what is known and where the gap is.

    Read this in the notes: Unit 2, Steps of Research

    Q28. “Students taught through simulations will score higher in statistics than students taught without them.” This hypothesis is:
    1. a null hypothesis
    2. a non-directional hypothesis
    3. a research question
    4. a directional hypothesis
    Show Answer

    Answer: (D) a directional hypothesis.

    It states that there is a difference and in which direction. The null hypothesis would say there is no difference; a non-directional hypothesis would say only that the two groups differ.

    Read this in the notes: Unit 2, Steps of Research

    Q29. In a study of the effect of hours of study on marks, the previous ability of the students also affects the marks and has to be held constant. Previous ability is:
    1. the independent variable
    2. an extraneous variable
    3. the dependent variable
    4. a criterion variable
    Show Answer

    Answer: (B) an extraneous variable.

    An extraneous variable affects the dependent variable but is not what the study is about; it must be controlled, for instance by matching or by random assignment, or it will be confused with the effect of the independent variable.

    Read this in the notes: Unit 2, Steps of Research

    Q30. Consider the statements about a good hypothesis:(I) It is testable.(II) It is specific.(III) It is stated before the data are seen.(IV) It must always be proved true by the study.Which of the statements are correct?
    1. I, II and III only
    2. I and II only
    3. II, III and IV only
    4. I, II, III and IV
    Show Answer

    Answer: (A) I, II and III only.

    A hypothesis is a testable, specific statement, consistent with known facts and set down in advance. It may be supported or rejected by the data; a rejected hypothesis is still a result. So (IV) is false.

    Read this in the notes: Unit 2, Steps of Research

    Q31. Training improves the knowledge of workers, and the improved knowledge in turn raises their output. In this chain, knowledge is:
    1. an extraneous variable
    2. the independent variable
    3. an intervening (mediating) variable
    4. the dependent variable
    Show Answer

    Answer: (C) an intervening (mediating) variable.

    An intervening or mediating variable carries the effect from the independent variable (training) to the dependent variable (output).

    Read this in the notes: Unit 2, Steps of Research

    Q32. The main purpose of the review of literature in research is to:
    1. increase the length of the thesis
    2. collect the primary data
    3. test the hypothesis
    4. find out what is already known and where the gap lies
    Show Answer

    Answer: (D) find out what is already known and where the gap lies.

    The review shows what has been done, with which methods and results, and what remains unanswered. It lets the researcher define the problem precisely, avoid repeating work, and choose a design.

    Read this in the notes: Unit 2, Steps of Research

    Q33. Which of the following is decided at the stage of the research design?
    1. The conclusions of the study
    2. The method, the variables, the sample and the tools
    3. The title of the published paper
    4. The style of referencing only
    Show Answer

    Answer: (B) The method, the variables, the sample and the tools.

    The research design is the plan of the study: which method, which variables and how they are measured, what sample and what tools. It is made after the problem and hypotheses are fixed and before data are collected.

    Read this in the notes: Unit 2, Steps of Research

    1. Introduction, Methods, Results and Discussion
    2. Index, Methods, References and Data
    3. Introduction, Materials, Review and Discussion
    4. Idea, Method, Review and Decision

    A research article has a title, abstract and keywords, then Introduction, Methods, Results and Discussion, followed by the references.

    Q36. A researcher has six papers, cited 25, 18, 9, 6, 4 and 2 times. Her h-index and i10-index are, respectively:
    1. 6 and 2
    2. 4 and 3
    3. 5 and 2
    4. 4 and 2
    Show Answer

    Answer: (D) 4 and 2.

    The h-index is the largest $h$ such that $h$ papers have at least $h$ citations each. Four papers have at least 4 citations (25, 18, 9, 6), but there are not five with at least 5, so $h=4$. The i10-index counts papers with at least 10 citations: two (25 and 18).

    Read this in the notes: Unit 2, Thesis and Article Writing: Format and Styles of Referencing

    Q37. Match the abbreviation in List I with its meaning in List II.
    List IList II
    (a) ibid.(i) And others
    (b) et al.(ii) Compare
    (c) sic(iii) In the same source as the one just cited
    (d) cf.(iv) Thus, as in the original

    Q38. Which of the following belongs to the reference material placed after the text of a thesis?
    1. Appendices
    2. Abstract
    3. Acknowledgements
    4. Certificate
    Show Answer

    Answer: (A) Appendices.

    A thesis has preliminary pages (title, declaration, certificate, acknowledgements, contents, abstract), the text (introduction to conclusions) and reference material (references or bibliography, and appendices such as questionnaires and raw tables).

    Read this in the notes: Unit 2, Thesis and Article Writing: Format and Styles of Referencing

    Q39.     Q40. An abstract of a research paper should contain:
    1. a detailed review of all earlier studies
    2. the list of references
    3. the problem, the method, the main result and the conclusion, in brief
    4. the questionnaire used
    Show Answer

    Answer: (C) the problem, the method, the main result and the conclusion, in brief.

    The abstract is a short paragraph that lets a reader decide whether to read the paper: what was asked, how it was studied, what was found and what it means.

    Q42. A database search uses the expression: sampling AND agriculture NOT forestry. Compared with a search for sampling alone, this search will return:
    1. fewer records, each containing both of the first two terms and not the third
    2. more records
    3. exactly the same records
    4. records containing any one of the three terms
    Show Answer

    Answer: (A) fewer records, each containing both of the first two terms and not the third.

    AND requires both terms and NOT excludes a term, so both narrow the search. OR is the operator that widens it, returning records with either term.

    Q45. A researcher leaves out three survey responses because they weaken her result, and does not mention it in the report. This is:
    1. plagiarism
    2. falsification
    3. fabrication
    4. duplicate publication
    Show Answer

    Answer: (B) falsification.

    Falsification is changing or omitting data or results so that the research is not accurately represented; nothing need be invented. Fabrication is making up data; plagiarism is using another's words or ideas without credit.

    Q46. A thesis shows 35% similarity after the exclusions permitted by the UGC regulations of 2018 on academic integrity. The level of plagiarism and the consequence for the student are:
    1. Level 1; a revised script to be submitted within the stipulated period
    2. Level 0; no penalty
    3. Level 2; debarred from submitting a revised script for one year
    4. Level 3; registration cancelled
    Show Answer

    Answer: (A) Level 1; a revised script to be submitted within the stipulated period.

    Level 0 is similarity up to 10% (no penalty); Level 1 above 10% to 40% (revised script within the set time, up to six months); Level 2 above 40% to 60% (debarred from resubmitting for a year); Level 3 above 60% (registration cancelled).

    Q47. The three basic ethical principles of the Belmont Report (1979) are:
    1. honesty, objectivity and openness
    2. consent, confidentiality and anonymity
    3. fabrication, falsification and plagiarism
    4. respect for persons, beneficence and justice
    Show Answer

    Answer: (D) respect for persons, beneficence and justice.

    The Belmont Report rests on respect for persons (from which informed consent follows), beneficence (do no harm, maximise benefit) and justice (fair sharing of burdens and benefits). Fabrication, falsification and plagiarism are the three kinds of misconduct.

    Q49. Consider the statements about informed consent in research:(I) Participants must be told the purpose, the procedure and the risks.(II) Participants may withdraw from the study at any time.(III) Consent must be given freely, without pressure.(IV) Once consent is given, participants may be identified by name in the report.Which of the statements are correct?
    1. I and II only
    2. I, II and III only
    3. II, III and IV only
    4. I, II, III and IV
    Show Answer

    Answer: (B) I, II and III only.

    Consent covers taking part; it does not remove the duty of confidentiality and anonymity. Participants should not be identifiable in the report unless they have separately and expressly agreed. So (IV) is false.

    Q50. Given below are two statements, one labelled Assertion (A) and the other Reason (R).Assertion (A): A researcher should avoid publishing in predatory journals.Reason (R): Predatory journals charge fees for publication without genuine peer review.Choose the correct answer.

    4. (A) is false but (R) is true
    Show Answer

    Answer: (A) Both (A) and (R) are true and (R) is the correct explanation of (A).
