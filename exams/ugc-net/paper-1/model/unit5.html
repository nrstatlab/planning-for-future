    Model Questions — Unit 5: Mathematical Reasoning and Aptitude

    UGC NET Paper I (General Paper) · 50 questions · Click "Show Answer" for the worked solution

    About this set

    Types of reasoning, number and letter series, codes and relationships, and mathematical aptitude: fractions, time and distance, ratio, proportion and percentage, profit and loss, interest and discounting, and averages.

    These are model questions written for practice at the standard of the paper; they are not questions from a past paper. Try each one before opening the answer. Every answer gives the working and a link to the section of the Unit 5 notes that teaches it.

    The questions

    Q1. Match the type of reasoning in List I with the example in List II.
    List IList II
    (a) Deductive(i) The grass is wet this morning, so it probably rained in the night
    (b) Inductive(ii) A drug that lowers blood pressure in rats may do so in humans
    (c) Abductive(iii) All metals conduct electricity; copper is a metal; so copper conducts electricity
    (d) Analogical(iv) Every crow seen so far is black, so all crows are black
    1. (a)-(iii), (b)-(i), (c)-(iv), (d)-(ii)
    2. (a)-(iii), (b)-(iv), (c)-(i), (d)-(ii)
    3. (a)-(iv), (b)-(iii), (c)-(i), (d)-(ii)
    4. (a)-(iii), (b)-(iv), (c)-(ii), (d)-(i)
    Show Answer

    Answer: (B) (a)-(iii), (b)-(iv), (c)-(i), (d)-(ii).

    Deduction goes from general premises to a particular conclusion; induction from particular observations to a general statement; abduction from an observation to its most likely explanation; analogy from a likeness between two cases.

    Read this in the notes: Unit 5, Types of Reasoning

    Q2. A proof that the sum of the first $n$ odd numbers is $n^2$ shows that it holds for $n=1$ and that, if it holds for $n$, it holds for $n+1$. The reasoning used is:
    1. inductive, and the conclusion is only probable
    2. abductive
    3. analogical
    4. deductive, and the conclusion is certain
    Show Answer

    Answer: (D) deductive, and the conclusion is certain.

    Mathematical induction, despite its name, is a deductive method of proof: given the two steps, the conclusion follows with certainty for every $n$.

    Read this in the notes: Unit 5, Types of Reasoning

    Q3. “In a survey of 1,000 voters, 54% favoured the proposal; therefore about 54% of all voters favour it.” This is an example of:
    1. inductive (statistical) reasoning
    2. deductive reasoning
    3. analogical reasoning
    4. circular reasoning
    Show Answer

    Answer: (A) inductive (statistical) reasoning.

    The argument goes from a sample to the population. Its conclusion is probable, not certain, and carries a margin of error: statistical reasoning is inductive.

    Read this in the notes: Unit 5, Types of Reasoning

    Q4. A doctor notes a patient's fever, rash and recent travel and concludes that the most likely explanation is a particular infection, to be confirmed by a test. The reasoning is:
    1. deductive
    2. inductive generalisation
    3. abductive
    4. analogical
    Show Answer

    Answer: (C) abductive.

    Abduction (C. S. Peirce's term) is inference to the best explanation: it yields a plausible hypothesis that has still to be tested.

    Read this in the notes: Unit 5, Types of Reasoning

    Q5. Consider the statements:(I) The conclusion of a valid deductive argument with true premises is certain.(II) The conclusion of an inductive argument becomes certain when enough cases have been observed.(III) The conclusion of an argument from analogy is probable.(IV) Statistical reasoning from a sample to a population is inductive.Which of the statements are correct?
    1. I and II only
    2. I, III and IV only
    3. II, III and IV only
    4. I, II, III and IV
    Show Answer

    Answer: (B) I, III and IV only.

    More cases make an inductive conclusion stronger but never certain; a single contrary case can overturn it. So (II) is false and the other three are correct.

    Read this in the notes: Unit 5, Types of Reasoning

    Q6. Given below are two statements, one labelled Assertion (A) and the other Reason (R).Assertion (A): The conclusion of an inductive argument is only probable.Reason (R): The conclusion of an inductive argument goes beyond what is contained in its premises.Choose the correct answer.
    1. Both (A) and (R) are true and (R) is the correct explanation of (A)
    2. Both (A) and (R) are true but (R) is not the correct explanation of (A)
    3. (A) is true but (R) is false
    4. (A) is false but (R) is true
    Show Answer

    Answer: (A) Both (A) and (R) are true and (R) is the correct explanation of (A).

    The premises report the cases observed; the conclusion speaks of all cases, including those not observed. Because it says more than the premises, they cannot guarantee it. Both statements are true and (R) is the reason.

    Read this in the notes: Unit 5, Types of Reasoning

    Q7. Find the next term of the series: 4, 9, 19, 39, 79, ?
    1. 119
    2. 149
    3. 158
    4. 159
    Show Answer

    Answer: (D) 159.

    Each term is twice the previous term plus one: $4\times2+1=9$, $9\times2+1=19$, and so on. $79\times2+1=159$. (The differences 5, 10, 20, 40 double each time, so the next difference is 80.)

    Q12. Which term is wrong in the series: 2, 6, 12, 20, 30, 44, 56?
    1. 20
    2. 30
    3. 44
    4. 56
    Show Answer

    Q22. In a code language ‘sky is blue’ is written as ‘4 7 2’, ‘blue and green’ as ‘2 9 5’ and ‘green is good’ as ‘5 7 1’. The code for ‘sky’ is:
    1. 7

    Answer: (B) 4.

    ‘blue’ is common to the first two sentences, and so is 2: blue is 2. ‘is’ is common to the first and third, and so is 7: is is 7. The remaining code in the first sentence, 4, stands for ‘sky’.

    Q27. A man walks 10 km towards the south, turns left and walks 6 km, then turns left again and walks 2 km. How far, and in which direction, is he from the starting point?
    1. 8 km, south
    2. 14 km, south-east
    3. 10 km, south-west
    4. 10 km, south-east
    Show Answer

    Answer: (D) 10 km, south-east.

    Facing south, a left turn faces east; facing east, a left turn faces north. Net movement: $10-2=8$ km south and 6 km east. Distance $=\sqrt{8^2+6^2}=10$ km, to the south-east of the start.

    Read this in the notes: Unit 5, Codes and Relationships

    Q32. A train 200 m long, running at 90 km/h, crosses a bridge 300 m long in:
    1. 20 seconds
    2. 12 seconds
    3. 8 seconds
    4. 25 seconds
    Show Answer

    Answer: (A) 20 seconds.

    $90$ km/h $=90\times\tfrac{5}{18}=25$ m/s. To cross the bridge the train covers its own length plus the bridge: $200+300=500$ m, in $\tfrac{500}{25}=20$ seconds.

    Read this in the notes: Unit 5, Mathematical Aptitude

    Q33. A cyclist goes from A to B at 30 km/h and returns over the same road at 60 km/h. The average speed for the whole journey is:
    1. 45 km/h
    2. 50 km/h
    3. 40 km/h
    4. 42 km/h
    Show Answer

    Answer: (C) 40 km/h.

    For two equal distances at speeds $x$ and $y$, the average speed is $\dfrac{2xy}{x+y}=\dfrac{2\times30\times60}{90}=40$ km/h. More time is spent at the lower speed, so the average is below 45.

    Read this in the notes: Unit 5, Mathematical Aptitude

    Q34. Two trains, 120 m and 180 m long, run towards each other on parallel tracks at 54 km/h and 36 km/h. The time they take to pass each other completely is:
    1. 60 seconds
    2. 12 seconds
    3. 30 seconds
    4. 10 seconds
    Show Answer

    Answer: (B) 12 seconds.

    Moving towards each other, the speeds add: $54+36=90$ km/h $=25$ m/s. They must cover the sum of their lengths, $300$ m, in $\tfrac{300}{25}=12$ seconds.

    Read this in the notes: Unit 5, Mathematical Aptitude

    Q36. The price of an article is raised by 25% and the new price is then reduced by 20%. The net change in the price is:
    1. no change
    2. a rise of 5%
    3. a fall of 5%
    4. a rise of 1%
    Show Answer

    Answer: (A) no change.

    Successive changes of $a\%$ and $b\%$ give $$\begin{aligned}&a+b+\dfrac{ab}{100} \\[4pt] &\quad = 25-20-\dfrac{500}{100} = 0\text{.}\end{aligned}$$ Directly: $1.25\times0.80=1.00$. The 20% is taken on the larger, raised price, which is why it cancels a rise of 25%.

    Q42. At what rate of simple interest per annum will a sum of money double itself in 8 years?

    Show Answer

    Answer: (B) 12.5%.

    To double, the interest must equal the principal: $\dfrac{P\times R\times8}{100}=P$, so $R=\dfrac{100}{8}=12.5\%$.

    Q43. The difference between the compound interest and the simple interest on ₹12,000 for 2 years at 5% per annum is:
    1. ₹60
    2. ₹15
    3. ₹120
    4. ₹30
    Show Answer

    Answer: (D) ₹30.

    For two years, $$\begin{aligned}CI-SI &= P\left(\dfrac{R}{100}\right)^2 \\[4pt] &= 12{,}000\times(0.05)^2 = 30\text{,}\end{aligned}$$ that is ₹30. Check: simple interest is ₹1,200; compound interest is $12{,}000(1.05^2-1)=1{,}230$, that is ₹1,230.

    Read this in the notes: Unit 5, Mathematical Aptitude

    Q44. The compound interest on ₹5,000 for 3 years at 10% per annum, compounded annually, is:

    Q45. The present value of ₹12,100 due two years hence, at 10% per annum compound interest, and the true discount are, respectively:

    Q48. The average age of the 5 members of a family is 30 years. A child aged 6 years joins the family. The new average age is:

    3. 25 years
    4. 28 years
    Show Answer

    Answer: (A) 26 years.

    Q49. The fourth proportional to 4, 6 and 10 is:
    1. 12
    2. 20
    3. 15
    4. 24
    Show Answer

    Answer: (C) 15.

    The fourth proportional to $a$, $b$, $c$ is the number $x$ for which $a:b=c:x$. If $4:6=10:x$, then the product of the extremes equals the product of the means: $4x=60$ and $x=15$.

    Q50. The pass mark in an examination is 35% of the maximum. A candidate who scores 120 marks fails by 20 marks. The maximum marks are:
    1. 350
    2. 400
    3. 500
    4. 450
    Show Answer

    Answer: (B) 400.

    The pass mark is $120+20=140$, which is 35% of the maximum. Maximum $=\dfrac{140}{0.35}=400$.
