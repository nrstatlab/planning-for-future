    Model Questions — Unit 1: Teaching Aptitude

    UGC NET Paper I (General Paper) · 50 questions · Click "Show Answer" for the worked solution

    About this set

    Teaching and its levels, learners and their differences, the factors that affect teaching, methods off-line and on-line, teaching support systems, and evaluation, including the Choice Based Credit System and computer-based testing.

    These are model questions written for practice at the standard of the paper; they are not questions from a past paper. Try each one before opening the answer. Every answer gives the working and a link to the section of the Unit 1 notes that teaches it.

    The questions

    Q1. Consider the statements about teaching:(I) It is a tripolar process joining the teacher, the learner and the content.(II) Its test is the learning that results, not the talk that was given.(III) In Jackson's scheme, the pre-active phase is the phase of evaluating and revising.(IV) It is both an art and a science.Which of the statements are correct?
    1. I and II only
    2. I, II and IV only
    3. II, III and IV only
    4. I, II, III and IV
    Show Answer

    Answer: (B) I, II and IV only.

    (I), (II) and (IV) are the standard ideas about teaching. (III) is false: the pre-active phase is planning (objectives, content, method); the interactive phase is the class itself; evaluating and revising belong to the post-active phase.

    Read this in the notes: Unit 1, Teaching: Concept and Objectives

    Q2. Match the item in List I with its entry in List II.
    List IList II
    (a) Highest level of the cognitive domain in the 2001 revision(i) Evaluation
    (b) Levels of the affective domain(ii) Create
    (c) Levels of the psychomotor domain (Dave)(iii) Receiving, responding, valuing
    (d) Highest level of the cognitive domain in Bloom (1956)(iv) Imitation, manipulation, precision
    1. (a)-(i), (b)-(iii), (c)-(iv), (d)-(ii)
    2. (a)-(ii), (b)-(iv), (c)-(iii), (d)-(i)
    3. (a)-(iii), (b)-(ii), (c)-(iv), (d)-(i)
    4. (a)-(ii), (b)-(iii), (c)-(iv), (d)-(i)
    Show Answer

    Answer: (D) (a)-(ii), (b)-(iii), (c)-(iv), (d)-(i).

    Bloom's original cognitive domain ended with evaluation; the revision by Anderson and Krathwohl renamed the levels as verbs and placed create (the old synthesis) above evaluate. The affective domain runs from receiving to characterisation; Dave's psychomotor levels begin with imitation, manipulation and precision.

    Q3. Arrange the levels of the revised taxonomy of the cognitive domain from the lowest to the highest: (1) Analyse, (2) Remember, (3) Create, (4) Understand, (5) Evaluate, (6) Apply.
    1. 2, 4, 6, 1, 5, 3
    2. 2, 4, 1, 6, 5, 3
    3. 2, 6, 4, 1, 3, 5
    4. 4, 2, 6, 1, 5, 3
    Show Answer

    Answer: (A) 2, 4, 6, 1, 5, 3.

    Remember, understand, apply, analyse, evaluate, create. Each level builds on the ones below it, and create is at the top, above evaluate.

    Read this in the notes: Unit 1, Teaching: Concept and Objectives

    Q4. A teacher writes the objective: “Students will compare two sampling methods and judge which of them suits a given survey, giving reasons.” In the revised taxonomy this objective is mainly at the level:
    1. Apply
    2. Understand
    3. Evaluate
    4. Remember
    Show Answer

    Answer: (C) Evaluate.

    Judging between alternatives against criteria, with reasons, is evaluate. Listing the methods would be remember, explaining them understand, and using one in a new survey apply. The verb of the objective tells the level.

    Read this in the notes: Unit 1, Teaching: Concept and Objectives

    Q5. The maxim of teaching “from the psychological to the logical” means that a teacher should:
    1. explain the psychology of learning before the logic of the subject
    2. begin from the learner's interest and experience and arrive at the logical order of the subject
    3. teach abstract rules first and examples afterwards
    4. arrange content strictly as the textbook does
    Show Answer

    Answer: (B) begin from the learner's interest and experience and arrive at the logical order of the subject.

    The psychological order is the order in which the learner can take the matter in, starting from what interests them; the logical order is the systematic order of the subject itself. Good teaching starts with the first and ends with the second.

    Read this in the notes: Unit 1, Teaching: Concept and Objectives

    Q6. Morrison's teaching model for the understanding level has five steps. Their correct sequence is:
    1. preparation, presentation, comparison, generalisation, application
    2. exploration, assimilation, presentation, recitation, organisation
    3. presentation, exploration, organisation, assimilation, recitation
    4. exploration, presentation, assimilation, organisation, recitation
    Show Answer

    Answer: (D) exploration, presentation, assimilation, organisation, recitation.

    Morrison: exploration, presentation, assimilation, organisation, recitation. The first option is the Herbartian sequence for the memory level, not Morrison's.

    Read this in the notes: Unit 1, Levels of Teaching: Memory, Understanding, Reflective

    Q7. Given below are two statements, one labelled Assertion (A) and the other Reason (R).Assertion (A): The reflective level is the highest level of teaching.Reason (R): It requires the learner to solve problems by critical and original thinking, which presupposes memory and understanding.Choose the correct answer.
    1. Both (A) and (R) are true and (R) is the correct explanation of (A)
    2. Both (A) and (R) are true but (R) is not the correct explanation of (A)
    3. (A) is true but (R) is false
    4. (A) is false but (R) is true
    Show Answer

    Answer: (A) Both (A) and (R) are true and (R) is the correct explanation of (A).

    Each level of teaching assumes the one below it. Reflective teaching (Hunt) puts the learner before a problem to be defined, hypothesised about and tested, which cannot be done without facts and understanding. Both statements are true and (R) is the reason.

    Read this in the notes: Unit 1, Levels of Teaching: Memory, Understanding, Reflective

    Q8. After a lesson on the five-year plans, the learners can explain why the emphasis of planning moved from agriculture to industry. The lesson has reached the:
    1. memory level
    2. reflective level
    3. understanding level
    4. autonomous development level
    Show Answer

    Answer: (C) understanding level.

    Explaining a relation or reason is grasp of meaning: the understanding level (Morrison), ‘memory plus insight’. Reciting the dates of the plans would be the memory level; arguing, with evidence, what the next plan should give priority to would be the reflective level.

    Read this in the notes: Unit 1, Levels of Teaching: Memory, Understanding, Reflective

    Q9. The Herbartian steps of a memory-level lesson, in the correct order, are:
    1. presentation, preparation, comparison, application, generalisation
    2. preparation, presentation, comparison, generalisation, application
    3. preparation, comparison, presentation, generalisation, application
    4. exploration, presentation, assimilation, organisation, recitation
    Show Answer

    Answer: (B) preparation, presentation, comparison, generalisation, application.

    Preparation, presentation, comparison (association), generalisation, application: set out by Herbart's followers from his teaching. The last option is Morrison's model for the understanding level.

    Read this in the notes: Unit 1, Levels of Teaching: Memory, Understanding, Reflective

    Q10. Consider the statements about the levels of teaching:(I) At the memory level the teacher is dominant and the learner passive.(II) The understanding level may be described as memory plus insight.(III) The reflective level is the most teacher-centred of the three.(IV) Each level assumes the level below it.Which of the statements are correct?
    1. I, II and IV only
    2. I and II only
    3. II, III and IV only
    4. I, II, III and IV
    Show Answer

    Answer: (A) I, II and IV only.

    (III) is false: the reflective level is the most learner-centred; the teacher only creates the problem situation. The memory level is the most teacher-centred. The other three statements are correct.

    Read this in the notes: Unit 1, Levels of Teaching: Memory, Understanding, Reflective

    Q11. From the answers to a short quiz a teacher notices that half the class confuses the mean with the median, and teaches the idea again with a new example. This shows that teaching is:
    1. a one-way process
    2. summative
    3. authoritarian
    4. diagnostic and remedial
    Show Answer

    Answer: (D) diagnostic and remedial.

    Finding what learners have not yet understood (diagnosis) and acting on it (remedy) is a characteristic of good teaching. It also shows teaching as two-way: the quiz is feedback from learners to the teacher.

    Read this in the notes: Unit 1, Characteristics and Basic Requirements of Teaching

    Q12. A teacher with full command of the subject gives a brilliant lecture that hardly any student can follow. The basic requirement of teaching that has not been met is:
    1. subject knowledge of the teacher
    2. availability of content
    3. readiness of the learners
    4. a way of evaluating
    Show Answer

    Answer: (C) readiness of the learners.

    Teaching needs learners who are ready: the teacher must start from what they already know. A teacher and content were present; the lecture failed because it did not begin where the learners were.

    Q15. Consider the following about adult learners, according to Knowles:(I) They are self-directed.(II) Their orientation to learning is problem-centred.(III) They need to know why they are learning something.(IV) They prefer subject-centred learning to be stored for use in the distant future.Which of these are among his assumptions?
    1. I and II only
    2. II and III only
    3. I, II and III only
    4. I, II, III and IV
    Show Answer

    Answer: (C) I, II and III only.

    Adults want learning they can use now, organised around the problems of their life roles, so (IV) is the opposite of Knowles's view. His other assumptions: experience is a resource, readiness comes from life roles, and motivation is mainly internal.

    Q16. A course on data handling for working nurses begins with a real problem from the wards that the nurses themselves bring, and not with definitions. The course design follows:
    1. pedagogy, because the teacher decides the content
    2. programmed instruction
    3. the memory level of teaching
    4. andragogy, because it is problem-centred and uses the learners' experience
    Show Answer

    Answer: (D) andragogy, because it is problem-centred and uses the learners' experience.

    Andragogy treats the adults' experience as a resource and organises learning around problems they need to solve. Pedagogy, for children, is subject-centred and teacher-directed.

    Read this in the notes: Unit 1, Adolescent and Adult Learners

    Q17. A first-year undergraduate class is found to work better in small groups with clear tasks and early, private feedback. The best explanation is that adolescent learners:
    1. are self-directed and need no guidance
    2. are peer-oriented and sensitive to public criticism
    3. are motivated only by internal satisfaction
    4. reason mainly from long experience
    Show Answer

    Answer: (B) are peer-oriented and sensitive to public criticism.

    For adolescents the peer group matters greatly, identity is still forming and public correction hurts. They need structure and guidance. Self-direction, internal motivation and reasoning from experience are the marks of adult learners.

    Q18. Given below are two statements, one labelled Assertion (A) and the other Reason (R).Assertion (A): Adult learners resist being treated like children.Reason (R): Adults have a self-concept of being responsible for their own decisions.Choose the correct answer.
    1. Both (A) and (R) are true and (R) is the correct explanation of (A)
    2. Both (A) and (R) are true but (R) is not the correct explanation of (A)

    4. (A) is false but (R) is true

    Answer: (A) Both (A) and (R) are true and (R) is the correct explanation of (A).

    One of Knowles's assumptions is that adults see themselves as responsible for their own lives; being directed at every step conflicts with that self-concept, and they resist it. Both statements are true and (R) explains (A).

    Q19. Which of the following is NOT one of the intelligences in Howard Gardner's theory of multiple intelligences?
    1. Bodily-kinaesthetic
    2. Intrapersonal
    3. Naturalistic
    4. Fluid
    Show Answer

    Answer: (D) Fluid.

    Gardner's list: linguistic, logical-mathematical, spatial, musical, bodily-kinaesthetic, interpersonal, intrapersonal and (added later) naturalistic. Fluid intelligence belongs to Cattell's distinction between fluid and crystallised intelligence, a different theory.

    Q20. Given below are two statements, one labelled Assertion (A) and the other Reason (R).Assertion (A): A teacher should use varied modes of presentation for the whole class and not label learners by a preferred learning style.Reason (R): Research has not shown that matching teaching to a learner's preferred style improves learning.Choose the correct answer.
    1. Both (A) and (R) are true and (R) is the correct explanation of (A)
    2. Both (A) and (R) are true but (R) is not the correct explanation of (A)
    3. (A) is true but (R) is false
    4. (A) is false but (R) is true
    Show Answer

    Answer: (A) Both (A) and (R) are true and (R) is the correct explanation of (A).

    Learning-style schemes such as Fleming's VARK (visual, aural, read/write, kinaesthetic) describe preferences, but the evidence does not show that teaching each learner in the preferred style helps. Varied modes help everyone. Both statements are true and (R) is the reason.

    Read this in the notes: Unit 1, Individual Differences

    Q21. In a class some students solve a problem at once while others need a worked example. The teacher gives the first group an extension task and the second a guided one. This practice is:
    1. streaming by ability into separate classes
    2. programmed instruction
    3. differentiated instruction
    4. summative evaluation
    Show Answer

    Answer: (C) differentiated instruction.

    Differentiated instruction adjusts tasks to learners' readiness inside one class: enrichment for the fast, support for the slow. It is how a teacher answers individual differences without separating the learners permanently.

    Q27. Students watch a recorded lecture and read a chapter at home; class time is then used for solving problems and discussion with the teacher's help. This arrangement is called:
    1. programmed instruction
    2. the Dalton plan
    3. team teaching
    4. the flipped classroom
    Show Answer

    Answer: (D) the flipped classroom.

    In a flipped classroom the first meeting with the content happens before class, and class time, the scarce resource, is given to the harder work of applying it. It is a learner-centred arrangement.

    Read this in the notes: Unit 1, Teacher-centred and Learner-centred Methods

    Q28. Which of the following is NOT a feature of linear programmed instruction as developed by B. F. Skinner?
    1. Learners compete in groups for the fastest answer
    2. Content is presented in small steps
    3. The learner gets immediate feedback on each response
    4. The learner proceeds at their own pace
    Show Answer

    Answer: (A) Learners compete in groups for the fastest answer.

    Programmed instruction is individual: small frames, an active response to each, immediate knowledge of results (reinforcement) and self-pacing. Group competition is no part of it.

    Q29. Given below are two statements, one labelled Assertion (A) and the other Reason (R).Assertion (A): In brainstorming, criticism of the ideas offered is postponed.Reason (R): The purpose of brainstorming is to arrive quickly at the single correct idea.Choose the correct answer.
    1. Both (A) and (R) are true and (R) is the correct explanation of (A)
    2. Both (A) and (R) are true but (R) is not the correct explanation of (A)
    3. (A) is true but (R) is false
    4. (A) is false but (R) is true
    Show Answer

    Answer: (C) (A) is true but (R) is false.

    (A) is true: Osborn's rule is many ideas first, judgement later, because early criticism stops people from offering ideas. (R) is false: the aim is a large number and variety of ideas, from which the useful ones are sifted afterwards.

    Read this in the notes: Unit 1, Teacher-centred and Learner-centred Methods

    Q30. Which of the following lists contains only learner-centred methods?
    1. Lecture, seminar, project
    2. Demonstration, discussion, case study
    3. Seminar, project, role play
    4. Recitation, brainstorming, lecture

    Answer: (C) Seminar, project, role play.

    Lecture, demonstration and recitation are teacher-centred: the teacher presents and learners receive. Seminar, project, role play, discussion, case study and brainstorming make the learners active while the teacher guides.

    Q31. A teacher-centred method such as the lecture is most suitable when:

    The lecture is economical: one teacher, many learners, much information, little time. Skills, attitudes, problem-solving and reflective aims are better served by learner-centred methods.

    Q36. Consider the following modes of online teaching:(I) A live webinar(II) A recorded lecture(III) A discussion forum(IV) A live online class with chatWhich of them are synchronous?

    Q37. The term MOOC (Massive Open Online Course) was coined in 2008 for an open online course run by:
    1. George Siemens and Stephen Downes
    2. Salman Khan
    3. Benjamin Bloom and David Krathwohl
    4. Sebastian Thrun and Peter Norvig
    Show Answer

    Answer: (A) George Siemens and Stephen Downes.

    The name was given to the open course taught by George Siemens and Stephen Downes in 2008. A MOOC is open to anyone, usually free to join, for very large numbers, and delivered online.

    Q38. Arrange the following from the most concrete to the most abstract in Edgar Dale's Cone of Experience: (1) Verbal symbols, (2) Demonstrations, (3) Direct purposeful experiences, (4) Television and films, (5) Visual symbols.
    1. 3, 4, 2, 5, 1
    2. 2, 3, 4, 1, 5
    3. 3, 2, 4, 5, 1
    4. 3, 2, 5, 4, 1
    Show Answer

    Answer: (C) 3, 2, 4, 5, 1.

    Direct purposeful experience is the base of the cone; then come contrived experiences, demonstrations, field trips, exhibits, television and film, recordings and pictures, visual symbols, and at the top verbal symbols, which depend on words alone.

    Q39. Which of the following lists contains only ICT-based teaching support?

    2. Interactive board, learning management system, virtual laboratory
    3. Overhead projector, slide projector, models
    4. Radio lessons, maps, specimens

    Answer: (B) Interactive board, learning management system, virtual laboratory.

    Interactive (smart) boards, learning management systems such as Moodle, MOOCs, simulations and virtual labs are ICT-based. The chalkboard, charts, maps, models and specimens are traditional aids; overhead and slide projectors, recordings and broadcast lessons are the ‘modern’ (pre-digital) group.

    Q40. Given below are two statements, one labelled Assertion (A) and the other Reason (R).Assertion (A): A field visit lies nearer the base of Dale's Cone of Experience than a lecture on the same topic.Reason (R): The cone arranges learning experiences from the concrete at its base to the abstract at its top.Choose the correct answer.

    Q43. An examination rule says: “The top 6% of the candidates who appear will qualify.” This is an example of:
    1. norm-referenced evaluation
    2. criterion-referenced evaluation
    3. formative evaluation
    4. diagnostic evaluation
    Show Answer

    Answer: (A) norm-referenced evaluation.

    Norm-referenced evaluation compares a candidate with the others (rank, percentile). “Everyone who scores 40% or more qualifies” would be criterion-referenced: comparison with a fixed standard. Robert Glaser made the distinction in 1963.

    Q44. A weekly ungraded quiz whose results decide what is taught again next week, and the end-of-semester examination, are examples respectively of:
    1. summative and formative evaluation
    2. formative and summative evaluation
    3. diagnostic and placement evaluation
    4. placement and summative evaluation

    Answer: (B) formative and summative evaluation.

    Evaluation used while teaching is going on, to improve it, is formative. Evaluation at the end, to judge and grade what was achieved, is summative.

    Q45. A test gives almost the same scores when repeated on the same students, but its questions call only for recall although the course aimed at application. The test is:
    1. valid but not reliable
    2. neither valid nor reliable
    3. both valid and reliable
    4. reliable but not valid
    Show Answer

    Answer: (D) reliable but not valid.

    Reliability is consistency of scores; validity is measuring what the test claims to measure. This test is consistent, hence reliable, but it does not measure application, the intended outcome, so it is not valid. A test can be reliable without being valid, but not valid without being reliable.

    Q46. Which statement about measurement, assessment and evaluation is correct?
    1. Measurement is the widest of the three terms
    2. Evaluation only assigns numbers to performance
    3. Evaluation is the widest: it adds a value judgement to the evidence collected
    4. Assessment and evaluation exclude qualitative evidence
    Show Answer

    Answer: (C) Evaluation is the widest: it adds a value judgement to the evidence collected.

    Measurement assigns numbers; assessment collects evidence, quantitative and qualitative; evaluation judges, against the objectives, how good the performance is. Evaluation is tied to the objectives and the learning experiences in a triangle.

    Q47. Under the ten-point grading scale of the Choice Based Credit System, a student earns grades A+ (9), B+ (7) and O (10) in three courses of 4, 3 and 3 credits. The SGPA is:
    1. 8.67
    2. 8.7
    3. 8.5
    4. 9.0
    Show Answer

    Answer: (B) 8.7.

    SGPA is the credit-weighted mean of the grade points: $$\begin{aligned}&\dfrac{4(9)+3(7)+3(10)}{4+3+3} \\[4pt] &\quad = \dfrac{36+21+30}{10} = 8.7\text{.}\end{aligned}$$ The simple average of 9, 7 and 10 is 8.67, which ignores the credits.

    Read this in the notes: Unit 1, Evaluation in the Choice Based Credit System

    Q48. A student has SGPA 7.5 in the first semester (20 credits) and SGPA 8.5 in the second semester (30 credits). The CGPA after two semesters is:
    1. 8.1
    2. 8.0
    3. 7.9
    4. 8.25
    Show Answer

    Answer: (A) 8.1.

    CGPA is weighted by credits: $$\begin{aligned}&\dfrac{20(7.5)+30(8.5)}{20+30} \\[4pt] &\quad = \dfrac{150+255}{50} = 8.1\text{.}\end{aligned}$$ It is not the simple average 8.0, because the second semester carries more credits.

    Q49. In the Choice Based Credit System, one credit stands for one hour of lecture or tutorial, or two hours of practical work, a week. A course with 3 lectures and 4 hours of practical work every week carries:
    1. 7 credits
    2. 3 credits
    3. 4 credits
    4. 5 credits
    Show Answer

    Answer: (D) 5 credits.

    Lectures: $3\times1=3$ credits. Practical: $4\div2=2$ credits. Total 5 credits.

    Q50. Consider the statements about innovations in evaluation:(I) In computer adaptive testing a harder question follows a right answer and an easier one a wrong answer.(II) In an adaptive test two candidates may see different questions and still be scored on the same scale.(III) Computer-based testing suits long essay-type answers better than objective questions.(IV) The Academic Bank of Credits stores the credits a student earns so that they can be carried between institutions.Which of the statements are correct?
    1. I and II only
    2. I, III and IV only
    3. I, II and IV only
    4. I, II, III and IV
    Show Answer

    Answer: (C) I, II and IV only.

    (I) and (II) describe adaptive testing: each next question is chosen from the answers so far, and since the difficulty of every question is known in advance, different sets give comparable scores. (IV) is the purpose of the Academic Bank of Credits. (III) is false: computer-based testing suits objective questions better than long answers.
