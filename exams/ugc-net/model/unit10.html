    Model Questions — Unit X: Indian Statistical System and Research Methodology

    UGC NET Statistics (Code 107) · 50 questions · Click "Show Answer" for the worked solution

    About this set

    The Ministry of Statistics and Programme Implementation, the National Statistical Commission and Office, the census and the large sample surveys, the contributions of Indian statisticians, and computing with R and typesetting with LaTeX.

    These are model questions written for practice at the standard of the paper; they are not questions from a past paper. Try each one before opening the answer. Every answer gives the working and a link to the section of the Unit X notes that teaches it.

    The questions

    Q1. The Ministry of Statistics and Programme Implementation (MoSPI) came into being as an independent ministry, by the merger of the Department of Statistics and the Department of Programme Implementation, in:
    1. 1947
    2. 1999
    3. 2005
    4. 2019
    Show Answer

    Answer: (B) 1999.

    MoSPI was formed in 1999 by merging the Department of Statistics and the Department of Programme Implementation. 2005 is the year the National Statistical Commission was constituted, and 2019 the year the National Statistical Office was formed by merging the CSO and the NSSO.

    Q3. The Annual Survey of Industries (ASI), the principal source of industrial statistics in India, is conducted under the:

    3. Factories Act, 1948
    4. Industrial Disputes Act, 1947

    The ASI is conducted under the Collection of Statistics Act, 2008. Its coverage is defined by the Factories Act, 1948 (factories registered under sections 2(m)(i) and 2(m)(ii)), but that Act is not the authority under which the survey is taken.     4. Social Statistics Division

    Q11. The sampling design generally adopted in the household surveys of the National Sample Survey is:
    1. simple random sampling of households from a national list
    2. systematic sampling of districts only
    3. quota sampling within states
    4. stratified multi-stage sampling, with villages or urban blocks as first-stage units and households as second-stage units
    Show Answer

    Answer: (D) stratified multi-stage sampling, with villages or urban blocks as first-stage units and households as second-stage units.

    The surveys use a stratified multi-stage design: census villages in rural areas and Urban Frame Survey blocks in urban areas are the first-stage units, and households the ultimate units. No national list of households exists, which is what makes a multi-stage design necessary.

    Read this in the notes: Unit X, National Statistical Office (NSO)

    Q12. In a surveyed population of 1000 persons, 400 are employed and 50 are unemployed (available for and seeking work). The labour force participation rate and the unemployment rate are, respectively:
    1. 45% and 11.1%
    2. 45% and 5%
    3. 40% and 11.1%
    4. 40% and 12.5%
    Show Answer

    Answer: (A) 45% and 11.1%.

    The labour force is the employed plus the unemployed, 450. $\text{LFPR}=\dfrac{450}{1000}=45\%$. The unemployment rate is the unemployed as a proportion of the labour force, not of the population: $\dfrac{50}{450}=11.1\%$. The worker population ratio is $40\%$.

    Read this in the notes: Unit X, National Statistical Office (NSO)

    Q19. Euler's conjecture that no pair of orthogonal Latin squares exists for orders of the form $4k+2$ was disproved in 1959 by:
    1. Mahalanobis, Bose and Roy
    2. Rao, Mitra and Mathai
    3. Fisher, Yates and Bose
    4. Bose, Shrikhande and Parker
    Show Answer

    Answer: (D) Bose, Shrikhande and Parker.

    R. C. Bose, S. S. Shrikhande and E. T. Parker showed that pairs of orthogonal Latin squares exist for every order except 2 and 6 (their first counter-example was of order 22). They came to be called ‘Euler's spoilers’.

    Read this in the notes: Unit X, Contributions of Indian Statisticians

    Q20. Which of the following is NOT a contribution of C. R. Rao?

    Answer: (A) The largest root test of multivariate analysis.

    The largest root test is due to S. N. Roy. C. R. Rao's contributions include the Cramér–Rao inequality, the Rao–Blackwell theorem, the score test and orthogonal arrays; he received the International Prize in Statistics in 2023.

    Q21. In R, the values of -7 %/% 2 and -7 %% 2 are, respectively:
    1. -3 and -1
    2. -3 and 1
    3. -4 and 1
    4. -4 and -1
    Show Answer

    Answer: (C) -4 and 1.

    %/% is integer division rounding towards minus infinity (the floor), so -7 %/% 2 is $\lfloor-3.5\rfloor=-4$. %% gives the remainder with the sign of the divisor: $-7-2(-4)=1$. The two always satisfy $x=y\times(x\ \%/\%\ y)+(x\ \%\%\ y)$.

    Read this in the notes: Unit X, R as a Calculator

    Q22. In R, 0/0 gives NaN. The value of is.na(0/0) is:
    1. FALSE
    2. TRUE
    3. NA
    4. an error
    Show Answer

    Answer: (B) TRUE.

    NaN (not a number) is treated as a kind of missing value: is.na(NaN) is TRUE, while is.nan(NA) is FALSE. Note that 5/0 is Inf, which is neither.

    Q26. After A <- matrix(c(2, 1, 1, 3), 2), the values of (A %*% A)[1, 1] and (A * A)[1, 1] are, respectively:
    1. 4 and 4
    2. 4 and 5
    3. 5 and 5
    4. 5 and 4
    Show Answer

    Answer: (D) 5 and 4.

    A is $\begin{pmatrix}2&1\\1&3\end{pmatrix}$. %*% is the matrix product, whose $(1,1)$ element is $2\times2+1\times1=5$; * multiplies element by element, giving $2\times2=4$.

    Read this in the notes: Unit X, Functions and Matrix Operations

    Q28. In R, mean(c(1, 2, NA, 5)) and mean(c(1, 2, NA, 5), na.rm = TRUE) give, respectively:
    1. 2 and 2.667
    2. 2.667 and 2.667
    3. NA and 2.667
    4. NA and 2
    Show Answer

    Answer: (C) NA and 2.667.

    Any arithmetic involving a missing value is missing, so the first is NA. With na.rm = TRUE the missing value is removed first and the mean of 1, 2, 5 is $\tfrac83=2.667$.

    Q29. In R, the value of var(c(2, 4, 6)) is:
    1. 2.667
    2. 4
    3. 2
    4. 8
    Show Answer

    Answer: (B) 4.

    var uses the divisor $n-1$: $\dfrac{(2-4)^2+0+(6-4)^2}{2}=4$, and sd gives 2. The divisor $n$ would give 2.667.

    Q30. After x <- c(1, 5, 8, 3), the value of sum(x > 2) is:
    1. 16
    2. 17
    3. 4
    4. 3
    Show Answer

    Answer: (D) 3.

    x > 2 is the logical vector FALSE TRUE TRUE TRUE, and in arithmetic TRUE counts as 1 and FALSE as 0. So the sum is 3, the number of elements greater than 2. sum(x[x > 2]) would give 16.

    Read this in the notes: Unit X, Built-in Functions, Missing Data & Logical Operators

    Q31. Consider the statements about logical values in R:(I) NA & FALSE is FALSE.(II) NA | TRUE is TRUE.(III) NA == NA is TRUE.(IV) is.na(NA) is TRUE.Which of the statements are correct?
    1. I, II and IV only
    2. I and II only
    3. III and IV only
    4. I, II, III and IV
    Show Answer

    Answer: (A) I, II and IV only.

    A logical operation with NA gives a definite answer when the answer does not depend on the missing value: anything AND FALSE is FALSE, anything OR TRUE is TRUE. But a comparison with NA is unknown, so NA == NA is NA; missing values are tested with is.na.

    Q32. What is the value of s after this R code is run?
    s <- 0
for (i in 1:5) {
  if (i %% 2 == 0) next
  s <- s + i
}
    1. 15
    2. 6
    3. 9
    4. 5
    Show Answer

    Answer: (C) 9.

    next skips the rest of the loop body for even $i$, so only the odd values are added: $1+3+5=9$. With break in place of next the loop would stop at $i=2$ and s would be 1.

    Read this in the notes: Unit X, Conditional Execution & Loops

    Q33. What is the value of n after this R code is run?

    Q39. After f <- factor(c("b", "a", "b", "c")), the result of as.integer(f) is:
    1. 2 1 2 3
    2. 1 2 1 3
    3. 1 2 3 4
    4. an error
    Show Answer

    Answer: (A) 2 1 2 3.

    The levels of a factor are the distinct values in sorted order, "a" "b" "c", coded 1, 2, 3. The data b, a, b, c therefore have codes 2, 1, 2, 3. nlevels(f) is 3 and table(f) gives the counts 1, 2, 1.

    Q40. After L <- list(a = 1:3, b = "x"), which statement is correct?
    1. L["a"] and L[["a"]] are both integer vectors
    2. L["a"] is an integer vector and L[["a"]] is a list
    3. L["a"] is a list of length 1 and L[["a"]] is the integer vector 1 2 3
    4. length(L) is 4
    Show Answer

    Answer: (C) L["a"] is a list of length 1 and L[["a"]] is the integer vector 1 2 3.

    Single brackets return a sub-list; double brackets (or L$a) return the element itself. So L[["a"]][2] is 2. The list has two components, so length(L) is 2.

    Read this in the notes: Unit X, Lists, Factors, Display & Formatting

    Q41. In R, the values of round(2.5) and round(3.5) are, respectively:
    1. 3 and 4
    2. 2 and 4
    3. 2 and 3
    4. 3 and 3
    Show Answer

    Answer: (B) 2 and 4.

    R follows the IEC 60559 standard and rounds a value exactly half-way to the even digit: 2.5 goes to 2 and 3.5 goes to 4. This avoids the upward bias of always rounding halves up.

    Read this in the notes: Unit X, Lists, Factors, Display & Formatting

    Q48. Which LaTeX source produces the formula $\bar x=\dfrac1n\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_i$?
    1. $\mean{x} = 1/n \sum{i=1}{n} x_i$
    2. $\bar{x} = \frac{1}{n} \sum^{i=1}_{n} x_i$
    3. $\bar{x} = \frac{n}{1} \sum_{i=1}^{n} x_i$
    4. $\bar{x} = \frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^{n} x_i$
    Show Answer

    Answer: (D) $\bar{x} = \frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^{n} x_i$.

    \bar{x} puts a bar over x; \frac{1}{n} takes the numerator first and the denominator second; the lower limit of a sum follows _ and the upper limit follows ^. Option B has the limits interchanged and option C the fraction inverted; \mean is not a LaTeX command.
