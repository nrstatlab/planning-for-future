    Model Questions — Unit IX: Stochastic Processes

    UGC NET Statistics (Code 107) · 50 questions · Click "Show Answer" for the worked solution

    About this set

    Markov chains, classification of states, Chapman–Kolmogorov equations, limiting and stationary distributions, gambler's ruin, random walks, the Poisson process and its waiting times, birth and death processes and the M/M/1 queue.

    These are model questions written for practice at the standard of the paper; they are not questions from a past paper. Try each one before opening the answer. Every answer gives the working and a link to the section of the Unit IX notes that teaches it.

    The questions

    Q1. A Markov chain on the states $\{0,1\}$ has transition matrix $P=\begin{pmatrix}0.7&0.3\\0.4&0.6\end{pmatrix}$ and initial distribution $P(X_0=0)=P(X_0=1)=0.5$. Then $P(X_2=0)$ equals:
    1. 0.550
    2. 0.565
    3. 0.610
    4. 0.571
    Show Answer

    Answer: (B) 0.565.

    The distribution after one step is $(0.5,0.5)P=(0.55,0.45)$, and after two steps $$\begin{aligned}&P(X_2=0) \\[4pt] &\quad = 0.55(0.7)+0.45(0.4) \\[4pt] &\quad = 0.565\text{.}\end{aligned}$$ ($0.610$ is $p_{00}^{(2)}$, the value when the chain starts in state 0; $0.571$ is the limiting value $\tfrac47$.)

    Read this in the notes: Unit IX, Markov Chains: Definitions

    Q2. For the chain with $P=\begin{pmatrix}0.7&0.3\\0.4&0.6\end{pmatrix}$ and $P(X_0=0)=P(X_0=1)=0.5$, the probability $P(X_0=0,\,X_1=1,\,X_2=1)$ is:
    1. 0.18
    2. 0.15
    3. 0.045
    4. 0.09
    Show Answer

    Answer: (D) 0.09.

    By the Markov property the joint probability is the initial probability times the successive transition probabilities: $$\begin{aligned}0.5\times p_{01}\times p_{11} &= 0.5\times0.3\times0.6 \\[4pt] &= 0.09\text{.}\end{aligned}$$

    Read this in the notes: Unit IX, Markov Chains: Definitions

    Q3. Let $P$ be the transition matrix of a finite Markov chain. Consider the statements:(I) Every row of $P$ sums to 1.(II) $P^n$ is a stochastic matrix for every $n\ge1$.(III) 1 is a characteristic root of $P$.(IV) Every column of $P$ sums to 1.Which of the statements are correct?
    1. I, II and III only
    2. I and II only
    3. I and III only
    4. I, II, III and IV
    Show Answer

    Answer: (A) I, II and III only.

    Rows are probability distributions: (I). A product of stochastic matrices is stochastic: (II). $P\mathbf 1=\mathbf 1$, so 1 is a characteristic root with the vector of ones: (III). Columns need not sum to 1; when they do the matrix is doubly stochastic, a special case: (IV) is false.

    Read this in the notes: Unit IX, Markov Chains: Definitions

    Q4. Let $X_1,X_2,\dots$ be independent Bernoulli variables with $P(X_i=1)=p$, $0\lt p\lt1$. Which of the following sequences is NOT in general a Markov chain?
    1. $S_n=X_1+\cdots+X_n$
    2. $M_n=\max(X_1,\dots,X_n)$
    3. $Y_n=X_n+X_{n-1}$
    4. $X_n$ itself
    Show Answer

    Answer: (C) $Y_n=X_n+X_{n-1}$.

    $S_n$ and $M_n$ are functions of their previous value and a fresh independent variable, so they are Markov; an independent sequence is trivially Markov. For $Y_n$: given $Y_n=1$, the value of $Y_{n-1}$ helps to tell whether $X_n=1$ (if $Y_{n-1}=2$ then $X_{n-1}=1$, so $X_n=0$), and that changes the distribution of $Y_{n+1}$. The past matters beyond the present.

    Read this in the notes: Unit IX, Markov Chains: Definitions

    Q5. For the chain with $P=\begin{pmatrix}0.7&0.3\\0.4&0.6\end{pmatrix}$, the two-step transition probability $p_{01}^{(2)}$ is:
    1. 0.30
    2. 0.39
    3. 0.09
    4. 0.61
    Show Answer

    Answer: (B) 0.39.

    $$\begin{aligned}p_{01}^{(2)} &= p_{00}p_{01}+p_{01}p_{11} \\[4pt] &= 0.7(0.3)+0.3(0.6) = 0.39\text{.}\end{aligned}$$ The first row of $P^2$ is $(0.61,0.39)$.

    Read this in the notes: Unit IX, Markov Chains: Definitions

    Q6. A Markov chain on $\{1,2,3,4\}$ has transition matrix $\begin{pmatrix}\tfrac12&\tfrac12&0&0\\\tfrac12&\tfrac12&0&0\\0&\tfrac13&\tfrac13&\tfrac13\\0&0&0&1\end{pmatrix}$. The transient states are:
    1. 1 and 2
    2. 3 and 4
    3. none
    4. 3 only
    Show Answer

    Answer: (D) 3 only.

    $\{1,2\}$ is a closed class and $\{4\}$ is absorbing; both are recurrent. From state 3 the chain leaves with probability $\tfrac23$ at each step and can never come back, so 3 is transient: the probability of ever returning to 3 is $\tfrac13\lt1$.

    Read this in the notes: Unit IX, Classification of States

    Q7. A Markov chain on $\{0,1,2\}$ has $p_{01}=1$, $p_{10}=p_{12}=\tfrac12$ and $p_{21}=1$. The period of each state is:
    1. 2
    2. 1
    3. 3
    4. 4
    Show Answer

    Answer: (A) 2.

    The chain alternates between the set $\{0,2\}$ and the state 1, so a return to any state is possible only after an even number of steps, and $p_{00}^{(2)}=\tfrac12\gt0$: the period is 2. The chain is irreducible, and period is a class property.

    Read this in the notes: Unit IX, Classification of States

    Q8. Consider the statements about Markov chains:(I) Recurrence is a class property.(II) Period is a class property.(III) Every state of a finite irreducible chain is positive recurrent.(IV) All the states of a finite chain can be transient.Which of the statements are correct?
    1. I and II only
    2. I, II and IV only
    3. I, II and III only
    4. I, II, III and IV
    Show Answer

    Answer: (C) I, II and III only.

    (I) and (II): communicating states are all recurrent or all transient, and have the same period. (III) A finite chain cannot have null recurrent states. (IV) is false: a finite chain must spend infinitely much time somewhere, so at least one state is recurrent.

    Read this in the notes: Unit IX, Classification of States

    Q9. For a state $i$ of a Markov chain, $p_{ii}^{(n)}=\dfrac{1}{n^2}$ for all $n\ge2$ and $p_{ii}^{(1)}=0$. Then state $i$ is:
    1. positive recurrent
    2. transient
    3. null recurrent
    4. absorbing
    Show Answer

    Answer: (B) transient.

    A state is recurrent if and only if $\sum_np_{ii}^{(n)}=\infty$. Here $\sum_{n\ge2}\tfrac1{n^2}\lt\infty$, so the expected number of returns is finite and the state is transient. (With $p_{ii}^{(n)}\sim\tfrac1n$ the state would be recurrent.)

    Read this in the notes: Unit IX, Classification of States

    Q10. The symmetric simple random walk on the integer lattice in one, two and three dimensions is, respectively:
    1. null recurrent, null recurrent, transient
    2. positive recurrent, null recurrent, transient
    3. null recurrent, transient, transient
    4. null recurrent, null recurrent, null recurrent
    Show Answer

    Answer: (A) null recurrent, null recurrent, transient.

    Pólya's theorem: $p_{00}^{(2n)}$ is of order $n^{-d/2}$ in $d$ dimensions, and $\sum n^{-d/2}$ diverges for $d=1,2$ and converges for $d\ge3$. In one and two dimensions the walk returns with probability 1, but the mean return time is infinite (null recurrent); in three dimensions it returns with probability about 0.34 only.

    Read this in the notes: Unit IX, Classification of States

    Q11. An irreducible Markov chain on three states has stationary distribution $\left(\tfrac14,\tfrac12,\tfrac14\right)$. The mean recurrence time of the second state is:
    1. 4
    2. 0.5
    3. 3
    4. 2
    Show Answer

    Answer: (D) 2.

    For a positive recurrent state $\mu_j=\dfrac{1}{\pi_j}=\dfrac{1}{1/2}=2$: a state visited half the time is revisited on average every 2 steps. The other two states have mean recurrence time 4.

    Read this in the notes: Unit IX, Classification of States

    Q12. A Markov chain on $\{0,1,2,3\}$ has 0 and 3 absorbing, $p_{10}=\tfrac13$, $p_{12}=\tfrac23$, $p_{21}=\tfrac13$ and $p_{23}=\tfrac23$. Starting from state 1, the probability of absorption in state 3 is:
    1. $\dfrac49$
    2. $\dfrac23$
    3. $\dfrac47$
    4. $\dfrac67$
    Show Answer

    Answer: (C) $\dfrac47$.

    Let $h_i$ be the probability of absorption at 3 from $i$. First-step analysis: $h_1=\tfrac23h_2$ and $h_2=\tfrac23+\tfrac13h_1$. So $h_1=\tfrac49+\tfrac29h_1$, giving $h_1=\tfrac47$ (and $h_2=\tfrac67$). $\tfrac49$ counts only the direct path $1\to2\to3$.

    Read this in the notes: Unit IX, Classification of States

    Q13. For the Markov chain with $P=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\\tfrac12&\tfrac12\end{pmatrix}$ on $\{0,1\}$, the three-step probability $p_{00}^{(3)}$ is:
    1. $\dfrac12$
    2. $\dfrac14$
    3. $\dfrac38$
    4. $\dfrac18$
    Show Answer

    Answer: (B) $\dfrac14$.

    $$\begin{aligned}&P^2 \\[4pt] &\quad = \begin{pmatrix}\tfrac12&\tfrac12\\\tfrac14&\tfrac34\end{pmatrix}\end{aligned}$$ and, by the Chapman–Kolmogorov equations, $$\begin{aligned}&p_{00}^{(3)} \\[4pt] &\quad = p_{00}^{(2)}p_{00}+p_{01}^{(2)}p_{10} \\[4pt] &\quad = \tfrac12(0)+\tfrac12\cdot\tfrac12 = \tfrac14\text{.}\end{aligned}$$ The only path is $0\to1\to1\to0$.

    Read this in the notes: Unit IX, Chapman–Kolmogorov Equations

    Q14. Given below are two statements, one labelled Assertion (A) and the other Reason (R).Assertion (A): For any states $i,j,k$ and any $m,n\ge0$, $p_{ij}^{(m+n)}\ge p_{ik}^{(m)}\,p_{kj}^{(n)}$.Reason (R): $p_{ij}^{(m+n)}=\sum_{k}p_{ik}^{(m)}p_{kj}^{(n)}$, and every term of the sum is non-negative.Choose the correct answer.
    1. Both (A) and (R) are true and (R) is the correct explanation of (A)
    2. Both (A) and (R) are true but (R) is not the correct explanation of (A)
    3. (A) is true but (R) is false
    4. (A) is false but (R) is true
    Show Answer

    Answer: (A) Both (A) and (R) are true and (R) is the correct explanation of (A).

    (R) is the Chapman–Kolmogorov equation. A sum of non-negative terms is at least as large as any one of them, which gives (A). This inequality is the tool for proving that communication is transitive and that recurrence and period are class properties. Both are true and (R) explains (A).

    Read this in the notes: Unit IX, Chapman–Kolmogorov Equations

    Q15. For the chain with $P=\begin{pmatrix}0.7&0.3\\0.4&0.6\end{pmatrix}$, $\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}P^n$ is the matrix each of whose rows is:
    1. $\left(\tfrac12,\tfrac12\right)$
    2. $\left(\tfrac37,\tfrac47\right)$
    3. $(0.7,0.3)$
    4. $\left(\tfrac47,\tfrac37\right)$
    Show Answer

    Answer: (D) $\left(\tfrac47,\tfrac37\right)$.

    The chain is irreducible and aperiodic, so $P^n$ converges to a matrix with identical rows $\pi$, where $\pi=\pi P$: $0.3\pi_0=0.4\pi_1$ with $\pi_0+\pi_1=1$ gives $\pi=\left(\tfrac47,\tfrac37\right)$. For a two-state chain $\pi_0=\dfrac{p_{10}}{p_{01}+p_{10}}$.

    Read this in the notes: Unit IX, Limiting Behaviour of $n$-Step Probabilities

    Q16. For the Markov chain with $P=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}$:
    1. $\lim P^n$ exists and has rows $\left(\tfrac12,\tfrac12\right)$
    2. there is no stationary distribution
    3. $\lim P^n$ does not exist, but $\tfrac1n\sum_{k=1}^{n}P^k$ converges to the matrix with rows $\left(\tfrac12,\tfrac12\right)$
    4. both states are transient
    Show Answer

    Answer: (C) $\lim P^n$ does not exist, but $\tfrac1n\sum_{k=1}^{n}P^k$ converges to the matrix with rows $\left(\tfrac12,\tfrac12\right)$.

    $P^n$ is $P$ for odd $n$ and $I$ for even $n$, so it oscillates: the chain has period 2. The stationary distribution $\left(\tfrac12,\tfrac12\right)$ exists and is unique, and it gives the long-run proportion of time in each state: the averages of the powers converge although the powers themselves do not.

    Read this in the notes: Unit IX, Limiting Behaviour of $n$-Step Probabilities

    Q17. For the two-state chain with $P=\begin{pmatrix}0.7&0.3\\0.4&0.6\end{pmatrix}$, $p_{00}^{(n)}-\tfrac47$ tends to zero like $c\,r^n$, where $r$ equals:
    1. 0.3
    2. 0.7
    3. 0.4
    4. 0.6
    Show Answer

    Answer: (A) 0.3.

    The characteristic roots of $P$ are 1 and $1-p_{01}-p_{10}=1-0.3-0.4=0.3$ (trace $1.3$ minus 1). So $p_{00}^{(n)}=\tfrac47+\tfrac37(0.3)^n$; check $n=2$: $0.571+0.039=0.61$. The second largest root governs the speed of convergence.

    Read this in the notes: Unit IX, Limiting Behaviour of $n$-Step Probabilities

    Q18. Consider the statements about an irreducible Markov chain:(I) If it is aperiodic and positive recurrent, $\lim_{n\to\infty}p_{ij}^{(n)}=\pi_j$ exists.(II) That limit does not depend on the initial state $i$.(III) If it is periodic, $\lim_{n\to\infty}p_{ij}^{(n)}$ still always exists.(IV) $\pi_j=1/\mu_j$, where $\mu_j$ is the mean recurrence time of $j$.Which of the statements are correct?
    1. I and II only
    2. I, II and IV only
    3. I, III and IV only
    4. I, II, III and IV
    Show Answer

    Answer: (B) I, II and IV only.

    (I), (II) and (IV) are the ergodic theorem for Markov chains. (III) is false: for a chain of period $d$, $p_{jj}^{(n)}=0$ unless $d$ divides $n$, while $p_{jj}^{(nd)}\to d/\mu_j\gt0$, so the sequence has no limit.

    Read this in the notes: Unit IX, Limiting Behaviour of $n$-Step Probabilities

    Q19. The stationary distribution of the chain with $P=\begin{pmatrix}0.5&0.5&0\\0.25&0.5&0.25\\0&0.5&0.5\end{pmatrix}$ is:
    1. $\left(\tfrac13,\tfrac13,\tfrac13\right)$
    2. $\left(\tfrac25,\tfrac15,\tfrac25\right)$
    3. $\left(\tfrac14,\tfrac12,\tfrac14\right)$
    4. $\left(\tfrac16,\tfrac23,\tfrac16\right)$
    Show Answer

    Answer: (C) $\left(\tfrac14,\tfrac12,\tfrac14\right)$.

    Solve $\pi=\pi P$: $\pi_0=0.5\pi_0+0.25\pi_1$ gives $\pi_1=2\pi_0$, and by symmetry $\pi_2=\pi_0$. With $\sum\pi_i=1$: $\left(\tfrac14,\tfrac12,\tfrac14\right)$. The matrix is not doubly stochastic (the middle column sums to 1.5), so the uniform distribution is not stationary.

    Read this in the notes: Unit IX, Stationary Distribution

    Q20. An irreducible Markov chain on 5 states has a doubly stochastic transition matrix. Its stationary distribution gives each state the probability:
    1. $\dfrac15$
    2. $\dfrac1{25}$
    3. $\dfrac12$
    4. it depends on the matrix
    Show Answer

    Answer: (A) $\dfrac15$.

    If the columns also sum to 1, then $$\begin{aligned}&\left(\tfrac1N,\dots,\tfrac1N\right)P \\[4pt] &\quad = \left(\tfrac1N,\dots,\tfrac1N\right)\text{,}\end{aligned}$$ so the uniform distribution is stationary, and for an irreducible finite chain the stationary distribution is unique.

    Read this in the notes: Unit IX, Stationary Distribution

    Q21. If it rains today it rains tomorrow with probability 0.6; if it is dry today it rains tomorrow with probability 0.2. In the long run, the proportion of rainy days is:
    1. $\dfrac25$
    2. $\dfrac12$
    3. $\dfrac23$
    4. $\dfrac13$
    Show Answer

    Answer: (D) $\dfrac13$.

    With states rain, dry: $\pi_R=0.6\pi_R+0.2\pi_D$, so $0.4\pi_R=0.2\pi_D$, $\pi_D=2\pi_R$ and $\pi_R=\tfrac13$. Equivalently $$\begin{aligned}\pi_R &= \dfrac{p_{DR}}{p_{DR}+p_{RD}} \\[4pt] &= \dfrac{0.2}{0.2+0.4}\text{.}\end{aligned}$$

    Read this in the notes: Unit IX, Stationary Distribution

    Q22. Two balls are distributed between two urns. At each step one of the two balls is chosen at random and moved to the other urn (Ehrenfest model). The stationary distribution of the number of balls in the first urn, on $\{0,1,2\}$, is:
    1. $\left(\tfrac13,\tfrac13,\tfrac13\right)$
    2. $\left(\tfrac14,\tfrac12,\tfrac14\right)$
    3. $\left(0,1,0\right)$
    4. $\left(\tfrac12,0,\tfrac12\right)$
    Show Answer

    Answer: (B) $\left(\tfrac14,\tfrac12,\tfrac14\right)$.

    $p_{01}=1$, $p_{10}=p_{12}=\tfrac12$, $p_{21}=1$. Detailed balance $\pi_0p_{01}=\pi_1p_{10}$ gives $\pi_1=2\pi_0$, and similarly $\pi_1=2\pi_2$: the $\text{Bin}(2,\tfrac12)$ distribution $\left(\tfrac14,\tfrac12,\tfrac14\right)$. The chain has period 2, so this is the long-run proportion of time, not a limit of $p_{ij}^{(n)}$.

    Read this in the notes: Unit IX, Stationary Distribution

    Q23. Consider the statements:(I) An irreducible finite Markov chain has a unique stationary distribution.(II) Every irreducible Markov chain has a stationary distribution.(III) If $X_0$ has the stationary distribution $\pi$, then $X_n$ has distribution $\pi$ for every $n$.(IV) A chain with two closed communicating classes has infinitely many stationary distributions.Which of the statements are correct?
    1. I and III only
    2. I, II and III only
    3. I, III and IV only
    4. I, II, III and IV
    Show Answer

    Answer: (C) I, III and IV only.

    (I) and (III) are basic. (II) is false: an irreducible chain has a stationary distribution only if it is positive recurrent; the symmetric random walk on the integers has none. (IV) Every mixture $\alpha\pi^{(1)}+(1-\alpha)\pi^{(2)}$ of the stationary distributions of the two classes is stationary.

    Read this in the notes: Unit IX, Stationary Distribution

    Q24. A gambler with 3 rupees plays a fair game, winning or losing 1 rupee each time, until he has 10 rupees or is ruined. The probability of his ruin is:
    1. 0.7
    2. 0.3
    3. 0.5
    4. 0.21
    Show Answer

    Answer: (A) 0.7.

    For a fair game the probability of reaching $N$ before 0 from $i$ is $\dfrac iN=0.3$, so the probability of ruin is $1-\dfrac iN=0.7$.

    Read this in the notes: Unit IX, Gambler's Ruin Problem

    Q25. A gambler wins 1 rupee with probability $\tfrac13$ and loses 1 rupee with probability $\tfrac23$ at each play. Starting with 1 rupee, the probability that he reaches 3 rupees before being ruined is:
    1. $\dfrac13$
    2. $\dfrac19$
    3. $\dfrac37$
    4. $\dfrac17$
    Show Answer

    Answer: (D) $\dfrac17$.

    With $r=\dfrac qp=2$: $$\begin{aligned}P(\text{reach }N) &= \dfrac{1-r^i}{1-r^N} \\[4pt] &= \dfrac{1-2}{1-8} = \dfrac17\text{.}\end{aligned}$$ Check by first-step analysis: $h_1=\tfrac13h_2$, $h_2=\tfrac13+\tfrac23h_1$ give $h_1=\tfrac17$.

    Read this in the notes: Unit IX, Gambler's Ruin Problem

    Q26. In a fair game with unit stakes a gambler starts with 3 rupees and stops on reaching 10 rupees or 0. The expected duration of the game is:
    1. 30
    2. 21
    3. 10
    4. 7
    Show Answer

    Answer: (B) 21.

    For $p=\tfrac12$ the expected duration from $i$ is $D_i=i(N-i)=3\times7=21$ plays. It satisfies $D_i=1+\tfrac12D_{i+1}+\tfrac12D_{i-1}$ with $D_0=D_N=0$.

    Read this in the notes: Unit IX, Gambler's Ruin Problem

    Q27. A gambler with 2 rupees plays against an infinitely rich opponent, winning 1 rupee with probability 0.6 and losing 1 rupee with probability 0.4 at each play. The probability that he is eventually ruined is:
    1. 1
    2. $\dfrac23$
    3. $\dfrac49$
    4. $\dfrac{4}{25}$
    Show Answer

    Answer: (C) $\dfrac49$.

    Letting $N\to\infty$ in the ruin formula with $p\gt q$: $$\begin{aligned}P(\text{ruin}) &= \left(\dfrac qp\right)^i = \left(\dfrac23\right)^2 \\[4pt] &= \dfrac49\text{.}\end{aligned}$$ If $p\le\tfrac12$ ruin is certain.

    Read this in the notes: Unit IX, Gambler's Ruin Problem

    Q28. For the symmetric simple random walk starting at 0, the probability that it is at 0 after 4 steps is:
    1. $\dfrac38$
    2. $\dfrac14$
    3. $\dfrac1{16}$
    4. $\dfrac12$
    Show Answer

    Answer: (A) $\dfrac38$.

    It must take two steps up and two down: $u_4=\binom42\left(\tfrac12\right)^4=\dfrac{6}{16}=\dfrac38$.

    Read this in the notes: Unit IX, Simple Random Walk

    Q29. A simple random walk starts at 0 and moves $+1$ with probability 0.6 and $-1$ with probability 0.4. The mean and variance of its position after 100 steps are:
    1. 20 and 24
    2. 60 and 24
    3. 20 and 100
    4. 20 and 96
    Show Answer

    Answer: (D) 20 and 96.

    Each step has mean $p-q=0.2$ and variance $1-(p-q)^2=4pq=0.96$. So $E(S_{100})=20$ and $V(S_{100})=96$. ($24=npq$ is the variance of the number of upward steps.)

    Read this in the notes: Unit IX, Simple Random Walk

    Q30. For the simple random walk on the integers with $p=0.6$, $q=0.4$, the probability of ever returning to the starting point is:
    1. 1
    2. 0.8
    3. 0.6
    4. 0.4
    Show Answer

    Answer: (B) 0.8.

    The probability of eventual return is $1-|p-q|=1-0.2=0.8$. It equals 1 only for the symmetric walk; for $p\ne q$ the walk drifts away and every state is transient.

    Read this in the notes: Unit IX, Simple Random Walk

    Q31. For the symmetric simple random walk starting at 0, the probability that the first return to 0 occurs at step 4 is:
    1. $\dfrac38$
    2. $\dfrac14$
    3. $\dfrac18$
    4. $\dfrac1{16}$
    Show Answer

    Answer: (C) $\dfrac18$.

    The walk must stay strictly on one side for steps 1 to 3 and return at step 4: the paths $++--$ and $--++$, each of probability $\tfrac1{16}$, total $\tfrac18$. In general $f_{2n}=\dfrac{u_{2n}}{2n-1}$; here $\dfrac{3/8}{3}$.

    Read this in the notes: Unit IX, Simple Random Walk

    Q32. $\{N(t)\}$ is a Poisson process with rate 2. Then $P\{N(3)=4\mid N(1)=1\}$ is about:
    1. 0.195
    2. 0.134
    3. 0.089
    4. 0.147
    Show Answer

    Answer: (A) 0.195.

    By independent and stationary increments this is $P\{N(3)-N(1)=3\}=P\{N(2)=3\}$ with mean $2\times2=4$: $e^{-4}\dfrac{4^3}{3!}=0.195$.

    Read this in the notes: Unit IX, Poisson Process

    Q33. For a Poisson process, $P\{N(1)=1\mid N(3)=2\}$ equals:
    1. $\dfrac13$
    2. $\dfrac29$
    3. $\dfrac23$
    4. $\dfrac49$
    Show Answer

    Answer: (D) $\dfrac49$.

    Given $N(t)=n$, the $n$ events are independently and uniformly placed on $(0,t)$, so $N(s)\sim\text{Bin}\left(n,\tfrac st\right)$. Here $\text{Bin}\left(2,\tfrac13\right)$: $2\cdot\tfrac13\cdot\tfrac23=\tfrac49$. The answer does not involve the rate.

    Read this in the notes: Unit IX, Poisson Process

    Q34. Two independent Poisson processes have rates 2 and 3. The probability that the first event to occur comes from the first process is:
    1. $\dfrac23$
    2. $\dfrac25$
    3. $\dfrac12$
    4. $\dfrac35$
    Show Answer

    Answer: (B) $\dfrac25$.

    The first events occur after independent exponential times with rates 2 and 3, and $P(T_1\lt T_2)=\dfrac{\lambda_1}{\lambda_1+\lambda_2}=\dfrac25$. The superposed process is Poisson with rate 5.

    Read this in the notes: Unit IX, Poisson Process

    Q35. Customers arrive in a Poisson process at 6 per hour, and each is independently of type A with probability $\tfrac13$. The probability that no customer of type A arrives in an hour is about:
    1. 0.0025
    2. 0.018
    3. 0.135
    4. 0.368
    Show Answer

    Answer: (C) 0.135.

    Thinning a Poisson process gives a Poisson process: type A customers arrive at rate $6\times\tfrac13=2$ per hour, independently of the others. $P(\text{none})=e^{-2}=0.135$.

    Q36. For a Poisson process with rate 2, the covariance between $N(3)$ and $N(5)$ is:
    1. 6
    2. 10
    3. 30
    4. 0
    Show Answer

    Answer: (A) 6.

    For $s\lt t$, $N(t)=N(s)+[N(t)-N(s)]$ with independent parts, so $$\begin{aligned}\operatorname{Cov}(N(s),N(t)) &= V(N(s)) \\[4pt] &= \lambda s = 6\text{;}\end{aligned}$$ in general $\lambda\min(s,t)$. The correlation is $\sqrt{s/t}=\sqrt{0.6}$.

    Read this in the notes: Unit IX, Poisson Process

    Q37. Claims arrive in a Poisson process with rate 3 per day; claim sizes are independent with mean 2 and variance 1. The mean and variance of the total claim amount in 2 days are:
    1. 12 and 6
    2. 12 and 24
    3. 6 and 30
    4. 12 and 30
    Show Answer

    Answer: (D) 12 and 30.

    For a compound Poisson process $E[X(t)]=\lambda tE(Y)=6\times2=12$ and $$\begin{aligned}V[X(t)] &= \lambda tE(Y^2) = 6\,(1+4) \\[4pt] &= 30\text{.}\end{aligned}$$ Note $E(Y^2)$, not $V(Y)$.

    Q38. For a Poisson process $\{N(t),t\ge0\}$ with rate $\lambda$, consider the statements:(I) It has independent increments.(II) It is a (weakly) stationary process.(III) The times between successive events are independent exponential variables.(IV) $N(t)-\lambda t$ is a martingale.Which of the statements are correct?
    1. I and III only
    2. I, III and IV only
    3. I, II and III only
    4. I, II, III and IV
    Show Answer

    Answer: (B) I, III and IV only.

    (I) and (III) are defining properties, and (IV) follows from independent increments with $E[N(t)-N(s)]=\lambda(t-s)$. (II) is false: the increments are stationary, but the process itself has mean $\lambda t$ and variance $\lambda t$ that grow with $t$.

    Read this in the notes: Unit IX, Poisson Process

    Q39. Events occur in a Poisson process with rate 2. The mean and variance of the waiting time until the third event are:
    1. 1.5 and 1.5
    2. 6 and 12
    3. 1.5 and 0.75
    4. 0.67 and 0.22
    Show Answer

    Answer: (C) 1.5 and 0.75.

    The waiting time to the $n$th event is the sum of $n$ independent exponential times: gamma with shape $n$ and rate $\lambda$. Mean $\dfrac n\lambda=1.5$, variance $\dfrac{n}{\lambda^2}=0.75$.

    Q40. In a Poisson process exactly 4 events occurred in the interval $(0,10)$. The expected time of the first of them is:
    1. 2
    2. 2.5
    3. 5
    4. 1
    Show Answer

    Answer: (A) 2.

    Given $N(t)=n$, the event times are distributed as the order statistics of $n$ independent uniform variables on $(0,t)$. The smallest has expectation $\dfrac{t}{n+1}=\dfrac{10}{5}=2$.

    Q41. The time $T$ between events of a Poisson process with rate 2 satisfies $P(T\gt1\mid T\gt0.5)=$
    1. 0.135
    2. 0.368
    3. 0.607
    4. 0.223
    Show Answer

    Answer: (B) 0.368.

    The exponential distribution has no memory: $$\begin{aligned}P(T\gt1\mid T\gt0.5) &= P(T\gt0.5) \\[4pt] &= e^{-2(0.5)} = e^{-1} = 0.368\text{.}\end{aligned}$$ The unconditional $P(T\gt1)=e^{-2}=0.135$.

    Q42. For a Poisson process with rate 1, the probability that the second event occurs before time 1 is about:
    1. 0.632
    2. 0.368
    3. 0.184
    4. 0.264
    Show Answer

    Answer: (D) 0.264.

    $$\begin{aligned}P(S_2\le1) &= P\{N(1) \ge 2\} \\[4pt] &= 1-e^{-1}(1+1) = 1-0.736 \\[4pt] &= 0.264\text{.}\end{aligned}$$ The waiting time to the second event exceeds $t$ exactly when fewer than two events have occurred by time $t$.

    Q43. A Yule (linear pure birth) process with birth rate $\lambda=1$ per individual starts with one individual. The distribution of the population size at time $t=\ln2$ is:
    1. Poisson with mean 2
    2. geometric with mean $\ln 2$
    3. geometric on $\{1,2,\dots\}$ with mean 2
    4. binomial
    Show Answer

    Answer: (C) geometric on $\{1,2,\dots\}$ with mean 2.

    For the Yule process with $X(0)=1$, $$\begin{aligned}P\{X(t) &= n\} \\[4pt] &= e^{-\lambda t}(1-e^{-\lambda t})^{n-1}\text{,}\end{aligned}$$ $n\ge1$: geometric with success probability $e^{-\lambda t}=\tfrac12$ and mean $e^{\lambda t}=2$.

    Q44. A pure death process starts with 10 individuals, each dying independently at rate $\mu$. At the time $t$ with $\mu t=\ln2$, the expected number alive is:

    Q45. A linear birth and death process has birth rate $\lambda=2$ and death rate $\mu=1$ per individual and starts with one individual. The probability of ultimate extinction is:
    1. 0
    2. $\dfrac12$
    3. $\dfrac14$
    4. 1
    Show Answer

    Answer: (B) $\dfrac12$.

    If $\lambda\le\mu$ extinction is certain; if $\lambda\gt\mu$ the probability of extinction from $i$ individuals is $\left(\dfrac\mu\lambda\right)^i=\dfrac12$. The mean population size $e^{(\lambda-\mu)t}$ grows without bound although extinction still has probability $\tfrac12$.

    Q46. In a system with Poisson arrivals at rate 4 and infinitely many servers each with exponential service rate 2 (birth rates $\lambda_n=4$, death rates $\mu_n=2n$), the stationary probability that the system is empty is about:
    1. 0.500
    2. 0.018
    3. 0.368
    4. 0.135
    Show Answer

    Answer: (D) 0.135.

    $$\begin{aligned}\pi_n &= \pi_0\prod_{k=1}^{n}\dfrac{\lambda_{k-1}}{\mu_k} \\[4pt] &= \pi_0\dfrac{(\lambda/\mu)^n}{n!}\text{,}\end{aligned}$$ so the stationary distribution is Poisson with mean $\dfrac\lambda\mu=2$, and $\pi_0=e^{-2}=0.135$.

    Read this in the notes: Unit IX, Birth and Death Processes

    Q47. An M/M/1 queue has arrival rate 4 and service rate 5 per hour. The expected number in the system and the expected waiting time in the queue (in hours) are:
    1. 4 and 0.8
    2. 4 and 1
    3. 3.2 and 0.8
    4. 5 and 1
    Show Answer

    Answer: (A) 4 and 0.8.

    $\rho=0.8$. $L=\dfrac{\rho}{1-\rho}=4$; $W=\dfrac{1}{\mu-\lambda}=1$ hour in the system; $W_q=W-\dfrac1\mu=0.8$ hour; $L_q=\lambda W_q=3.2$. Little's formula $L=\lambda W$ ties them together.

    Read this in the notes: Unit IX, M/M/1 Queue

    Q48. In an M/M/1 queue with traffic intensity $\rho=0.8$, the stationary probability that there are at least 3 customers in the system is:
    1. 0.102
    2. 0.488
    3. 0.512
    4. 0.640
    Show Answer

    Answer: (C) 0.512.

    The stationary distribution is geometric, $\pi_n=(1-\rho)\rho^n$, so $P(N\ge n)=\rho^n=(0.8)^3=0.512$. ($0.102$ is $P(N=3)$.)

    Q50. Consider the statements about the M/M/1 queue with traffic intensity $\rho=\lambda/\mu$:(I) A stationary distribution exists if and only if $\rho\lt1$.(II) The stationary distribution of the number in the system is geometric.(III) In the steady state the departure process is Poisson with rate $\lambda$.(IV) The stationary probability that the server is idle is $\rho$.Which of the statements are correct?
    1. I and II only
    2. I, II and IV only
    3. II, III and IV only
    4. I, II and III only
    Show Answer

    Answer: (D) I, II and III only.

    (I) and (II): $\pi_n=(1-\rho)\rho^n$, summable only for $\rho\lt1$. (III) is Burke's theorem. (IV) is false: the server is idle with probability $\pi_0=1-\rho$ and busy with probability $\rho$.
