    Model Questions — Unit VII: Time Series

    UGC NET Statistics (Code 107) · 50 questions · Click "Show Answer" for the worked solution

    About this set

    Trend and seasonality, autocorrelation and partial autocorrelation, stationarity, linear processes, MA, AR and ARMA models, Yule–Walker equations, model identification, forecasting, ARIMA models and spectral analysis. Throughout, $Z_t$ denotes white noise with mean 0 and variance $\sigma^2$.

    These are model questions written for practice at the standard of the paper; they are not questions from a past paper. Try each one before opening the answer. Every answer gives the working and a link to the section of the Unit VII notes that teaches it.

    The questions

    Q1. A centred moving average of period 4 is obtained by averaging two successive 4-term moving averages. The weights it gives to $y_{t-2},y_{t-1},y_t,y_{t+1},y_{t+2}$ are:
    1. $\tfrac15(1,1,1,1,1)$
    2. $\tfrac18(1,2,2,2,1)$
    3. $\tfrac14(1,1,0,1,1)$
    4. $\tfrac1{10}(1,2,4,2,1)$
    Show Answer

    Answer: (B) $\tfrac18(1,2,2,2,1)$.

    $$\begin{aligned}&\tfrac12\left[\tfrac14(y_{t-2}+y_{t-1}+y_t+y_{t+1})+\tfrac14(y_{t-1}+y_t+y_{t+1}+y_{t+2})\right] \\[4pt] &\quad = \tfrac18(y_{t-2}+2y_{t-1}+2y_t+2y_{t+1}+y_{t+2})\text{.}\end{aligned}$$ Centring is needed because an even-period average falls between two time points.

    Read this in the notes: Unit VII, Time Series & Descriptive Measures

    Q2. The values of a series in five successive years are 10, 12, 15, 17 and 21. A straight-line trend is fitted by least squares. The trend value for the sixth year is:
    1. 22.7
    2. 23.7
    3. 24.0
    4. 23.1
    Show Answer

    Answer: (D) 23.1.

    Code the years $t=-2,-1,0,1,2$: $\sum y=75$, $\sum ty=-20-12+0+17+42=27$, $\sum t^2=10$. So $a=\bar y=15$, $b=\dfrac{27}{10}=2.7$, and for the sixth year ($t=3$) the trend value is $15+2.7(3)=23.1$.

    Read this in the notes: Unit VII, Time Series & Descriptive Measures

    Q3. In a multiplicative model the seasonal indices of the first three quarters are 80, 110 and 120. The seasonal index of the fourth quarter is:
    1. 90
    2. 100
    3. 110
    4. 77.5
    Show Answer

    Answer: (A) 90.

    Quarterly seasonal indices average 100, so they add up to 400. The fourth is $400-(80+110+120)=90$. (Monthly indices add up to 1200.)

    Read this in the notes: Unit VII, Time Series & Descriptive Measures

    Q4. Simple exponential smoothing with constant $\alpha=0.3$ gave the forecast 20 for the current period, in which the actual value turned out to be 30. The forecast for the next period is:
    1. 27
    2. 25
    3. 23
    4. 21
    Show Answer

    Answer: (C) 23.

    $$\begin{aligned}F_{t+1} &= \alpha y_t+(1-\alpha)F_t \\[4pt] &= F_t+\alpha(y_t-F_t) = 20+0.3(10) \\[4pt] &= 23\text{.}\end{aligned}$$ Using weight 0.7 on the actual value gives 27.

    Read this in the notes: Unit VII, Time Series & Descriptive Measures

    Q5. Applying a moving average to a purely random series produces a smoothed series that shows regular-looking oscillations which are not present in the original data. This is called the:
    1. Gibbs phenomenon
    2. Slutsky–Yule effect
    3. Yule–Walker effect
    4. aliasing effect
    Show Answer

    Answer: (B) Slutsky–Yule effect.

    A moving average of uncorrelated terms is autocorrelated (successive averages share terms), and an autocorrelated series wanders in waves. Slutsky and Yule showed that such spurious cycles can be created by the smoothing itself. Aliasing is the confusion of frequencies caused by sampling at discrete times.

    Read this in the notes: Unit VII, Time Series & Descriptive Measures

    Q6. Which of the following pairs $(\rho_1,\rho_2)$ can NOT be the first two autocorrelations of a stationary process?
    1. (0.5, 0.25)
    2. (0.4, 0)
    3. ($-0.5$, 0.4)
    4. (0.8, 0.1)
    Show Answer

    Answer: (D) (0.8, 0.1).

    The autocorrelation matrix must be non-negative definite. For lags up to 2 this requires $\rho_2\ge2\rho_1^2-1$. For $\rho_1=0.8$ the bound is $0.28\gt0.1$, so $(0.8,0.1)$ is impossible: two observations each highly correlated with the one between them cannot be nearly uncorrelated. The other pairs satisfy the condition.

    Read this in the notes: Unit VII, Autocovariance, ACF, PACF, Correlogram

    Q7. A stationary process has $\rho_1=0.5$ and $\rho_2=0.4$. Its partial autocorrelation at lag 2 is:
    1. 0.20
    2. 0.40
    3. 0.15
    4. 0.25
    Show Answer

    Answer: (A) 0.20.

    $$\begin{aligned}\phi_{22} &= \dfrac{\rho_2-\rho_1^2}{1-\rho_1^2} \\[4pt] &= \dfrac{0.40-0.25}{0.75} = 0.20\text{.}\end{aligned}$$ It is the correlation between $X_t$ and $X_{t-2}$ after removing the linear effect of $X_{t-1}$.

    Read this in the notes: Unit VII, Autocovariance, ACF, PACF, Correlogram

    Q8. For a white-noise series of length $n=100$, the approximate 95% limits within which the sample autocorrelations should lie are:
    1. $\pm0.100$
    2. $\pm0.010$
    3. $\pm0.196$
    4. $\pm0.020$
    Show Answer

    Answer: (C) $\pm0.196$.

    For white noise the sample autocorrelations are approximately independent $N\left(0,\tfrac1n\right)$, so the limits are $\pm\dfrac{1.96}{\sqrt n}=\pm0.196$. About one lag in twenty will fall outside by chance.

    Read this in the notes: Unit VII, Autocovariance, ACF, PACF, Correlogram

    Q9. Let $X_t=A\cos\omega t+B\sin\omega t$, where $A$ and $B$ are uncorrelated random variables with mean 0 and variance $\sigma^2$, and $\omega$ is a constant. The autocovariance function of $\{X_t\}$ is:
    1. $\sigma^2$ for $h=0$ and 0 otherwise
    2. $\sigma^2\cos\omega h$
    3. $\sigma^2\sin\omega h$
    4. $\sigma^2e^{-|h|}$
    Show Answer

    Answer: (B) $\sigma^2\cos\omega h$.

    $$\begin{aligned}&\operatorname{Cov}(X_t,X_{t+h}) \\[4pt] &\quad = \sigma^2[\cos\omega t\cos\omega(t+h)+\sin\omega t\sin\omega(t+h)] \\[4pt] &\quad = \sigma^2\cos\omega h\text{.}\end{aligned}$$ It depends only on $h$, so the process is stationary, but the autocovariance never dies out: the process is deterministic, completely predictable from two past values.

    Read this in the notes: Unit VII, Autocovariance, ACF, PACF, Correlogram

    Q10. Match the model in List I with the theoretical behaviour of its ACF and PACF in List II.
    List IList II
    (a) AR($p$)(i) ACF tails off; PACF cuts off after lag $p$
    (b) MA($q$)(ii) ACF cuts off after lag $q$; PACF tails off
    (c) ARMA($p,q$)(iii) Both ACF and PACF tail off
    (d) White noise(iv) Both are zero at all non-zero lags
    1. (a)-(i), (b)-(ii), (c)-(iii), (d)-(iv)
    2. (a)-(ii), (b)-(i), (c)-(iii), (d)-(iv)
    3. (a)-(i), (b)-(ii), (c)-(iv), (d)-(iii)
    4. (a)-(iii), (b)-(ii), (c)-(i), (d)-(iv)
    Show Answer

    Answer: (A) (a)-(i), (b)-(ii), (c)-(iii), (d)-(iv).

    This duality is the basis of Box–Jenkins identification: the PACF identifies the order of a pure autoregression, the ACF the order of a pure moving average, and when neither cuts off a mixed model is indicated.

    Read this in the notes: Unit VII, Autocovariance, ACF, PACF, Correlogram

    Q11. Consider the processes:(I) $X_t=Z_t$(II) $X_t=X_{t-1}+Z_t$, $X_0=0$(III) $X_t=Z_t+0.5Z_{t-1}$(IV) $X_t=t+Z_t$Which of them are weakly stationary?
    1. I only
    2. I, II and III only
    3. I and III only
    4. I, III and IV only
    Show Answer

    Answer: (C) I and III only.

    (I) and (III) have constant mean and autocovariances depending only on the lag (every finite moving average of white noise is stationary). (II) is a random walk with $V(X_t)=t\sigma^2$, growing with $t$. (IV) has mean $t$, not constant.

    Read this in the notes: Unit VII, Stationarity & Ergodicity

    Q12. Let $Z_t$ be independent $N(0,1)$ variables and $X_t=Z_tZ_{t-1}$. Then $\{X_t\}$ is:
    1. not stationary
    2. stationary with $\rho_1=0.5$
    3. a sequence of independent random variables
    4. white noise, but its terms are not independent
    Show Answer

    Answer: (D) white noise, but its terms are not independent.

    $E(X_t)=0$, $V(X_t)=E(Z_t^2)E(Z_{t-1}^2)=1$ and $$\begin{aligned}&\operatorname{Cov}(X_t,X_{t-1}) \\[4pt] &\quad = E(Z_t)E(Z_{t-1}^2)E(Z_{t-2}) = 0\text{,}\end{aligned}$$ so the series is uncorrelated with constant mean and variance: white noise. But $X_t$ and $X_{t-1}$ share $Z_{t-1}$; for instance $X_t^2$ and $X_{t-1}^2$ are correlated. Uncorrelated does not mean independent.

    Read this in the notes: Unit VII, Stationarity & Ergodicity

    Q13. Given below are two statements, one labelled Assertion (A) and the other Reason (R).Assertion (A): A strictly stationary process with finite second moments is weakly stationary.Reason (R): A weakly stationary Gaussian process is strictly stationary.Choose the correct answer.
    1. Both (A) and (R) are true and (R) is the correct explanation of (A)
    2. Both (A) and (R) are true but (R) is not the correct explanation of (A)
    3. (A) is true but (R) is false
    4. (A) is false but (R) is true
    Show Answer

    Answer: (B) Both (A) and (R) are true but (R) is not the correct explanation of (A).

    Both are true. (A): if all joint distributions are invariant under shifts, so are the means and covariances, when they exist. (R): a Gaussian process is determined by its means and covariances, so invariance of these gives invariance of every joint distribution. (R) is the converse of (A) for Gaussian processes, not an explanation of it.

    Read this in the notes: Unit VII, Stationarity & Ergodicity

    Q14. For a stationary AR(1) process with $\phi=0.5$ and variance $\gamma_0$, the variance of the mean of $n$ consecutive observations is, for large $n$, approximately $c\,\dfrac{\gamma_0}{n}$, where $c$ is:
    1. 3
    2. 1
    3. 2
    4. 4
    Show Answer

    Answer: (A) 3.

    $$\begin{aligned}nV(\bar X) &\to \sum_{h=-\infty}^{\infty}\gamma_h \\[4pt] &= \gamma_0\left(1+2\sum_{h\ge1}\phi^h\right) \\[4pt] &= \gamma_0\dfrac{1+\phi}{1-\phi} = 3\gamma_0\text{.}\end{aligned}$$ Positive autocorrelation makes the mean three times as variable as for independent observations; the sample is ‘worth’ only $n/3$ independent values.

    Read this in the notes: Unit VII, Stationarity & Ergodicity

    Q15. The ARMA(1,1) process $X_t=0.5X_{t-1}+Z_t+0.4Z_{t-1}$ is written as $X_t=\sum_{j\ge0}\psi_jZ_{t-j}$ with $\psi_0=1$. The values of $\psi_1$ and $\psi_2$ are:
    1. 0.5 and 0.25
    2. 0.9 and 0.81
    3. 0.9 and 0.45
    4. 0.4 and 0.2
    Show Answer

    Answer: (C) 0.9 and 0.45.

    Equating coefficients in $(1-0.5B)\psi(B)=1+0.4B$: $\psi_1-0.5=0.4$, so $\psi_1=0.9$; and $\psi_j=0.5\psi_{j-1}$ for $j\ge2$, so $\psi_2=0.45$. In general $\psi_j=(\phi+\theta)\phi^{j-1}$.

    Read this in the notes: Unit VII, General Linear Process & Wold Decomposition

    Q16. A linear process $X_t=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\psi_jZ_{t-j}$ has $\psi_j=(0.5)^j$. Its variance is:
    1. $2\sigma^2$
    2. $\dfrac43\sigma^2$
    3. $\sigma^2$
    4. $4\sigma^2$
    Show Answer

    Answer: (B) $\dfrac43\sigma^2$.

    $$\begin{aligned}V(X_t) &= \sigma^2\sum\psi_j^2 = \sigma^2\sum(0.25)^j \\[4pt] &= \dfrac{\sigma^2}{1-0.25} = \dfrac43\sigma^2\text{.}\end{aligned}$$ This is the AR(1) process with $\phi=0.5$ in its moving-average (Wold) form. $2\sigma^2$ is $\sigma^2\sum\psi_j$, not the variance.

    Read this in the notes: Unit VII, General Linear Process & Wold Decomposition

    Q17. For the MA(1) process $X_t=Z_t+0.5Z_{t-1}$, the autocorrelation at lag 1 is:
    1. 0.5
    2. 0.25
    3. 0.8
    4. 0.4
    Show Answer

    Answer: (D) 0.4.

    $\rho_1=\dfrac{\theta}{1+\theta^2}=\dfrac{0.5}{1.25}=0.4$, and $\rho_k=0$ for $k\ge2$.

    Read this in the notes: Unit VII, Moving Average (MA) Process

    Q18. For an MA(1) process, the largest possible value of $|\rho_1|$ is:
    1. 0.5
    2. 1
    3. 0.25
    4. 0.707
    Show Answer

    Answer: (A) 0.5.

    $\rho_1=\dfrac{\theta}{1+\theta^2}$ and $1+\theta^2\ge2|\theta|$, so $|\rho_1|\le\tfrac12$, with equality at $\theta=\pm1$. A series with lag-one autocorrelation 0.7 and nothing beyond cannot be an MA(1).

    Read this in the notes: Unit VII, Moving Average (MA) Process

    Q19. The MA(1) processes $X_t=Z_t+2Z_{t-1}$ and $X_t=Z_t+0.5Z_{t-1}$:
    1. have different autocorrelation functions, and both are invertible
    2. have the same autocorrelation function, and both are invertible
    3. have the same autocorrelation function, and only the second is invertible
    4. have the same autocorrelation function, and only the first is invertible
    Show Answer

    Answer: (C) have the same autocorrelation function, and only the second is invertible.

    $\dfrac{\theta}{1+\theta^2}$ is unchanged when $\theta$ is replaced by $\dfrac1\theta$: both give $\rho_1=0.4$. Invertibility requires $|\theta|\lt1$ (the root of $1+\theta z$ outside the unit circle), which holds only for $\theta=0.5$. Invertibility is imposed precisely to make the model unique.

    Read this in the notes: Unit VII, Moving Average (MA) Process

    Q20. For the MA(2) process $X_t=Z_t+0.5Z_{t-1}+0.5Z_{t-2}$, the autocorrelations $\rho_1$ and $\rho_2$ are:
    1. $\dfrac13$ and $\dfrac13$
    2. $\dfrac12$ and $\dfrac13$
    3. $\dfrac12$ and $\dfrac14$
    4. $\dfrac34$ and $\dfrac12$
    Show Answer

    Answer: (B) $\dfrac12$ and $\dfrac13$.

    $\gamma_0=\sigma^2(1+\theta_1^2+\theta_2^2)=1.5\sigma^2$, $\gamma_1=\sigma^2(\theta_1+\theta_1\theta_2)=0.75\sigma^2$, $\gamma_2=\sigma^2\theta_2=0.5\sigma^2$. So $\rho_1=\tfrac12$, $\rho_2=\tfrac13$ and $\rho_k=0$ for $k\ge3$.

    Read this in the notes: Unit VII, Moving Average (MA) Process

    Q21. The MA(2) process $X_t=Z_t-1.3Z_{t-1}+0.4Z_{t-2}$ is:
    1. invertible, since the roots of $1-1.3z+0.4z^2=0$ are 1.25 and 2
    2. not invertible, since one coefficient exceeds 1 in absolute value
    3. not invertible, since the roots of $1-1.3z+0.4z^2=0$ are 0.8 and 0.5
    4. not stationary
    Show Answer

    Answer: (A) invertible, since the roots of $1-1.3z+0.4z^2=0$ are 1.25 and 2.

    $$\begin{aligned}&1-1.3z+0.4z^2 \\[4pt] &\quad = (1-0.8z)(1-0.5z)\text{,}\end{aligned}$$ with roots $z=1.25$ and $z=2$, both outside the unit circle: invertible. Invertibility is a condition on the roots, not on the size of individual coefficients. Every finite moving average is stationary.

    Read this in the notes: Unit VII, Moving Average (MA) Process

    Q22. For the AR(1) process $X_t=0.8X_{t-1}+Z_t$ with $\sigma^2=1$, the variance of $X_t$ and the autocorrelation at lag 3 are:
    1. 5.00 and 0.512
    2. 1.64 and 0.512
    3. 2.78 and 0.640
    4. 2.78 and 0.512
    Show Answer

    Answer: (D) 2.78 and 0.512.

    $\gamma_0=\dfrac{\sigma^2}{1-\phi^2}=\dfrac{1}{0.36}=2.78$ and $\rho_3=\phi^3=0.512$. ($5.00=\tfrac{1}{1-\phi}$ is the sum of the $\psi$ weights, not the variance.)

    Read this in the notes: Unit VII, Autoregressive (AR) Process

    Q23. For which of the following values of $(\phi_1,\phi_2)$ is the AR(2) process $X_t=\phi_1X_{t-1}+\phi_2X_{t-2}+Z_t$ stationary?
    1. (1.2, $-0.1$)
    2. (0.2, 0.9)
    3. (0.5, 0.3)
    4. ($-0.5$, 0.6)
    Show Answer

    Answer: (C) (0.5, 0.3).

    The stationarity region is the triangle $\phi_1+\phi_2\lt1$, $\phi_2-\phi_1\lt1$, $|\phi_2|\lt1$. $(0.5,0.3)$ satisfies all three. $(1.2,-0.1)$ and $(0.2,0.9)$ have $\phi_1+\phi_2=1.1$; $(-0.5,0.6)$ has $\phi_2-\phi_1=1.1$.

    Read this in the notes: Unit VII, Autoregressive (AR) Process

    Q24. For the stationary AR(2) process $X_t=0.5X_{t-1}+0.3X_{t-2}+Z_t$, the autocorrelations $\rho_1$ and $\rho_2$ are approximately:
    1. 0.500 and 0.550
    2. 0.714 and 0.657
    3. 0.714 and 0.510
    4. 0.625 and 0.613
    Show Answer

    Answer: (B) 0.714 and 0.657.

    The Yule–Walker equations give $$\begin{aligned}\rho_1 &= \dfrac{\phi_1}{1-\phi_2} = \dfrac{0.5}{0.7} \\[4pt] &= 0.714\end{aligned}$$ and $$\begin{aligned}\rho_2 &= \phi_1\rho_1+\phi_2 = 0.357+0.3 \\[4pt] &= 0.657\text{.}\end{aligned}$$

    Read this in the notes: Unit VII, Autoregressive (AR) Process

    Q25. The autocorrelation function of the AR(2) process $X_t=X_{t-1}-0.5X_{t-2}+Z_t$ is:
    1. a damped sine wave
    2. a mixture of two decaying exponentials
    3. zero after lag 2
    4. constant
    Show Answer

    Answer: (A) a damped sine wave.

    The process is stationary ($\phi_1+\phi_2=0.5$, $\phi_2-\phi_1=-1.5$, $|\phi_2|\lt1$) and $\phi_1^2+4\phi_2=1-2\lt0$, so the roots of the characteristic equation are complex and the autocorrelation function is a damped sine wave with damping factor $\sqrt{-\phi_2}=0.707$: the series shows pseudo-periodic behaviour.

    Read this in the notes: Unit VII, Autoregressive (AR) Process

    Q26. The mean of the stationary process $X_t=5+0.5X_{t-1}+Z_t$ is:
    1. 5
    2. 2.5
    3. 7.5
    4. 10
    Show Answer

    Answer: (D) 10.

    Taking expectations, $\mu=5+0.5\mu$, so $\mu=\dfrac{5}{1-0.5}=10$. The constant in an autoregression is not the mean; it is $\mu(1-\phi)$.

    Read this in the notes: Unit VII, Autoregressive (AR) Process

    Q27. For the AR(2) process $X_t=0.5X_{t-1}+0.3X_{t-2}+Z_t$, the partial autocorrelations at lags 2 and 3 are:
    1. 0.657 and 0.3
    2. 0.3 and 0.15
    3. 0.3 and 0
    4. 0.5 and 0.3
    Show Answer

    Answer: (C) 0.3 and 0.

    For an AR($p$) process the partial autocorrelation at lag $p$ equals $\phi_p$ and is zero beyond it. So $\phi_{22}=\phi_2=0.3$ and $\phi_{33}=0$. (At lag 1, $\phi_{11}=\rho_1=0.714$.)

    Read this in the notes: Unit VII, Autoregressive (AR) Process

    Q28. For the ARMA(1,1) process $X_t=0.5X_{t-1}+Z_t+0.4Z_{t-1}$, the autocorrelation at lag 1 is about:
    1. 0.900
    2. 0.692
    3. 0.500
    4. 0.577
    Show Answer

    Answer: (B) 0.692.

    $$\begin{aligned}\rho_1 &= \dfrac{(1+\phi\theta)(\phi+\theta)}{1+2\phi\theta+\theta^2} \\[4pt] &= \dfrac{(1.2)(0.9)}{1+0.4+0.16} \\[4pt] &= \dfrac{1.08}{1.56} = 0.692\text{.}\end{aligned}$$ After that $\rho_k=\phi\rho_{k-1}$.

    Read this in the notes: Unit VII, ARMA Process & Conditions

    Q29. The process defined by $X_t-0.5X_{t-1}=Z_t-0.5Z_{t-1}$ is:
    1. white noise
    2. an ARMA(1,1) process with $\rho_1\ne0$
    3. non-stationary
    4. non-invertible
    Show Answer

    Answer: (A) white noise.

    The autoregressive and moving-average operators have the common factor $(1-0.5B)$, which cancels, leaving $X_t=Z_t$. (The formula gives $\rho_1\propto(1+\phi\theta)(\phi+\theta)=0$.) Such parameter redundancy is why an ARMA model is required to have no common factors.

    Read this in the notes: Unit VII, ARMA Process & Conditions

    Q30. The ARMA(1,1) process $(1-0.5B)X_t=(1+2B)Z_t$ is:
    1. causal and invertible
    2. neither causal nor invertible
    3. invertible but not causal
    4. causal but not invertible
    Show Answer

    Answer: (D) causal but not invertible.

    Causality (stationarity) needs the root of $1-0.5z=0$, $z=2$, outside the unit circle: satisfied. Invertibility needs the root of $1+2z=0$, $z=-0.5$, outside the unit circle: not satisfied.

    Read this in the notes: Unit VII, ARMA Process & Conditions

    Q31. For a stationary ARMA(1,1) process with autoregressive parameter $\phi$, the autocorrelations satisfy:
    1. $\rho_k=\phi^k$ for all $k\ge1$
    2. $\rho_k=0$ for $k\ge2$
    3. $\rho_k=\phi\rho_{k-1}$ for $k\ge2$
    4. $\rho_k=\phi\rho_{k-1}$ for all $k\ge1$
    Show Answer

    Answer: (C) $\rho_k=\phi\rho_{k-1}$ for $k\ge2$.

    Multiply the model by $X_{t-k}$ and take expectations: for $k\ge2$ the moving-average terms are uncorrelated with $X_{t-k}$, so $\gamma_k=\phi\gamma_{k-1}$. At $k=1$ the term $\theta Z_{t-1}$ is correlated with $X_{t-1}$, so $\rho_1\ne\phi$: the decay starts from $\rho_1$, not from $\rho_0$.

    Read this in the notes: Unit VII, ARMA Process & Conditions

    Q32. A series has $r_1=0.5$ and $r_2=0.4$. The Yule–Walker estimates of $(\phi_1,\phi_2)$ for an AR(2) model are:
    1. (0.4, 0.2)
    2. (0.5, 0.4)
    3. (0.2, 0.4)
    4. (0.6, 0.1)
    Show Answer

    Answer: (A) (0.4, 0.2).

    Solve $r_1=\phi_1+\phi_2r_1$, $r_2=\phi_1r_1+\phi_2$: $$\begin{aligned}\hat\phi_2 &= \dfrac{r_2-r_1^2}{1-r_1^2} \\[4pt] &= \dfrac{0.15}{0.75} = 0.2\end{aligned}$$ and $$\begin{aligned}\hat\phi_1 &= \dfrac{r_1(1-r_2)}{1-r_1^2} \\[4pt] &= \dfrac{0.30}{0.75} = 0.4\text{.}\end{aligned}$$

    Read this in the notes: Unit VII, Yule–Walker Equations

    Q33. A series has sample variance $c_0=4$ and $r_1=0.5$. For an AR(1) model the Yule–Walker estimate of the white-noise variance is:
    1. 4
    2. 2
    3. 1
    4. 3
    Show Answer

    Answer: (D) 3.

    $\hat\phi=r_1=0.5$ and $$\begin{aligned}\hat\sigma^2 &= c_0(1-\hat\phi r_1) = 4(1-0.25) \\[4pt] &= 3\text{.}\end{aligned}$$

    Q34. An AR(2) process has $\gamma_0=10$, $\phi_1=0.4$, $\phi_2=0.2$, $\rho_1=0.5$ and $\rho_2=0.4$. The white-noise variance $\sigma^2$ is:
    1. 8.0
    2. 7.2
    3. 6.0
    4. 2.8
    Show Answer

    Answer: (B) 7.2.

    $$\begin{aligned}\sigma^2 &= \gamma_0(1-\phi_1\rho_1-\phi_2\rho_2) \\[4pt] &= 10(1-0.20-0.08) = 7.2\text{.}\end{aligned}$$ The fraction of the variance explained by the two lagged values is $\phi_1\rho_1+\phi_2\rho_2=0.28$.

    Q35. For a model with $k$ estimated parameters fitted to $n$ observations, consider the statements:(I) $AIC=-2\ln L+2k$.(II) For $n\ge8$, BIC penalises an additional parameter more heavily than AIC.(III) AIC selects the true order consistently as $n\to\infty$.(IV) BIC selects the true order consistently as $n\to\infty$.Which of the statements are correct?
    1. I and II only
    2. I, II and III only
    3. I, II and IV only
    4. I, II, III and IV
    Show Answer

    Answer: (C) I, II and IV only.

    $BIC=-2\ln L+k\ln n$, and $\ln n\gt2$ for $n\ge8$: (I), (II). BIC is consistent, while AIC has a positive probability of choosing too large an order however large $n$ is, so it tends to overfit: (IV) true, (III) false.

    Q36. The stages of the Box–Jenkins approach to time-series modelling, in the correct order, are:
    1. identification, estimation, diagnostic checking, forecasting
    2. estimation, identification, diagnostic checking, forecasting
    3. identification, diagnostic checking, estimation, forecasting
    4. diagnostic checking, identification, estimation, forecasting
    Show Answer

    Answer: (A) identification, estimation, diagnostic checking, forecasting.

    A tentative model is identified from the ACF and PACF (after differencing if needed), its parameters are estimated, the residuals are checked for whiteness, and only an adequate model is used for forecasting. If the check fails, the cycle is repeated.

    Q37. An ARMA(1,1) model is fitted and the Ljung–Box statistic is computed from the first 20 residual autocorrelations. Under the hypothesis that the model is adequate, it is referred to chi-square with degrees of freedom:
    1. 20

    Q38. The sample ACF of a stationary series is significant at lags 1 and 2 and negligible afterwards, while the sample PACF decays gradually. The model suggested is:

    An ACF that cuts off after lag $q$ with a PACF that tails off is the signature of MA($q$); here $q=2$. The opposite pattern (PACF cutting off after lag 2) would indicate AR(2).

    Q39. An AR(1) process has mean 10 and $\phi=0.5$, and the last observation is $X_n=12$. The minimum mean square error forecast of $X_{n+2}$ is:
    1. 11.0
    2. 10.0
    3. 10.5
    4. 12.0
    Show Answer

    Answer: (C) 10.5.

    $$\begin{aligned}\hat X_n(h) &= \mu+\phi^h(X_n-\mu) \\[4pt] &= 10+0.25(2) = 10.5\text{.}\end{aligned}$$ The forecasts decay geometrically to the mean: 11, 10.5, 10.25, ...

    Q40. For the AR(1) process with $\phi=0.5$ and $\sigma^2=1$, the variance of the two-step-ahead forecast error is:
    1. 1.25
    2. 1.00
    3. 1.33
    4. 1.50
    Show Answer

    Answer: (A) 1.25.

    $$\begin{aligned}V[e_n(h)] &= \sigma^2\sum_{j=0}^{h-1}\psi_j^2 \\[4pt] &= \sigma^2\,\dfrac{1-\phi^{2h}}{1-\phi^2}\text{.}\end{aligned}$$ For $h=2$: $1+\phi^2=1.25$. As $h\to\infty$ it rises to the process variance $\tfrac43$.

    Q41. For the MA(1) process $X_t=Z_t+0.6Z_{t-1}$ with $\sigma^2=1$, the variances of the one-step and two-step forecast errors are:
    1. 1 and 1
    2. 1 and 1.36
    3. 1.36 and 1.36
    4. 1 and 1.6
    Show Answer

    Answer: (B) 1 and 1.36.

    One step ahead the forecast is $0.6Z_n$ and the error is $Z_{n+1}$: variance 1. Two or more steps ahead the forecast is the mean, and the error is $X_{n+h}$ itself: variance $1+\theta^2=1.36$. An MA($q$) process has no forecasting memory beyond $q$ steps.

    Q42. For the random walk $X_t=X_{t-1}+Z_t$ with $\sigma=2$, the standard error of the forecast of $X_{n+4}$ made at time $n$ is:
    1. 2
    2. 8
    3. 16
    4. 4
    Show Answer

    Answer: (D) 4.

    The forecast is $X_n$ for every lead time, and the error $Z_{n+1}+\cdots+Z_{n+h}$ has variance $h\sigma^2=16$, standard error 4. The forecast limits widen as $\sqrt h$ without bound, unlike those of a stationary process.

    Q43. For the random walk $X_t=Z_1+\cdots+Z_t$, the correlation between $X_4$ and $X_9$ is:
    1. $\dfrac23$
    2. $\dfrac49$
    3. $\dfrac{4}{13}$
    4. $\dfrac12$
    Show Answer

    Answer: (A) $\dfrac23$.

    $\operatorname{Cov}(X_s,X_t)=\min(s,t)\,\sigma^2$, so for $s\lt t$, $$\begin{aligned}\operatorname{Corr}(X_s,X_t) &= \dfrac{s}{\sqrt{st}} \\[4pt] &= \sqrt{\dfrac st} = \sqrt{\dfrac49} = \dfrac23\text{.}\end{aligned}$$ It depends on $s$ and $t$ separately, not on $t-s$ alone: the process is not stationary.

    Q44. The process $X_t=1.5X_{t-1}-0.5X_{t-2}+Z_t$ is:
    1. a stationary AR(2) process
    2. ARIMA(0,1,1)
    3. ARIMA(1,1,0)
    4. ARIMA(0,2,0)
    Show Answer

    Answer: (C) ARIMA(1,1,0).

    $$\begin{aligned}&1-1.5B+0.5B^2 \\[4pt] &\quad = (1-B)(1-0.5B)\text{.}\end{aligned}$$ One root is on the unit circle, so the process is not stationary; its first difference $W_t=(1-B)X_t$ satisfies $W_t=0.5W_{t-1}+Z_t$, a stationary AR(1). Hence ARIMA(1,1,0).

    Q45. If white noise $Z_t$ is differenced unnecessarily, the series $W_t=Z_t-Z_{t-1}$ is:
    1. white noise
    2. a non-invertible MA(1) process with $\rho_1=-0.5$
    3. an AR(1) process with $\phi=-1$

    Q46. Consider the statements about the Dickey–Fuller test:(I) The null hypothesis is that the series has a unit root.(II) Under the null hypothesis the test statistic has Student's $t$ distribution.(III) The augmented test adds lagged differences to allow for serial correlation in the errors.(IV) Rejection of the null hypothesis supports stationarity.Which of the statements are correct?
    1. I and II only
    2. I and III only
    3. I, II and IV only
    4. I, III and IV only
    Show Answer

    Answer: (D) I, III and IV only.

    The test regresses $\nabla X_t$ on $X_{t-1}$ and tests whether its coefficient is zero (a unit root) against the alternative that it is negative (stationarity). Under the null the $t$-ratio does not have a $t$ distribution; it has the skewed Dickey–Fuller distribution with its own critical values: (II) is false.

    Q47. For the MA(1) process $X_t=Z_t+0.5Z_{t-1}$ with spectral density $f(\lambda)$, $-\pi\le\lambda\le\pi$, the ratio $f(0)/f(\pi)$ is:
    1. 3
    2. 1
    3. 9
    4. 2.25
    Show Answer

    Answer: (C) 9.

    $$\begin{aligned}f(\lambda) &= \dfrac{\sigma^2}{2\pi}\left|1+\theta e^{-i\lambda}\right|^2 \\[4pt] &= \dfrac{\sigma^2}{2\pi}(1+\theta^2+2\theta\cos\lambda)\text{.}\end{aligned}$$ So $f(0)\propto(1+\theta)^2=2.25$ and $f(\pi)\propto(1-\theta)^2=0.25$: the ratio is 9. With $\theta\gt0$ the power is concentrated at low frequencies.

    Read this in the notes: Unit VII, Spectral Analysis

    Q48. The spectral density of the AR(1) process $X_t=\phi X_{t-1}+Z_t$ with $0\lt\phi\lt1$ is:
    1. largest at frequency 0 and decreasing on $(0,\pi)$
    2. largest at frequency $\pi$ and increasing on $(0,\pi)$
    3. constant
    4. largest at frequency $\pi/2$
    Show Answer

    Answer: (A) largest at frequency 0 and decreasing on $(0,\pi)$.

    $f(\lambda)=\dfrac{\sigma^2}{2\pi(1-2\phi\cos\lambda+\phi^2)}$. For $\phi\gt0$ the denominator is smallest at $\lambda=0$, so low frequencies dominate, matching a smooth, slowly wandering series. For $\phi\lt0$ the peak is at $\pi$ and the series oscillates rapidly. White noise has a constant spectrum.

    Q50. Given below are two statements, one labelled Assertion (A) and the other Reason (R).Assertion (A): The periodogram is not a consistent estimator of the spectral density.Reason (R): The variance of a periodogram ordinate does not tend to zero as the length of the series increases.Choose the correct answer.
    1. Both (A) and (R) are true and (R) is the correct explanation of (A)
    2. Both (A) and (R) are true but (R) is not the correct explanation of (A)
    3. (A) is true but (R) is false
    4. (A) is false but (R) is true
    Show Answer

    Answer: (A) Both (A) and (R) are true and (R) is the correct explanation of (A).

    The periodogram is asymptotically unbiased, but each ordinate behaves like $f(\lambda)$ times a $\tfrac12\chi^2_2$ variable, with variance about $f(\lambda)^2$ however long the series is, and neighbouring ordinates are nearly independent. So it never settles down; consistent estimates are obtained by smoothing it with a spectral window. Both statements are true and (R) is the reason.
