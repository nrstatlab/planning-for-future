    Unit 1 — Probability and Distributions (50 MCQs)

    Probability, RVs, distributions, MGF, joint distributions, CLT, LLN.

    1. If $A$ and $B$ are independent events with $P(A)=0.3,\,P(B)=0.4$, then $P(A\cup B)$ equals:
    1. 0.58
    2. 0.70
    3. 0.42
    4. 0.12
    Show Answer

    A. $P(A\cup B)=0.3+0.4-0.12=0.58$.

    2. Two cards are drawn without replacement from a standard deck. The probability that both are kings is:
    1. $1/169$
    2. $1/221$
    3. $1/52$
    4. $4/52$
    Show Answer

    B. $\frac{4}{52}\cdot\frac{3}{51}=\frac{1}{221}$.

    3. Bayes' theorem expresses the posterior probability in terms of:
    1. Likelihood only
    2. Prior only
    3. Prior × Likelihood / Marginal
    4. Conditional / Joint
    Show Answer

    C. Posterior $=$ (prior $\times$ likelihood) / marginal: $P(B_k\mid A)=\dfrac{P(A\mid B_k)P(B_k)}{\sum_i P(A\mid B_i)P(B_i)}$.

    4. The MGF of a Poisson distribution with mean $\lambda$ is:
    1. $e^{\lambda(e^t-1)}$
    2. $e^{\lambda t}$
    3. $(1-\lambda t)^{-1}$
    4. $e^{-\lambda+\lambda t}$
    Show Answer

    A. $M(t)=\sum_x e^{tx}e^{-\lambda}\lambda^x/x!=e^{-\lambda}e^{\lambda e^t}=e^{\lambda(e^t-1)}$. D is $e^{\lambda(t-1)}$, which is not the same function.

    5. If $X\sim N(0,1)$, then $E(X^4)$ equals:
    1. 1
    2. 2
    3. 3
    4. 6
    Show Answer

    C. Fourth central moment of $N(0,1)$ is $3\sigma^4=3$.

    6. For $X\sim\text{Exp}(\lambda)$, the median is:
    1. $1/\lambda$
    2. $\ln 2/\lambda$
    3. $\lambda\ln 2$
    4. $2/\lambda$
    Show Answer

    B. Solve $1-e^{-\lambda m}=1/2$.

    7. The Chebyshev inequality gives, for $k=2$:
    1. $P(|X-\mu|\ge 2\sigma)\le 1/4$
    2. $P(|X-\mu|\ge 2\sigma)\le 1/2$
    3. $P(|X-\mu|\ge 2\sigma)\ge 1/4$
    4. $P(|X-\mu|\ge 2\sigma)=1/4$
    Show Answer

    A. Chebyshev: $P(|X-\mu|\ge k\sigma)\le 1/k^2$; with $k=2$ the bound is $1/4$, an upper bound, not an equality.

    8. If $X_1,\ldots,X_n$ are iid $N(\mu,\sigma^2)$, then $(n-1)S^2/\sigma^2$ follows:
    1. $\chi^2_n$
    2. $\chi^2_{n-1}$
    3. $F$-distribution
    4. $t_{n-1}$
    Show Answer

    B. $(n-1)S^2/\sigma^2=\sum(X_i-\bar X)^2/\sigma^2\sim\chi^2_{n-1}$: one degree of freedom is used in estimating $\mu$ by $\bar X$.

    9. The characteristic function of $N(\mu,\sigma^2)$ is:
    1. $e^{\mu t+\sigma^2 t^2/2}$
    2. $e^{i\mu t-\sigma^2 t^2/2}$
    3. $e^{-i\mu t+\sigma^2 t^2/2}$
    4. $e^{i\mu t+\sigma^2 t^2/2}$
    Show Answer

    B. $\phi(t)=E(e^{itX})=e^{i\mu t-\sigma^2t^2/2}$; A is the MGF, which has $+\sigma^2t^2/2$ and no $i$.

    10. If $X\sim\text{Bin}(n,p)$ and $np\to\lambda$ as $n\to\infty$, $p\to 0$, then $X$ tends in distribution to:
    1. $N(0,1)$
    2. $\text{Poisson}(\lambda)$
    3. $\text{Exp}(\lambda)$
    4. $\chi^2_1$
    Show Answer

    B. Poisson limit theorem: $\binom{n}{x}p^x(1-p)^{n-x}\to e^{-\lambda}\lambda^x/x!$ as $n\to\infty$, $p\to 0$ with $np\to\lambda$.

    11. The convergence in probability implies convergence in:
    1. $L^2$
    2. Distribution
    3. Almost sure
    4. Mean square
    Show Answer

    B. The chain of implications is a.s. $\Rightarrow$ in probability $\Rightarrow$ in distribution, so convergence in probability implies convergence in distribution (converse false in general).

    12. The variance of the sample mean from $n$ iid observations with population variance $\sigma^2$ is:
    1. $\sigma^2$
    2. $\sigma^2/n$
    3. $n\sigma^2$
    4. $\sigma^2/\sqrt n$
    Show Answer

    B. $\text{Var}(\bar X)=\frac{1}{n^2}\sum\text{Var}(X_i)=\frac{n\sigma^2}{n^2}=\sigma^2/n$; its square root $\sigma/\sqrt n$ is the standard error.

    13. The pdf $f(x)=cx^2,\,0<x<3$ requires $c=$:
    1. $1/3$
    2. $1/9$
    3. $1/27$
    4. $2/9$
    Show Answer

    B. $\int_0^3 cx^2 dx=9c=1\Rightarrow c=1/9$.

    14. If $X$ and $Y$ are independent, $\text{Var}(X+Y)$ equals:
    1. $\text{Var}(X)\text{Var}(Y)$
    2. $\text{Var}(X)+\text{Var}(Y)$
    3. $\text{Var}(X)-\text{Var}(Y)$
    4. $2\text{Var}(X)+\text{Var}(Y)$
    Show Answer

    B. $\text{Var}(X+Y)=\text{Var}(X)+\text{Var}(Y)+2\text{Cov}(X,Y)$, and independence makes the covariance $0$.

    15. For a Geometric($p$) distribution, $E(X)$ equals (number of trials until first success):
    1. $p$
    2. $1/p$
    3. $1/(1-p)$
    4. $p/(1-p)$
    Show Answer

    B. With $X$ = number of trials up to the first success, $P(X=x)=q^{x-1}p$ and $E(X)=\sum xq^{x-1}p=1/p$.

    16. The CDF $F(x)$ of any random variable is:
    1. Always continuous
    2. Always differentiable
    3. Right-continuous and non-decreasing
    4. Left-continuous
    Show Answer

    C. Every distribution function is non-decreasing and right-continuous with limits $0$ and $1$; it need not be continuous (discrete jumps) or differentiable.

    17. For a Cauchy distribution:
    1. Mean exists, variance does not
    2. Neither mean nor variance exists
    3. Both exist
    4. Variance exists, mean does not
    Show Answer

    B. The Cauchy density has tails so heavy that $\int|x|f(x)\,dx$ diverges; hence neither the mean nor the variance exists.

    1. $\chi^2_2$
    2. $N(0,2)$
    3. $F_{1,1}$
    4. $t_2$
    Show Answer

    A. The sum of squares of $k$ independent $N(0,1)$ variables is $\chi^2_k$; here $k=2$.

    2. iid and finite mean (Khintchin)
    3. Identical distribution only
    4. Independence only
    Show Answer

    B. Khintchin's weak law needs only iid variables with a finite mean; finite variance (Chebyshev's version) is a stronger condition than needed.

    25. Markov's inequality states for non-negative $X$:

    4. $X$ is symmetric
    Show Answer

    B. If the MGF exists in an open interval around $0$, it determines all moments and the distribution uniquely.

    1. Always
    2. Only if $X_n$ is iid
    3. Never
    4. Only if variance is finite

    2. $\sigma/\mu$
    3. $\sigma^2/\mu$

    Show Answer

    B. The coefficient of variation is SD/mean $=\sigma/\mu$ (usually quoted as a percentage).

    1. The set $\mathbb Q$ of rationals is:
    1. Finite
    2. Countable
    3. Uncountable
    4. Empty
    Show Answer

    B. The rationals can be listed as a sequence (e.g. by Cantor's diagonal enumeration of $p/q$), so $\mathbb Q$ is countably infinite.

    2. The sequence $a_n=(1+1/n)^n$ converges to:
    1. 1
    2. $e$
    3. $2$
    4. $\infty$
    Show Answer

    B. $(1+1/n)^n$ is increasing and bounded above by $3$, and its limit is $e\approx 2.718$.

    3. Cauchy criterion for convergence of sequence: $(x_n)$ converges iff:
    1. Bounded
    2. Monotonic
    3. For all $\varepsilon>0,\,\exists N$ s.t. $|x_m-x_n|<\varepsilon$ for $m,n\ge N$
    4. $x_n\to 0$
    Show Answer

    C. In $\mathbb R$ a sequence converges iff it is Cauchy: for every $\varepsilon>0$ there is $N$ with $|x_m-x_n|<\varepsilon$ for all $m,n\ge N$.

    4. The series $\sum 1/n^p$ converges iff:
    1. $p>0$
    2. $p\ge 1$
    3. $p>1$
    4. $p<1$
    Show Answer

    C. By the integral test, $\sum 1/n^p$ converges iff $\int_1^\infty x^{-p}dx<\infty$, i.e. $p>1$; $p=1$ is the divergent harmonic series.

    5. Radius of convergence of $\sum x^n/n!$ is:
    1. 0
    2. 1
    3. $e$
    4. $\infty$
    Show Answer

    D. Ratio test: $|a_{n+1}/a_n|=|x|/(n+1)\to 0$ for every $x$, so the radius is $\infty$ (the series is $e^x$).

    6. Bolzano–Weierstrass theorem: every bounded sequence has:
    1. A limit
    2. A convergent subsequence
    3. A monotone subsequence only
    4. Cauchy subsequence only
    Show Answer

    B. Bolzano–Weierstrass: every bounded sequence in $\mathbb R$ has a convergent subsequence.

    7. The function $f(x)=1/x$ on $(0,1)$ is:
    1. Uniformly continuous
    2. Continuous but not uniformly continuous
    3. Not continuous
    4. Bounded
    Show Answer

    B. $1/x$ is continuous on $(0,1)$ but its slope is unbounded near $0$: $x_n=1/n$, $y_n=1/(n+1)$ get arbitrarily close while $|f(x_n)-f(y_n)|=1$.

    8. Mean Value Theorem (Lagrange) requires $f$ to be:
    1. Continuous on $[a,b]$, differentiable on $(a,b)$
    2. Differentiable everywhere
    3. Just continuous
    4. $f(a)=f(b)$
    Show Answer

    A. Lagrange's MVT needs $f$ continuous on $[a,b]$ and differentiable on $(a,b)$; then $f'(c)=(f(b)-f(a))/(b-a)$ for some $c\in(a,b)$.

    9. Rolle's theorem requires additionally:
    1. $f$ monotone
    2. $f(a)=f(b)$
    3. $f$ bounded
    4. $f$ periodic
    Show Answer

    B. Rolle's theorem is the MVT with the extra condition $f(a)=f(b)$, which gives $f'(c)=0$.

    1. $0/0$ only
    2. $\infty/\infty$ only
    3. Both $0/0$ and $\infty/\infty$
    4. $0\cdot\infty$ only
    Show Answer

    C. L'Hôpital's rule applies directly to $0/0$ and $\infty/\infty$; other forms such as $0\cdot\infty$ must first be rewritten as one of these.

    Show Answer

    3. $p\ge 1$
    4. $p<1$
    Show Answer

    B. $\int_1^\infty x^{-p}dx=1/(p-1)$ for $p>1$ and diverges for $p\le 1$.
