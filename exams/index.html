    Statistics for Examinations

    UGC NET · CSIR NET · ASRB NET · Indian Statistical Service · APPSC

    These examinations ask about the same statistics. Estimation is estimation whether the paper is set by the UPSC, the UGC, the ASRB or a state commission. So this site teaches the subject once — properly, with the steps shown — and each examination gets a way in to it rather than a second copy of it.

    1. Pick your examination from the cards below.
    2. Read its syllabus map — every line of the official syllabus, pointed at the page here that teaches it.
    3. Study from the linked notes, and read the gaps each map names. How this section works

    NET exams

    The three national eligibility tests a statistics candidate sits. Each has its own way in; the notes behind all three are the same courses.

    BUILT

    UGC NET Statistics

    Subject code 107. All ten units written out in full, with at least two worked examples per concept — and a syllabus map pointing every line of the official syllabus at the unit and the full course that teach it.

    130 syllabus lines mapped · 500 model MCQs · a solved 2026 paper

     BUILT

    CSIR NET Mathematical Sciences

    Built from the official syllabus. A statistics candidate sits Units 1 and 4; both are mapped line by line onto the notes here, and Units 2 and 3 are named as the mathematics candidate’s and left out.

    52 syllabus lines graded · 9 named as gaps

     BUILT

    ASRB NET — Agricultural Statistics

    Eight units, from measure-theoretic probability through to optimisation and soft computing. Every line pointed at the page that teaches it — and one whole unit, Statistical Genetics, marked red because this site does not teach it at all.

    99 syllabus lines graded · 23 named as gaps

    Other exams

    Two recruitment examinations. The ISS map is the one the others lean on: it is the widest syllabus of them all, so a line in any NET paper is usually a line it already carries.

    BUILT

    Indian Statistical Service — the spine

    The widest statistics syllabus in the country that is also fixed, so this site maps itself against it once and every other syllabus can be read through that map. All four statistics papers, every line pointed at the page that teaches it.

    147 syllabus lines graded · 28 named as gaps

     BUILT

    APPSC Assistant Director & Assistant Statistical Officer

    Built from both official notifications. The two posts turn out to share one syllabus word for word — the Assistant Director splits it across two papers at P.G. standard, the Assistant Statistical Officer takes it as one at degree standard — so one map serves both and says which paper each line sits in.

    73 syllabus lines graded, every one with a page behind it · the 2025 and 2022 Paper-II solved, all 150 questions of each

    No exam pattern is stated here, and that is deliberate. Marks, duration, negative marking, the number of papers and eligibility all change between notifications, and a stale figure in front of someone planning months of study is worse than no figure at all. Nothing of that kind appears on this site unless it was taken from the official document for that exam, and the page says which document and when.

    Always read the current official notification for the exam you are sitting. Use this section for the statistics, not for the rules.

    How this section works

    1. The syllabus map is the thing. Each exam page takes that exam's official topic list and points each line at the page here that teaches it — so you read in the exam's order, not the textbook's.
    2. The gaps are named. Every map carries a list of what this site does not cover for that exam. A map that quietly skips its holes is worse than no map, because you only find out in the hall.
    3. The notes are shared, not copied. A map links into the study sections below. Correct a proof once and every exam that points at it is corrected too.
    4. Papers get solved, not listed. Where a previous paper is available it is worked through, the way the 2026 paper already is.

    What is not here yet

    All five examinations above have a map behind them, so the honest answer to “what is missing” is no longer an examination but a subject. The ASRB map grades its whole sixth unit — Statistical Genetics — red: Hardy–Weinberg equilibrium, gene and genotypic frequencies, heritability, breeding value and selection indices are not taught anywhere on this site. That is the largest single gap any map here reports, and naming it is more use to a candidate than quietly pointing those lines at something adjacent.
