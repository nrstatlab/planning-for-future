    Indian Statistical Service — the syllabus, mapped

    Four statistics papers · 147 syllabus lines · 82% covered here · 26 gaps named

    The Indian Statistical Service syllabus is the widest statistics syllabus in the country that is also fixed. Every state degree board writes its own; every university writes its own MSc; every commission writes its own paper. The ISS list sits above all of them, and almost every topic any of them asks for appears somewhere on it. So this site maps itself against the ISS syllabus once, and any other syllabus can be read through that map.

    All four Statistics papers are mapped below, 147 syllabus lines in total. Each line is reproduced as prescribed, pointed at the page here that teaches it, and graded for depth. Where nothing here teaches it, the line says so.

    • 147syllabus lines
    • 92taught in depth
    • 29covered at exam level
    • 26not here yet
    • 82%covered

    The four papers

    Paper I — Statistics-I

    Probability, Statistical Methods, Numerical Analysis, Computer Application and Data Processing.
    35 lines · 23 deep · 5 brief · 7 not here · 80% covered

    Paper II — Statistics-Ii

    Linear Models, Statistical Inference and Hypothesis Testing, Official Statistics.
    27 lines · 18 deep · 5 brief · 4 not here · 85% covered

    Paper III — Statistics-Iii

    Sampling Techniques, Econometrics, Applied Statistics.
    30 lines · 15 deep · 10 brief · 5 not here · 83% covered

    Paper IV — Statistics-Iv

    Operations Research and Reliability, Demography and Vital Statistics, Survival Analysis and Clinical Trial, Quality Control, Multivariate Analysis, Design and Analysis of Experiments, Computing with C and R.
    55 lines · 36 deep · 9 brief · 10 not here · 82% covered

    The written examination, as the notification sets it out

    SubjectMaximum marksTime allowed
    General English1003 hrs.
    General Studies1003 hrs.
    Statistics-I (Objective)2002 hrs.
    Statistics-II (Objective)2002 hrs.
    Statistics-III (Descriptive)2003 hrs.
    Statistics-IV (Descriptive)2003 hrs.

    “Statistics I & II will be of Objective Type Questions (80 questions with maximum marks of 200 in each paper) to be attempted in 120 minutes.”

    “Statistics III and IV will be of Descriptive Type having Short Answer/ Small Problems Questions (50%) and Long Answer and Comprehension problem questions (50%). At least one Short Answer and One Long Answer Question from each section is compulsory. In Statistics-IV, there will be SEVEN Sections in the paper. Candidates have to choose any TWO Sections out of them. All Sections will carry equal marks.”

    Examination Notice No. 07/2026-IES/ISS, dated 11.02.2026 — Appendix-I, Scheme of Examination, and Section-II, Standard and Syllabi

    That table is quoted, not remembered. It is reproduced from the notification named under it, and from nowhere else. Marks, timings and the number of sections change between notifications, so read the current official notice for the year you are sitting and treat this page as being about the statistics.

    Why the descriptive papers change what it means to be covered

    “The candidates will be expected to illustrate theory by facts, and to analyse problems with the help of theory. They will be expected to be particularly conversant with Indian problems in the field(s) of Economics/Statistics.”

    Examination Notice No. 07/2026-IES/ISS, dated 11.02.2026 — Appendix-I, Scheme of Examination, and Section-II, Standard and Syllabi

    Papers III and IV are written by hand, and that sentence is why a brief grade is not good enough for them. Recognising Horvitz–Thompson in a list of four options is an objective-paper skill; deriving its variance and then saying what the estimate means for the survey in front of you is a descriptive-paper skill. Where a line below is graded brief and sits in Paper III or IV, treat it as work still to do.

    How to read the depth column

    deep a full unit page here, with the derivation worked out and problems solved step by step — enough to answer a descriptive paper, not just recognise the term.  brief covered, but at exam-summary level: the definition, the formula and a worked example, without the derivation.  not here nothing on this site teaches it. It is named rather than quietly skipped, because a map that hides its holes only tells you about them in the hall.

    The work queue

    26 of the 147 lines have nothing behind them. They are listed here together, paper by paper, because this list is what gets built next — in that order, roughly, by how many other syllabuses also ask for them.

    Paper I — 7 lines.

    • Statistical Methods — Concept of asymptotic relative efficiency
    • Numerical Analysis — Finite differences of different orders; Δ, E and D operators; factorial representation of a polynomial; separation of symbols; sub-division of intervals; differences of zero
    • Numerical Analysis — Interpolation and extrapolation: Newton–Gregory forward and backward formulae for equal intervals; formula for unequal intervals
    • Numerical Analysis — Central difference formulae due to Gauss, Stirling and Bessel; error terms in interpolation formulae
    • Numerical Analysis — Inverse interpolation and the different methods for it
    • Numerical Analysis — Summation of series whose general term is a first difference, or in geometric progression
    • Numerical Analysis — Numerical solution of differential equations — Runge–Kutta method

    Paper II — 4 lines.

    • Official Statistics — Official statistics: need, uses, users, reliability, relevance, limitations, transparency, visibility
    • Official Statistics — Compilation, collection, processing, analysis and dissemination; agencies involved; methods
    • Official Statistics — Sector-wise statistics — agriculture, health, education, women and child; important surveys and censuses, indicators, agencies
    • Official Statistics — Socio-economic indicators; gender awareness and statistics

    Paper III — 5 lines.

    • Sampling Techniques — Methodologies in sample surveys — questionnaires, sampling design and field investigation as followed by NSSO
    • Sampling Techniques — Des Raj and Das estimators for n = 2
    • Sampling Techniques — Interpenetrating sub-sampling
    • Applied Statistics — Estimation of the mean, autocovariance and autocorrelation functions under large sample theory; estimation of ARIMA parameters
    • Applied Statistics — Spectral analysis of weakly stationary processes; periodogram and correlogram analyses; computations based on the Fourier transform

    Paper IV — 10 lines.

    • Operations Research and Reliability — Dynamic programming and its application to linear programming problems
    • Operations Research and Reliability — Inventory problems; the EOQ formula of Harris, its sensitivity analysis and extensions with quantity discounts and shortages; multi-item inventory subject to constraints; models with random demand; P and Q systems
    • Operations Research and Reliability — Replacement problems — block and age replacement policies
    • Operations Research and Reliability — Reliability concepts and measures; components and systems; coherent systems and their reliability
    • Operations Research and Reliability — Notions of ageing — IFR, IFRA, NBU, DMRL and NBUE classes and their duals; loss of memory property of the exponential
    • Operations Research and Reliability — Reliability estimation from censored life tests and tests with replacement; stress–strength reliability and its estimation
    • Demography and Vital Statistics — Internal migration and its measurement; migration models; international migration; net migration
    • Survival Analysis and Clinical Trial — Competing risk model; parametric and non-parametric inference for it
    • Quality Control — OC and ARL of control charts; control by gauging; moving average and exponentially weighted moving average charts; Cu-Sum charts with V-masks and decision intervals; economic design of the X-bar chart
    • Quality Control — Plans for inspection by variables for one-sided and two-sided specifications

    Using this map for a syllabus that is not the ISS one

    Take your own syllabus a line at a time and find the nearest ISS line above. The destination is the same page either way, because the statistics is the same — a state degree board asking for stratified sampling and the ISS asking for stratified sampling want the same derivation, at different depths. What differs is how far down the page you need to read, and the depth column tells you that. The two places this breaks down are worth knowing: ISS Paper I asks for numerical analysis, which most statistics degrees do not, and ISS Paper II asks for official statistics at a level no degree syllabus reaches. Both are in the work queue above.

    The other maps on this site — CSIR NET and the UGC NET units — point at the same pages, so a correction to a proof reaches every map at once.
